# Scene tabs

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 5a. Scene Tabs

Several scenes stay open at once, each with its own parts, undo history, selection, comments and agent conversation.

- **Studies** (§8b) save their captured baseline, knob values, seed, Checks and graded rows in `project.study`. Background scene tabs stay mounted, so their Study workers keep running when another tab is in front. The saved baseline is serialized once across incremental result saves, using the same immutable part cache as the scene. Portable scene files carry the Study too. Export and import remove machine-local print, quote, policy, try, skill and build-guide records from its captured baseline as well as the live project.

- **Architecture:** `App.tsx` is the tab host; each tab is a `SceneWorkspace` (`src/SceneWorkspace.tsx`).
  - Every tab stays mounted (background tabs mount shortly after launch, §0f), so a background agent keeps building and can render its review shots. Background tabs are `invisible` + `inert`, and their `Viewport3D` is `paused` (no render loop; captures still render on demand). Closing a tab frees its WebGL context (`forceContextLoss`).
  - Up to 8 tabs (browsers cap WebGL contexts near 16). A ninth says “Close a scene to open another. Up to 8 stay open.”
  - App-wide state lives in the host and reaches scenes as `studio` (`StudioShared`): preferences, panel open state and widths, libraries, chat list, the agent connection, Settings. Per scene: everything about the scene, Left/Right panel tabs, the palette, toasts.
  - **Reading another tab's robot:** `studio.robotsBeside(tabId)` returns every other open tab's robot (`sceneRobots` in `src/app/tabModel.ts`: the scene's name, parts and joints, from the tab's live snapshot, or its seed while unmounted; tabs without joints are skipped). It's a call, not state, so a scene reads it in a handler (opening the Case builder, opening a Case, starting a try) and never during render. A Case's extra robot slots are filled this way (§8h).
  - **Running another tab's robot:** `studio.handleOf(tabId)` returns that tab's `WorkspaceHandle`. `caseRobot()` measures its parts in its own viewport (paused or not) and returns them with its project and triangles; `fingerprint()` is its readiness fingerprint; `openCase(kase)` opens that Case there (or adds it) in the Simulate tab. Compare With uses all three (§8h); like `robotsBeside`, they're called from handlers and effects, never during render.
  - Only the scene in front listens to the keyboard, paste, the menu bar and the dev `__studio` hook. Background scenes skip re-renders for app-wide changes (custom `memo` comparator) and catch up when brought to front.
- **Where:** in the title bar (§2), ending 8px before the scene's actions. With the sidebar open, the first tab starts over the canvas card's left edge (rail + sidebar width + 8px gap), so the strip lines up with the canvas, and slides with it as the sidebar resizes or toggles; with the sidebar closed (or too narrow to clear the title bar controls) it starts 8px after the Scenes menu. `App.tsx` lays the sidebar toggle and `SceneTabs` over each scene's title bar drag region. No glass: the tabs are part of the window chrome.
- **Tabs (`SceneTabs`):** 28px rounded pills (8px corners), 180px wide, shrinking to 96px, 6px apart, on the title bar's centerline.
  - The active tab sits on a flat `#262626` fill (no edge or highlight); its label is `white/90`, 13px medium, and its × always shows.
  - Background tabs are labels (`white/50`, `white/90` on hover) in a faint `white/[0.07]` inset ring (box-shadow, so it takes no space), with a soft fill on hover and × on hover.
  - Nothing else: no icons or separators. While a scene's agent works, a 12px white spinner sits before its name.
  - Past their minimum width they scroll sideways (the wheel scrolls; an edge fades only while tabs are hidden past it, measured from layout so sliding tabs don't trigger it).
  - New tabs grow in (`animate-tab-in`); tabs slide into place after insert, close and drop (FLIP, 220ms).
  - Press selects. Drag past 4px reorders. Double-click renames inline. Middle-click closes. × closes (`Close  ⌘W`). It shows on the active tab and on hover; elsewhere it takes no room and no clicks, so a background tab's name runs to its right edge, and on hover the × fades in over the name's faded end (§4). It's a 28px mouse target hidden from the accessibility tree, since a button inside a tab breaks the tab role, so keyboard and screen reader users close with ⌘W or the tab's menu. Right-click: Rename, Duplicate, New Scene, Reopen Closed Scene, Close, Close Other Scenes, Close Scenes to the Right.
  - Resting 800ms on a background tab opens a small preview card under it: a fresh frame of that scene, its name, and its part count in the Scenes list's words (“0 parts”, “1 part”, `scenes/partCount.ts`), or the agent's live activity while it works. The frame is copied at a maximum side of 560px and encoded once as JPEG in the image worker (§0f).
  - + (28px) 2px after the last tab (its glyph carries 6px of air, so it reads about as far out as the tabs are apart): `New scene  ⌘T`.
- **Opening and closing:**
  - **Browser storage:** `utils/platform/web/webScenes.ts` lists the `sceneSummaries` IndexedDB store, without reading full saved scenes. Saves preserve the previous preview and dimensions from that summary too. Database version 4 builds those summaries from existing scenes with a cursor during the upgrade. Saving, updating a preview and deleting write the scene and its summary in one transaction and return only after it commits. Full records load for opening a scene or generating and updating a missing preview. `webScenes.test.ts` covers each write path, `idbWrite.test.ts` covers transaction completion and aborts, and `e2e/webScenes.e2e.test.ts` checks the version 3 upgrade in the browser.
  - New scenes are numbered so open tabs never share a name: Untitled, Untitled 2…
  - **First launch** (`launchTabs` in `app/openScenes.ts`, `app/firstLaunch.ts`): the window restores the tabs it had open. When none of them loads and the Library holds no saved scene, the first tab is the gallery's `rover-reach-the-flag` bundle instead of a blank scene: fetched from `/gallery/`, checked by `parseGalleryBundle` and turned into a scene by `bundleScene`, the same path Open Scene takes (§8h). It's unsaved under a fresh id, and its seed's `playCase` opens the Case and plays its reference (`useOpeningCase`). Autosave writes nothing until it's edited, so a person who closes it untouched sees it again next launch; once any scene is saved, launches restore tabs or open a blank one. When the Library can't be read, the tab is blank. The web studio does the same from IndexedDB. The e2e harness lists one saved scene by default, so suites start on a blank canvas; `e2e/firstLaunch.e2e.test.ts` passes an empty Library.
  - Library → a scene: shows its tab if it's open, reuses the tab in front if it's untouched (blank, unrenamed, unused), otherwise opens a new tab after it.
  - Closing a tab stops its agent, saves its chat and ends its session. The toast reads “Closed ⟨name⟩” + [Undo] (“…and stopped its agent” when one was running); Undo reopens it in place with its chat. Untouched tabs close silently. Closing the last tab leaves a blank one, which Undo removes again.
  - ⇧⌘T reopens the last 10 closed scenes. Duplicate (the tab's menu, ⌘K, or the Scenes menu) copies the scene, not the chat, and names it “Sprout copy”, then “Sprout copy 2”, skipping any name an open tab or a saved scene already has (`sceneCopyName`).
  - When a background agent finishes: “Agent finished in ⟨name⟩” + [Show].
- **Sharing scenes** (`utils/sceneFile.ts`): Export ▸ Scene (also File ▸ Export Scene and ⌘K) downloads `<name>.basecase`, gzipped JSON `{ format: 'basecase-scene', version, scene }` holding the name, description, parts, comments, project settings and preview, built from the scene in memory. It leaves out what belongs to the sender's machine: the chat, `prints` (so Skip N printed parts doesn't skip the sender's prints), `policies` and `buildGuide` (their files live in the sender's `library/`). File ▸ Open Scene File, ⌘K, or dropping the file on the canvas saves it to the Library under a new id and opens it in a tab (`importSceneFile` in `useSceneLibrary`). The file is untrusted: it must match the format, parts need an id, kind and position, images must be `data:image/` URLs, and a newer `version` says “Made with a newer Basecase. Update to open it.” Bump `VERSION` only when an older app can't read the new shape. A robot imported from Onshape keeps its link as ids only, never the account's keys: `group.onshape` on each of its parts (document, workspace or version, element), `robotLink.onshape` (its Onshape occurrences) and `onshape` on each joint (the mate's id, and `gone` once the mate is deleted in Onshape), which Update from Onshape uses (§8c). A shared scene keeps them, so whoever opens it can update the robot with their own keys if their account can open the document.
- **Shortcuts:** ⌘T new, ⌘W close, ⇧⌘T reopen, ⌃Tab / ⌃⇧Tab and ⇧⌘] / ⇧⌘[ next / previous, ⌘1–⌘8 a tab, ⌘9 the last. They work while typing (except with Settings open). ⌘K lists every other open scene under Open Scenes, plus New / Rename / Duplicate / Close / Reopen Scene. Rename Scene opens the tab's rename field, the same one double-click opens; the field's tab lives in `useTabActions` (`renamingTab`), so ⌘K can open it.
- **Sheet imports:** scene files and gallery bundles validate optional sheet fields through `src/sheet/validation.ts`, including boolean operands and custom model nodes. A sheet needs a supported material and positive finite thickness. Its transform must contain 16 finite numbers, the affine bottom row `[0, 0, 0, 1]`, and a finite positive determinant. Missing fields keep older scenes readable. `src/sheet/importValidation.test.ts` checks valid round trips and malformed settings, nonfinite values, perspective, singular and reflected transforms at both import boundaries.
- **Imported world assets:** a scene's `worldAssets` table stores each imported world's visual mesh, materials, texture and splat bytes, and collision hulls once under the SHA-256 of its canonical serialized content. Cases carry only the hash and placement. `src/cases/worldAssets.ts` registers the typed geometry in memory, restores and validates saved tables in a worker before a scene opens, and collects the worlds referenced by its Cases for autosave and export. The binary codec writes little-endian numeric buffers and byte payloads as base64. Scene imports check coordinates and ordered bounds within 500 m, buffer sizes, vertex indices, material groups, transforms and at most 64 collision hulls, then verify every content hash before registration. The saved material table accepts 128 authored glTF materials plus the importer's fallback material. Each asset is capped at 128 MiB of decoded binary data, including at most 64 MiB of textures and splat bytes. The same budgets apply when the worker prepares an asset, so accepted worlds remain readable after saving. A scene with missing world content is refused even if another open scene already registered the same hash. Desktop scene JSON, browser IndexedDB and portable `.basecase` files carry the same table, while Library summaries leave it out. Autosave reuses each immutable asset's cached JSON across project edits.

## Performance: scene storage and startup

Measurements and limits: [Scene storage and startup performance evidence](../notes/performance-workspace.md).

- **Dated lists prepare timestamps once.** `utils/dateGroups.ts` parses each item's update time once for sorting and labeling in both Scenes and Chat History. It prepares the local midnight and year once per grouping call. Equal timestamps retain source order, input arrays stay unchanged, and later calls read timestamp edits and calendar changes afresh. [Evidence](../notes/performance-workspace.md#dated-lists-prepare-timestamps-once).

  Older entries reuse one formatted label per local calendar month and year within the call. Recent-day cutoffs still run before month lookup. `dateGroups.test.ts` covers distinct years, unchanged labels and formatting counts at 300 and 600 entries alongside the existing boundary and ownership checks.

- **Insertion ranges scan scalar values.** `utils/centerSizeRange.ts` tracks the minimum and maximum of each axis without temporary coordinate arrays. Robot imports, articulated catalog parts, CAD body placement and agent robot-height reports share it. Existing-scene right edges reduce rotated or affine bounds without spreading scene counts into function arguments. [Evidence](../notes/performance-workspace.md#insertion-ranges-scan-scalar-values).

- **Autosave never re-serializes unchanged parts.** `sceneJson` caches each part's JSON by object identity, slices the work across frames and sends one string (`saveSceneJson`).

- **Duplicate naming indexes taken names once.** `workspace/helpers.ts` builds a set before checking copy suffixes for parts, groups, scenes and Library models. It keeps the first available suffix without rescanning every name for each occupied suffix. [Evidence](../notes/performance-workspace.md#duplicate-naming-indexes-taken-names-once).

- **Desktop scene replacement is atomic.** `electron/library/sceneLibrary.mjs` uses the existing `writeAtomic` helper for scene records. A replacement writes a sibling temporary file before renaming it over the saved record. An interruption before rename leaves the previous record readable. Listings ignore unfinished temporary files. This does not guarantee recovery from power loss.

- **Small payloads across IPC and HTTP.** Lists are summaries (`SavedSceneSummary`: counts, size, a small WebP preview); full records load on demand (`platformBridge.getScene`). Library JSON is written compact. Never send a whole library over IPC. Saving, renaming, duplicating or deleting a Library model, a scene in the Scenes menu or a chat changes the list on screen at once, then writes that one record (`withModel`, `sceneStore`); the list is read again only when the write fails. A scene's writes run in order, and opening it waits for them (`queueSceneWrite`). The desktop models-folder watcher sends only the record that changed, and skips the app's own writes (`ModelChanges`).
  - The browser lists `sceneSummaries` directly from IndexedDB, so listing scenes never clones every scene's parts and project. Saves read the previous preview and dimensions from the summary instead of cloning the old scene. Saves, previews and deletes keep the scene and summary together in one transaction (§5a). The version 4 upgrade reads old scenes one at a time to seed the summaries.

- **Boot never waits on anything it doesn't need to draw.** The window opens before the agent connects (`connectAcpAgent` runs in parallel, and status reads `connecting` meanwhile, so nothing says “disconnected”). Anything that needs a session awaits that first connect (`AcpAgent.pending`) instead of starting a second. Only the active tab mounts at first; background tabs mount one per idle callback after its first frame (`useMountedTabs`), since each builds its own WebGL context, environment map and shaders. Events for a tab that hasn't mounted are dropped, not refused: on mount it adopts its runs from the journal and the bridge replays unanswered requests. [Evidence](../notes/performance-workspace.md#boot-never-waits-on-anything-it-doesnt-need-to-draw).

## Testing coverage

### First-launch relaunch

### Passive fixture imports

Portable scene decoding validates articulated Case records through `validateFixtureImports` before returning their project. Malformed pivots, axes, topology and physics values refuse the import. Existing rigid Case records retain their loading path. `src/cases/fixturePersistence.test.ts` checks both valid topology round trips and damaged fixture refusal in portable scenes and gallery bundles, along with robot undo and redo. ## Assignment attempts

Classroom imports and submission inspection call `classroom/storage.ts` and ordinary browser `saveScene` with a fresh `attempt-<uuid>` id. Repeated imports of the same assignment never derive an id from its gallery slug and never replace another scene. Project settings retain the original assignment and local display name through browser autosave. The assignment's referenced world stays in `sceneWorldAssets` even if the student removes its exploratory Case entry.

Submit flushes the scene through its existing workspace handle before delivering the file, then refuses delivery if parts or project settings changed during preparation. Opening a submission creates another independent scene, preserves Classroom in its original tab and carries the failing Check's selection context. The original grid and its submission references remain in the IndexedDB settings store across reloads. An index references separate assignment and submission records, and writes update only changed tiles in the same transaction as the index. These are ordinary snapshots, with no classroom version or merge format.

`e2e/classroom.e2e.test.ts` declares reload of a student's edited attempt, preserved assignment identity and a second independent import of the same assignment. It also checks that opening a submitted design leaves the teacher's grid available. File authority and worker grading coverage lives in [Cases](cases.md#classroom-assignments). [Observations and limits](../notes/testing-cases.md#classroom-assignments).


### Versions, linked branches and selective merge

Scene History is in the scene tab's right-click menu and ⌘K. Save Version names a snapshot of parts, annotations, goal, project settings and referenced world content. Restore first retains the current design, then commits the selected snapshot through ordinary document history. Undo and redo restore the whole project, annotations and goal with the parts. Restore and merge are disabled while the scene's agent works.

Branch opens any selected version as a linked scene. Duplicate uses the same graph and chooses the first available copy name across open and saved scenes. Both branches retain their own working design and history. The graph stores the source scene and branch-point version, with immutable snapshots identified by canonical SHA-256. Existing scenes gain a graph only when a version or branch is made, or an agent begins. Startup does not rewrite old scenes.

Before an agent sends a prompt, its lane's baseline must reach durable storage. Completion, Stop and tab closure retain a changed design. Adopted runs finish through the same capture path. Relaunch retains the saved working design and ends orphaned lane checkpoints. Closing a tab flushes those checkpoints before its ordinary autosave. Each workspace serializes lane captures, restores and merges, so simultaneous lanes retain their baselines and completions. A queued lane captures the design after preceding work applies, and a scene reset invalidates older requests. Revert Changes uses the lane's recorded versions and touched parts, with required dependencies. A conflicting later edit is refused rather than overwritten, with the recovery message shown in an error toast.

Compare measures two open scenes through existing Checks, BOM and Weight paths. Each result displays the captured design fingerprint. If both robots fit the active Case, it reuses Compare With on that same Case. Added, removed and changed parts and joints appear separately. Changed scene fingerprints, immutable snapshot references or either scene’s history refuse a pending merge and require another comparison. A named checkpoint made after comparison cannot be overwritten by that comparison’s older graph. The reference check also covers project fields outside the Checks fingerprint, such as skeleton settings.

Merge Parts applies selected changes through a three-way comparison with the shared branch base. Required joints, mates, referenced parts and project fields are included and listed. Newly required assembly members also bring their attached joints, without pulling unrelated edits attached to an existing parent. The validated scene is used for both the merged checkpoint and working design. Conflicts require an explicit current or incoming design choice. Missing parts, joints, mate ports, wiring endpoints, marks and interface part references refuse the merge. The current design remains in history and the merge is undoable. Incoming readiness decisions, local print records, policies, tries, skills, build guides, handoff proofs and Study results are not imported. Existing evidence keeps its original binding and passes through the normal freshness checks.

Selective merges record the incoming version and merged units separately from ordinary ancestry. Later merges retain a base for each previously selected unit, so an unselected change remains available and later incoming edits do not conflict merely because a previous selection landed. Restoring a version also restores that version's merge-base context.

Desktop scenes store a small history reference beside their normal fields. Immutable content and manifests live in `scenes/.history/<sha256>` and are shared across scene branches. Content is verified and written before the scene reference is atomically replaced. The workspace publishes a checkpoint only after its queued scene write succeeds, so a failed write leaves the last durable graph active. Browser history uses Blob records under `scene-history/content/` in the existing IndexedDB settings store. A worker writes new blocks, the scene, summary and history reference in one transaction. Normal autosave and preview writes preserve the reference without retransmitting the graph. Deletion retains shared content for surviving branches. There is no content garbage collection.

Canonical serialization, content hashing, graph validation and browser history storage run in workers. The scene serializer reuses its existing immutable part cache. A cached quoted graph crosses the normal scene save path only for a checkpoint. Desktop lists and browser summaries leave history out. Portable `.basecase` exports include the selected working design and its ordinary portable records, without history, branch links, chats or machine-local artifacts. Import starts an independent history.

The history transport is capped at 64 MiB, 1,000 versions, 100,000 content blocks and 10,000 parts per descriptor. Manifests declare exact block byte lengths with a shared 64 MiB budget before content is read. Readers verify hashes and identifiers, reject duplicate content keys and apply normal scene-file, world and fixture validation before restoration. Local artifact references remain references. A missing file cannot be reconstructed from a version or treated as ready evidence.

`sceneVersions.test.ts`, `versionOperations.test.ts` and `versionMerge.test.ts` cover graph recovery, concurrent lanes, interrupted completion, skeletons, imported assemblies, sheets, grippers, leg changes, dependency closure, joint conflicts, repeat selective merges, evidence exclusion, portable snapshots and whole-project undo/redo. `sceneRecovery.test.mjs` covers desktop atomic replacement, shared content, relaunch, corrupt content and forged hash paths. `webSceneHistory.test.ts`, `webScenes.test.ts` and `idbWrite.test.ts` cover browser block sharing and transactional reference publication. `caseCompare.test.ts` retains the shared Case pipeline and binds its measurements to a fingerprint. `e2e/sceneVersions.e2e.test.ts` declares separate named-version close and reopen coverage, and restoration with reload from a prebuilt saved history. `useSceneVersions.test.ts` checks failed checkpoint publication and a deleted scene refusing a queued write. `e2e/sceneVersionMerge.e2e.test.ts` declares separate branch-menu and selected-merge entry-point checks with prebuilt linked records. The merge declaration closes the sheet before checking the unchanged source. Branch ancestry and conflict combinations remain in the fast graph and merge suites. [Measurements and limits](../notes/performance-workspace.md#scene-version-codec-and-atomic-replacement).
