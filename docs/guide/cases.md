# Cases

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 8h. Cases

A Case is a world with a robot slot, controls and goals that any compatible robot can enter. It lives in `src/cases/`.

- **A Case is the test.** A Skill (§8i) is a solution to it.
- **Case objects never edit parts.** They render on their own layer, and stay out of the BOM, Build Check and picking.

### Format

`src/cases/schema.ts` holds the Case type and `parseCase`, a hand validator that fails closed. A file with another `formatVersion`, an unknown shape, too many objects or a non-finite number throws, in plain words. A Case holds:

- **Slot:** the capabilities it needs (`arm`, `wheels`, `legs`), the bindings it was authored with (end effector, wheels, base), and the reach and anchor its world was authored at. `src/cases/robotFit.ts` works out what a robot has; `slotRefusal` says plainly why one doesn't fit. A bound end effector only counts on a robot where that part ends a chain and the bound point lies on the part, otherwise the fit finds its own (`boundEffector`), so a Case written on one arm binds the hand of the next.
  - **Anchor and reach are the shoulder's:** the anchor is the first chain joint that swings the hand (a base yaw under a straight-up arm doesn't), and the reach is the chain's length from there. Measured from a yaw joint, a five-joint arm with its shoulder 60 mm up read a reach no pitch could cover, so its scaled targets fell out of other arms' reach.
- **World:** `ScenePart`-shaped objects (`worldItem`), each `static`, `dynamic` or `marker` (no collision). `src/cases/scale.ts` scales the whole world and the reference about the anchor to the entering robot's reach, so one Case runs on arms of different sizes.
- **Controls:** typed inputs (`move`, `turn`, `lift`, `grip`).
- **Reference:** a command timeline. An empty one must not pass, and the Stage 2 gate's canary checks that. `runCase` drives it with the same drive classes as live play (§ Live drive: arm, rover, legs). For an arm, `target` is where the hand goes. For a robot that moves around, `target` is a floor waypoint for its base, followed in order by `GroundSteer` (`groundSteer.ts`) from the base's pose each record tick: a rover turns toward it (in place past 60°), legs walk straight at it, forward and sideways at once, since their gait turns poorly in place. `v[0]` with a target caps the speed, and `push: <object id>` makes the target where that object must go: the base aims behind the object along its line to the target (legs aim past it), and stops when the object arrives. A command without a target is a plain velocity and clears the route.
- **Task:** seconds, probes (distances that become trace signals) and a task contract.

A Case's task contract is the one contract of §8i, judged by the Rust crate through `src/skills/contract.ts`; `src/cases` keeps no evaluator of its own. `src/cases/contract.ts` only reads it as bounded plain JSON (version 1, depth 64, 4096 nodes, finite numbers, at most 16 failures) and scales it with the world (every atom's value and scale, except atoms that read an `angle`). `runCase` (`src/cases/runCase.ts`) runs a Case headless in Node or a worker: it checks the fit, scales the world, builds the model, drives it and judges the trace with `evaluateContract`; `CaseResult` carries that `trace` and the `failed` labels, and a contract the crate refuses makes `runCase` reject. `scoreTry` judges a recorded try the same way.

### Seeded runs

A clean run in a perfect world says little about the real robot, so Checks judges every Case across randomized runs (§8b).

- **`RunOptions.seed`:** without one, `runCase` runs the perfect world as before. With one, it draws the same `Randomization` Arena uses (§8i: friction, link masses, servo strength, servo delay; default `CASE_RANDOMIZATION`, Arena's floor with delay up to 20 ms) from a stream seeded by `case-run|<seed>` (`caseRandom.ts`), and applies it through Arena's own `ArenaWorld` (`prepare`), with no joint noise and no change to the start pose, since the reference's waypoints assume it. A second stream moves each `dynamic` world object up to 15 mm each way along the floor (`jitteredWorld`); static objects and markers stay put. The same seed always gives the same result.
- **Controller:** the reference, or with `RunOptions.walk` a ready Walk policy for any robot that moves around (not a fixed base), bipeds and wheels on legs included, as Drive picks it (`driveAccess`). An arm Case still drives the arm. The Walk follows ground targets like scripted legs (`LEGS_FORWARD`, forward and sideways at once), and the steering commands go through `policyCommand`, like the keys. `caseRunner(…).controller` says which one a Case will use.
- **`caseRunner`** does everything that doesn't depend on the seed once (rig, fit, scaling, the drive setup and its baked gait), and `run(seed)` builds the jittered world's model and rolls it. `runCase` is `caseRunner(…).run()`.
- **`runCaseTimes`** (`caseRuns.ts`) runs seeds 1 to N (`CASE_RUNS` = 20) and returns the passes, the median margin and the most common failure label (`missed` for a run that failed no named failure). `runSeeds` and `summarizeRuns` are the same split in two, so the Checks workers can each take a batch of seeds (`caseCheckJob.ts`).
- **Tests** (`caseRuns.test.ts`): same seed, same result; a limp robot gets 0 of 20 on every template, each on a fixture it fits; the fixture rover reaches the flag 18 or more of 20; the weak quadruped fails Walk Without Falling by falling; a misfit's row carries the refusal; a biped, a wheel-legged robot and a quadruped with a Walk run on the policy, and a fixed arm ignores it. Seeded fault: the rover on 0.25 N·m wheel motors passes Reach the Flag 20 of 20, and cut to 30% it fails every run.

### Making a Case

`src/cases/caseBuilder.ts` turns a goal, a world and numbers into a Case and runs it through `parseCase`, so a built Case is the same format as any other. It's pure, so `caseBuilder.test.ts` builds every goal in every world it suits on the fixture robots and runs some in MuJoCo.

- **Goals** (`caseGoals.ts`), each with editable numbers and defaults sized to the robot (`goalDefaults`): Reach a Spot (stop on a spot ahead; wheels or legs), Push a Box (a dynamic cube onto a spot; wheels or legs), Follow a Path (spots in order, swaying left and right; wheels or legs), Walk Without Falling (travel from the start; legs) and Touch a Point (the end effector on a sphere; arms). `goalsFor` lists only the goals the robot can try. Touch a Point starts on the lowest spot Touch the Dots would use (`dotSpots`), and carries the arm's reach and anchor, so it scales to other arms. `dotSpots` puts spots in the vertical plane straight ahead of the anchor, at 0.85 × reach and 60°, 30° and 0° up (more angles if one is too low), keeping those the arm's IK reaches; any arm with two pitch joints can reach that plane, so the spots carry to arms that only bend forward. Only an arm that reaches none of them falls back to posing its joints.
- **Ground references** are waypoints (`GoalPlan.waypoints`), routed around every static object by `groundRoute` (`groundRoute.ts`): a leg that crosses an object grown by the robot's half-size plus 80 mm detours past its nearer side, and a waypoint inside the grown box shrinks the growth until it's outside. Reach a Spot and Walk go to their spot (Walk to 1.25 × its distance), Follow a Path through each spot, Push a Box lines up behind the box and pushes it onto the spot at 0.3 × speed. The box is sized to the robot: half its width, and on legs 1.3 × the body's height, so it meets the body instead of passing under it or between a rover's front wheels. In the Block world, Push a Box puts the block between the robot and the box, with room to get around it and line up again, and every ground goal gets more time (5 s on wheels, 8 s on legs). Every ground goal on a legged robot also fails on a fall (the `fell` failure: the base drops below half its start height). Height is a distance probe to a point 1 km under the start (`FLOOR_PROBE_DEPTH_MM`), since probes only measure distances.
- **Worlds** (`caseWorlds.ts`): Open Floor; Corridor (a wall each side of the route) and Block (one in the way) for ground goals; Shelf (a box under the point) for Touch a Point. A static object over the robot's footprint is refused with the fix (“Move the goal farther ahead…”).
- **Name** defaults to the goal and world (“Push a Box in a Corridor”); the id is its slug, made unique by `add`.
- A limp robot must miss every built Case, as with the templates; Walk Without Falling exists instead of a bare “stay up”, because joint friction holds a limp robot standing.
- `useCaseBuilder` (`src/workspace/`) holds the builder's open state, the robot's facts and `create` (adds through `useCases.add`, the same path as the examples, then opens it).

### World layer

World items go into `MjcfInput.world`, written by `src/sim/mjcfWorld.ts`. They are bodies in `SimModel.bodies` with `parts: []` and `world: [id]`, so `simDeltas` never sees them and `worldDeltas` gives their poses. They never join `parts`, so they skip the BOM, checks and stop-on-edit.

On the canvas they live in a second `PartLayer` on `stage.worldRoot` (`ViewportProps.worldParts`). Picking, USDZ export, interference, measures and framing all read `ctx.layer` and `partsRoot`, so they never see the world. Silhouette, label and `onlyIds` captures hide it. `setPose(deltas, worldDeltas)` routes poses to both layers.

### Live drive

Drive works in the live sim, never as a physics-off puppet. A command is `{type: 'command', v: [vx, vy, wz], target?, aux?}` in `SimRequest`: forward, left and counter-clockwise turn, each −1 to 1 (ROS order), an optional end-effector target in scene mm, and `aux` (arm lift, grip). `src/sim/drive.ts` turns it into actuator targets by class, chosen by `driveAccess` (`src/sim/driveAccess.ts`) from the robot's morphology:

- **Arm:** a warm-started `solveIK` at 50 Hz in the worker, on a `Rig` cloned without `rig.parts` (`rigForWorker`). The aim moves at 0.9 × reach per second and stays inside the reach sphere. When the keys let go, the drive holds the hand where it is: the engine hands it the measured joints plus each servo's gravity sag (`qfrc_bias / kp`), so the hand neither catches up the servo lag nor droops (it used to run on 22 mm).
- **Rover:** a differential mix over the `<velocity>` wheel actuators. Each wheel's spin sign is `(axis × up) · forward`, so reversed axes still roll forward; its lateral offset is from the wheels' centre along the robot's left.
- **Legs (four or more):** `src/sim/driveLegs.ts` bakes stand, forward, strafe and turn cycles with `buildGait` (per-foot directions, true alternating phases for six feet, point feet), each in both directions, then blends them by command each tick, ramping in from the pose over 0.5 s. A negative command plays the reversed bake, never the cycle backwards in time: played backwards, its stance falls on the other cycles' swing, so walking while turning right barely turned. The bake is kept per setup object (`bakeCycles`), so a respawn or a second controller change doesn't bake again. Drive unlocks only after a 5 s headless fall check (`fallCheckPassed`: no fall, tilt under 35°, at least 3 cm forward, height above 60%). The fixture hexapod and all three quadrupeds pass; the weak quadruped is refused.
- **Through a skill:** a legged robot with a ready Walk (the Motor Kit's velocity tracking, §8i) drives through that policy, bipeds included, with no fall check; it comes before the scripted gait.
- **Never a refusal** (`driveOffer`, `withVelocity`): two legs, wheels on legs, or a walker that fails its fall check get **Teach It to Move** in place of Drive, which trains the Kit's Walk (or sends `/train move` when the robot has no Kit, like wheels on legs); a robot with nothing that moves it around or reaches gets no Drive control. Stand, Push and Grab stay. The agent's `start_drive` answers with the same action in words.

`useDrive` (`src/sim/useDrive.ts`) owns the drive state and the fall check; `useDriveKeys` (`src/workspace/useDriveKeys.ts`) reads W A S D or the arrows, Q E, Space, R (start over: `engine.respawn` puts the robot back without touching the world) and Esc (stop). While driving, `useWorkspaceKeys` runs no plain or ⌘ key, so S, F, Backspace and undo never edit parts (any parts edit still stops the sim). Keys zero on window blur and when the page hides; with a text field focused the canvas says “Click the canvas to drive”, and a click on the canvas takes focus back. Gamepads are read in the sim's frame handler (`frameHookRef`), with no extra animation frame; a stick inside its deadzone never overrides the keys.

An arm drive reports its hand in `SimStats.effector` (mm, world frame, from the drive's bound part and point); the dev `simState()` passes it on. The canvas shows stats ten times a second, but `sim.latestStats()` returns the newest frame's, and the dev `simState()` and the gallery's `__gallery.state()` read that: a reading 100 ms stale put a stopped rover 3 cm short of where it really stopped.

The canvas overlay (`DriveOverlay`) shows “Fell. Press R.”, speed in m/s for robots that move around, and Esc with Stop Driving, the name the button and ⌘K use. The side panel's key list leaves Esc out: the overlay and the Stop Driving button already show it. The camera follows the robot's base (`followPart`, `FollowCamera`), and the key light keeps fitting while it follows.

### Tries

A try records at 50 Hz in the worker (`src/sim/recorder.ts`, hung on `SimEngine.stepHook`, the second step slot): time, actions, qpos, qvel and every body's pose (kinematics refreshed first, so poses match qpos). It ends when the Case's time runs out, on End Try, or when driving stops. `scoreTry` runs the Case's probes and contract over it; `tryManifest` stores the Case and model hashes, seed, frame count and step. Recordings live in IndexedDB (`basecase-tries`, `src/cases/tryStore.ts`); `project.tries` holds only the records and `sharedScene` strips them from `.basecase` files, like policies. Replay sets the recorded qpos frame by frame in the worker.

The filmstrip (`src/cases/filmstrip.ts`, drawn by `filmstrip.worker.ts` on an `OffscreenCanvas`) projects each part's box and the world's objects at four moments of a try, in the same three-quarter view as captures. Results list tries worst first.

A recording also keeps each joint's `qpos` address (`Recording.jointQpos`, from `SimEngine.jointAddresses`), so a dataset can read joint state without loading MuJoCo. Tries recorded before it existed have none and can't be exported.

### Datasets

**Export Dataset** (Results section and ⌘K, shown once the open Case has a passed try) turns the open Case's passed tries into a LeRobot v2.1 dataset with GR00T's `meta/modality.json`, downloaded as one zip. It's the demonstration data that GR00T, LeRobot or any vision-language-action model fine-tunes on: Cases with live drive are the teleop, the contract is the success label, and the Case's brief is the instruction. Code in `src/datasets/`.

- **Only tries that match the robot now.** A try counts when it passed and its `manifest.modelHash` equals the SHA-256 of the current model's XML (the open Case's world included); the toast says how many were left out. Missed tries never go in: a dataset is demonstrations of the task done right.
- **Rows** (`episodes.ts`): every second 50 Hz record frame, so `DATASET_FPS` = 25. `observation.state` is the robot's joint positions (rad or m, `SimModel.joints` order for `robots[0]`), `action` is each joint's actuator control at the same frame (the position target, or the velocity on a wheel). One task per Case: its brief, else its name. `next.reward` is 1 on the last frame (every exported try passed), `next.done` marks it, and `annotation.human.task_description` repeats `task_index` for GR00T.
- **Files** (`lerobot.ts`, parquet through `hyparquet-writer` with an explicit schema so state and action are `list<float>`): `data/chunk-000/episode_000000.parquet`, `videos/chunk-000/observation.images.<camera>/episode_000000.mp4`, and in `meta/`: `info.json`, `episodes.jsonl`, `tasks.jsonl`, `episodes_stats.jsonl` (per-episode min, max, mean, std and count; images per channel in 0–1, shaped 3 × 1 × 1), `modality.json` (one `joints` group for state and action, each camera, the task annotation) and `basecase.json` (the Case, model hash and each episode's try id and margin). GR00T computes its own `stats.json`. GR00T reads LeRobot v2 only; LeRobot 0.4 and later read v3, and convert v2.1 with their own script.
- **Cameras** (`cameras.ts`): a fixed **scene** camera framing the robot and the Case's world from the front three-quarters (60° horizontal), then one per camera part (catalog group Cameras or Depth Cameras, `isCameraPart`) keyed by its name in snake case (`Wrist Camera` → `wrist_camera`). The view sits on the part's `lens` port looking along its axis; a camera with only `back` ports looks out of the opposite face; one with neither is left out. The lens comes from the shared camera model (§8l): the catalog's field of view, or the part's own `cameraLens`, and a fisheye renders as one through `LensRenderer`. A camera part rides on its body through `simDeltas`, as the canvas does, so a wrist camera moves with the wrist. Camera views aren't MJCF `<camera>`s: the sim model stays unchanged and every physics gate still holds.
- **Rendering off the main thread** (`renderVideos.ts`, `dataset.worker.ts`, client `datasetClient.ts`): the worker builds the visible solid parts and the world with `buildPartGroup` (meshes sent as in thumbnails), lights them with `createStudioScene` on a grey floor at y = 0, poses each sampled frame with `simDeltas` / `worldDeltas`, renders 640 × 480 per camera on one `OffscreenCanvas` and encodes it with WebCodecs through `mediabunny` (H.264, else VP9, else AV1; one key frame a second; `moov` first). The parquet and metadata are written in the same worker (`buildDataset` returns the files); its `zip` call also zips them (`src/utils/zipFolder.ts`: media stored, metadata deflated) for Export, and its `files` call hands them over as they are for Publish. The main thread only downloads or forwards the bytes. Nothing renders while a try records.
- **Tests:** `src/datasets/*.test.ts` check camera placement and field of view, read state and action back against a MuJoCo replay, and read the parquet back with `hyparquet`. `e2e/drive.e2e.test.ts` exports a real arm try in the browser through `__studio.datasetFiles()` (the export's zip, unzipped, on any try) and checks the rows, the codec and each video's size and frame count with `mediabunny`.

### Publishing to Hugging Face

**Publish to Hugging Face** (Results section under Export Dataset, and ⌘K; desktop only) sends the same files to a dataset repo on the signed-in account, `<account>/<case>-<hash>`, and the toast's Open goes to it. The six hex characters are the start of the SHA-256 of the scene id and the Case id (`repoName` in `useDatasetExport.ts`), so a template Case like Touch the Dots gets its own repo in each scene and publishing from one scene never replaces another's dataset. Two clicks in a row start one publish. Signed out, it opens Settings → Accounts instead (§6a). There's no second writer: the dataset worker's `files` call builds them, `src/hub/hubBridge.ts` passes them to the main process (IPC `hub:*`, or the bridge's `/hub-*` routes in the browser), and `electron/hub/` does every Hub request. The page never holds the token; it only learns whether someone is signed in and the account name.

- **The Hub's HTTP API** (`hubClient.mjs`, `publishRepo.mjs`): `POST /api/repos/create` (public; 409 means it's there), the repo's tree, `preupload` (the Hub says which files go through LFS: the parquet and videos), the LFS batch API with basic and multipart uploads and verify, then one NDJSON commit (`header`, `file` inline in base64, `lfsFile` by SHA-256, `deletedFile`). Publishing again replaces the dataset: files under the same top folders (`data/`, `videos/`, `meta/`) that the new set lacks are deleted, `README.md` and anything else stays. Then the `codebase_version` from `meta/info.json` (`v2.1`) is deleted and tagged again on the new commit, because LeRobot's reader loads a dataset at its version tag and refuses one without it.
- **Tests:** `electron/hub/hub.test.mjs` runs against a fake Hub (`e2e/fakeHub.ts`, an HTTP server that keeps repos, LFS objects, commits and tags in memory): repo creation, the commit payload, LFS and multipart uploads, updates, the token sealed on disk and absent from logs, errors and replies, and the exploit cases (§0h). `e2e/drive.e2e.test.ts` records a try on an arm, marks it passed (`__studio.passTries()`, dev only), clicks Publish, signs in through Settings with the fake's token, double-clicks Publish, and reads `meta/info.json`, the parquet and the video back from the fake. The harness lets `hub-*` bridge calls through to Vite's real backend, pointed at the fake by `BASECASE_HUB_URL` (honoured only for https or a loopback http address).

### In the app

- **Cases** tab in the Play view (§5). It opens with **New Case** (primary; tooltip “Pick a world and a goal”; hidden when the robot can try no goal), which opens the builder card in place: Goal (only the goals that fit; hidden when there's one), World, the goal's numbers, Name, then the brief it will get, or the fix when the numbers can't work, with Cancel (Esc) and Create (↩). Create adds the Case, opens it and switches to the Simulate tab. **Ask the Agent** (desktop only) puts `/case ` in the agent composer, focused. Then “In this scene” and “Examples”: the templates that fit this robot, and nothing else (a misfit is never listed with its reason). Without a robot: “No robot” + [Browse Robots].
- **Simulate tab** with a Case open: **Brief** (name, brief, Close), **Robot fit** (Check Fit: fits this Case (Yes or No, never a list of what's missing), compiles, settles 1.5 s, body class, fall check; skipped rows aren't shown; never restates Ready to build; a Case that doesn't fit offers New Case), **Try** (Try It, a clock, End Try, the keys), **Emulated robot** while one is connected (§8k: its rung, the newest daemon message and the servos; from Test up it follows the live sim), **Control**, **Train** (Teach it to and the skills list, §8d), **Results** (tries worst first, then Export Dataset and Publish to Hugging Face once one passed, § Datasets), then the Result, Model and Policies sections as before. Without a Case, Control has Drive.
- **Checks** (Build tab, §8b) lists every Case as a row with how many of 20 seeded runs it passed (§ Seeded runs); clicking the row opens the Case here.
- ⌘K: Show Cases, New Case, Drive or Stop Driving, Start Over, Try ⟨Case⟩, Export Dataset, Publish to Hugging Face.
- **Agent:** `create_case` (`electron/cad-mcp/caseTools.mjs` → bridge kind → `src/workspace/agentCaseRequests.ts`) builds a Case through the same builder from a goal, a world, numbers in mm and extra `set_world`-style objects in the agent frame, and replies `{ ok, id, name, brief, seconds, objects }` or `{ ok: false, problem }`. The prompt's Scope reads a message starting with `/case` as a request for one.
- `e2e/drive.e2e.test.ts` drives the rover, arm and hexapod in the real app, checks a biped gets Teach It to Move instead of Drive, key gating and the composer prompt, records a try, and makes a Case with New Case (only the fitting example listed, the Case opened with its world). The e2e harness allows the real `node_modules` path, so the MuJoCo WASM loads from a worktree whose `node_modules` is a link.

### Web gallery

`app.basecase.so/c/<slug>` opens one Case in the browser. `src/webMain.ts` reads the slug (`gallerySlug`: letters, digits and dashes only) before anything heavy loads; phones keep the landing page. The gallery page (`src/gallery/`) is its own small app: the canvas, the Case card (name, brief, Drive, See Inside) and the drive overlay. It starts the simulator as soon as the canvas is up, so MuJoCo's WASM is fetched while the visitor reads. Nothing autosaves; See Inside saves the scene once into the web app's IndexedDB, opens it as the first tab and goes to the studio.

- **Bundles** (`public/gallery/<slug>.json`, `index.json`) carry everything they need: the robot's parts and joints and the Case, validated by `parseGalleryBundle` on read. `npm run gallery` (`tools/gallery/build.mjs`, entries in `src/gallery/galleryEntries.ts`) writes them; a unit test fails when they drift from the code. A Case with meshes or a trained policy must inline them in its bundle, because `webBackend.ts` serves no policies.
- **Budgets** (`npm run perf:gallery`, `tools/perf/galleryBench.mjs`, production web build in headless Chrome): at most 8 MB compressed before driving, Drive working within 6 s of opening the link, and the live sim at real time (0.97× or better on its smoothed meter). Last run: rover 3.5 MB, 0.8 s, 1.00×; hexapod 6.7 MB (the fall check's worker fetches the WASM a second time), 1.0 s, 1.00×; arm 3.8 MB, 0.6 s, 1.00×.
- `e2e/gallery.e2e.test.ts` runs the web build mode (`IS_WEB_DEMO`): drives the rover, checks real time, See Inside, the phone landing and a wrong link.
- **Training in the tab** (Forge F6, §8g): a gallery page sets `window.__forge` (`src/forge/forgeHook.ts`), whose `train({ robot?, entry? })` runs the Motor Kit's entry (Stand by default) on the page's robot, or on a fixture robot by id, through Forge's WGSL engine on WebGPU, and resolves with Arena's verdict, steps and wall time; `events()` lists Forge's status and progress lines. Nothing loads until it's called: the hook imports `tabTraining` and the Forge worker on demand. There's no button for it, and nothing saves the skill. `e2e/forgeTab.e2e.test.ts` ("forge tab trains", gate `s1-f6-webgpu`) trains quad-servo's Stand from the hexapod link in headless Chrome on the GPU and needs Arena's pass within 15 minutes.
