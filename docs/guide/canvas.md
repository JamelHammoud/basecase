## 12. 3D Canvas

Board previews (`workspace/useBoardPreview.ts`) request the board solid and mounting bores from the electrical worker. The view substitutes that mesh only for the immutable matching host and board record. Other part identities stay intact. The saved rectangular host and its dimensions remain authoritative. Preview sends position and index buffers by transfer and releases its worker after delivery. Changing the board cancels the prior preview client, and stale results cannot replace the current view. Sourced connector models use their checked pin-one offsets, and picking or marquee-selecting them selects the carrier. `ViewportProps.sourceParts` retains the immutable source objects separately from this rendered projection. Cable-route preparation compares those source identities after model loading and still refuses a source edit during preparation. It measures the rendered solids, including the board bores, without treating derived header instances as another wiring graph. Generic assembly and interference measurements use source scene identities, avoiding the intentional pin press-fit inside a derived connector. Board fit owns the derived connector courtyard, access and pin-envelope checks against actual source geometry. Wiring obstacles, STL and USDZ exports include the derived connector geometry.

Derived headers receive their carrier’s pose through `ViewportProps.poseOwners`. `PartLayer` resolves that owner for drawing, picked-point and transform conversion, and temporary rest measurements. The same mapping applies to declarative poses and live simulation updates without changing source geometry or joints. `wiring/boardComponents.test.ts` covers carrier rotation and translation, picking, restoration after measurement and returning to rest. [Board test evidence](../notes/testing-build.md#board-placement-records) records the regression cases.

Collision baseline keys and crease adjacency share `electron/cad-kernel/pairKey.mjs` with the other unordered string-pair paths (§0f). It preserves lexical ordering and the existing separator without sorting a temporary pair.

Screw recess construction shares `three/hardwareRecessGeometry.ts` across socket, pan, button, countersunk and set screws. Cleanup reads each triangle’s indices and positions once, retaining three corner vectors for output and using two edge vectors for the strict squared-cross-product cutoff of `1e-12`. All five vectors and the three index slots are reused within the call. Material groups write positions and normals directly into their own Float32 buffers. Cleanup disposes its input geometry without modifying source attributes. Recess materials, edge flags and triangle order stay unchanged. `hardwareRecessGeometry.test.ts` compares complete CSG pieces for every supported recessed screw and drive, checks attribute layouts and the cutoff, and counts source reads. [Read-count evidence](../notes/performance-geometry.md#screw-recess-cleanup-retains-source-corners).

Imported mesh files and robot-link visuals share `linkSoup` in `three/geometryOps.ts`. The geometry worker transforms each source vertex directly into the combined triangle buffer and accumulates bounds in the same pass. Bounds use float32-rounded coordinates, matching the stored triangles. Source arrays stay unchanged, and each call owns its output and transform workspace.

Captured Case fixtures reuse the geometry worker to build source shapes and decompose their collision surfaces. Local hull vertices receive the source rotation before returning as transferable buffers. Source CAD, mesh and affine data remain unchanged. The geometry client loads its local fallback handlers dynamically, keeping their geometry and decomposition imports inside the worker during browser use. [Fixture geometry coverage](cases.md#passive-lid-and-drawer-fixtures) and [preparation observations](../notes/performance-cases.md#reusable-fixture-preparation).

Recorded map fusion and world reconstruction share chronological sensor ordering in `dataLayers/mapFeed.ts`. Each call indexes sensor ranks once for timestamp ties, preserving scan, cloud and depth order, then message order within each sensor. No rank index survives the call.

Recorded world reconstruction shares `dataLayers/mapWorldBoxes.ts` across planar walls, inferred floors and measured 3D surfaces. Box growth checks each adjacent face directly and stops at its first missing cell, without copied face bounds or cell lists. Consumed cells use scalar coordinate keys. Axis order, cell order, exact occupied coverage and piece-budget refusals stay unchanged.

Coplanar collision face sampling (`three/analysis/shapeSolid.ts`) writes subdivided centers through one reusable vector into each shape’s cached buffers. Those buffers hold centers, normals and area weights. Face preparation also accumulates the surface total from Float32-rounded weights in emission order, removing the later area-buffer reduction. Each visited triangle computes one cross product, shared by its area and normal. `facePieces.test.ts` checks exact Float32 normals and weights against Three.js triangle calculations, sampling boundaries, winding, stride, cache ownership and one cross product per visited triangle. [Evidence](../notes/performance-collision.md#face-sampling-shares-area-and-normal-products). Sampling retains its barycentric order, minimum area, stride and subdivision cap (§0f). Each face-preparation call builds barycentric weights once for each subdivision count it encounters, then reuses them across triangles. The operation-local cache holds at most 12 grids and ends with preparation. `facePieces.test.ts` compares every emitted center against the previous calculation across all 12 grids and independently prepared shapes. [Weight preparation evidence](../notes/performance-collision.md#face-sampling-prepares-barycentric-grids-once).

Visibility surface sampling (`three/analysis/visibility.ts`) shares the cumulative-area binary lookup in `utils/surfaceSampleIndex.ts` with printed-label sampling. The lookup retains the first matching boundary, including repeated areas. Visibility keeps its existing seed, 4,000 samples per part and shape-local sample cache. World placement writes these samples directly into one combined Float32 buffer through one reusable vector, preserving sample and owner order. It rounds each transformed coordinate with `Math.fround` before extending the shared bounds, removing the later bounds scan while retaining the stored Float32 projection limits. `visibility.test.ts` covers exact shares, mixed empty caches, transform edits and one vector load per sample. [Coverage and evidence](../notes/performance-collision.md#visible-share-projections-reuse-one-workspace).

Imported Case visuals (`three/viewport/importedWorld.ts`) attach to `stage.worldRoot` alongside the world PartLayer. Embedded mesh textures use the imported sampler settings. Spark receives splat bytes directly and schedules redraws through `onDirty` after worker loading or sorting completes. Moving the same world retains its buffers. Closing or replacing it disposes textures, splats and the renderer, and rejects stale asynchronous loads. Collision pieces stay hidden.

The imported world visual layer (`three/viewport/importedWorld.ts`) also serves camera and dataset workers. Its manual mode disables Spark automatic updates and exposes an awaited `prepare(scene, camera)` for deterministic captures. Canvas rendering keeps automatic updates and on-demand redraw callbacks.

Coplanar contact scans (`three/analysis/collide.ts`) reuse one triangle and its three corner vectors per directional scan. Consecutive nearby samples hitting the same target face reuse its normal. A changed face reloads all corners through the current BVH index before calculating the next normal. Interference checks and Study grading share this path. Joint sweeps disable coplanar checks. The workspace ends with the scan, preserving independent calls and current geometry.

- **Build step display** (`three/viewport/buildStep.ts`, the `buildStep` prop, §8b): a display state, never a scene edit. Parts outside `shown` are hidden through their groups' `visible` (the batches follow it), so measurements, which read `part.visible`, are unchanged. While it's set the layer's pose map is the step's own: each move starts at its offset and eases to the seat (450 ms hold, 900 ms move) through `RenderLoop.animators`, which re-poses only the moved parts (`PartLayer.applyPoseTo`) and renders only while they move; reduced motion skips it. A repair removal sets `BuildStepView.removing`, starts at the seat and finishes at its outward offset, holding there until the next step. Reduced motion applies that final offset immediately. Repair steps provide explicit shown ids so removed parts disappear on subsequent steps while untouched branches remain visible. Arrows are blue cylinders and cones on `stage.overlays`, drawn over everything; servo labels are glass chips placed after each render at the top of the part's box. Wiring routes draw the analysis worker's world points as blue polylines on the same overlay stage. Changing or clearing a step disposes the previous route buffers, and routes add no animation loop. The gizmo, clicks, marquee and selection outline are off. Clearing it restores visibility and the scene's pose. `shownPartIds` on the handle lists the parts on screen.
- **Skeleton** (§8c, `three/viewport/skeletonOverlay.ts`): while Edit Skeleton is on, `project.skeleton` draws on `stage.overlays` as white 2px bones (parent to child, joint to tip) and 14px joint balls coloured by torque margin, both over everything (no depth test), so parts never rebuild and captures never show it. A press within 12px of a ball grabs it before orbit and selection (capture-phase `pointerdown`, `draggingGizmo` set until the release); the cursor turns to move over a ball. A release without moving is a click, which opens the joint's servo menu. When a skeleton appears in a scene with no parts (Arm or Biped), the camera frames it, down to the floor (`frameBox`).
- **Case trails** (`three/viewport/caseTrails.ts`, §8h): opening a Case's Checks row draws each seeded base or effector path as a thin line on `stage.overlays`, in the Checks pass and fail colors. Clicking within six screen pixels of a failed segment opens that seed's recording on the try timeline. Dragging still orbits. Trail changes replace only their line buffers and invalidate the existing render loop. Captures hide the overlay group. `setCaseTrails` and `caseTrailState` on the handle support the workspace and its test hook.
- **Data layers** (`src/dataLayers/`, drawn by `three/viewport/dataLayers.ts`): a robot's sensor data in 3D, on `stage.overlays` (so captures never show it), redrawn only when a layer's message changes. One `DataTrack` per topic or source: its message times and a `message(i)` read on demand, in the ROS message shapes (`dataLayers/messages.ts`, ROS field names). Kinds: point clouds (`PointCloud2`, colored by a field: a float field on a turbo ramp, `rgb` / `rgba` as packed bytes), laser scans (`LaserScan`, and the sim's own hits in scene mm, `scene/Points`), TF frames (`TFMessage`: 100 mm red, green and blue axes, the frame name in a glass chip within 12 px of the pointer, `frameLabel.ts`), paths (`Path`, and `Odometry` as a trail to the playhead with an arrow at its end), markers (`Marker` / `MarkerArray` arrows, cubes, spheres, cylinders, cube and sphere lists, points, line strips and lists, with add, delete and delete-all; `foxglove.SceneUpdate` cubes, spheres, cylinders, arrows and lines), occupancy grids (`OccupancyGrid` as a textured plane: unknown cells clear, free cells dark at 25% opacity, occupied up to white) and poses (`PoseStamped`) as arrows.
  - **Frames.** `three/viewport/dataFrames.ts` resolves a message's `frame_id` through the TF tree at the playhead (moving transforms interpolated, static ones held) to the robot's links by name, else to a link elsewhere in the same tree, else the tree's root to the robot's base. ROS metres with Z up become scene millimetres with Y up (`ROS_TO_SCENE`, the same mapping as MuJoCo's `fromMj`). A frame it can't resolve isn't drawn, and the layer's row in Layers shows the warn mark with the frame's name.
    Each path accumulates one transform and stops at its nearest robot link, without retaining matrices for intermediate frames. Paths through a sibling link use the same accumulator up to the root. The traversal stays bounded on cycles and retains no matrices between calls, so seeking and moving anchors resolve afresh. `dataFrames.paths.test.ts` checks placements, input preservation, cycles and operation counts on 300-edge and 600-edge chains. Before accumulation, the 600-edge fixture read 601 edges despite a link one hop away, and a full path cloned 602 matrices.
  - **Time.** The layer store (`dataLayers/store.ts`, one per scene tab, made by `useDataLayers`) holds a playhead: none while live, so each layer draws its newest message, and the try timeline's time while a try replays, so each draws the message at or before it (`spanAt`). A layer's `decayS` keeps older scans and clouds in the window; odometry and markers accumulate from the start.
  - **Buffers.** Each layer owns one point buffer (positions as floats, colors as normalized bytes) that grows by fourfold steps from 4,096 to 1,000,000 points and then stays; later messages rewrite it in place with an update range, never a new array. Markers draw as one `InstancedMesh` per shape and one `LineSegments`; a grid reuses its `DataTexture` while its size holds.
    Sensor polylines share `dataLayers/shapes.ts`: paths, odometry trails, ROS line markers and Foxglove lines transform each strip vertex once, reusing the previous endpoint for its next segment. Strips and line lists write endpoints directly without temporary segment arrays. Empty strips and single points draw nothing, and line lists ignore an unpaired last point. `shapes.test.ts` checks exact coordinates and colors under affine and projective transforms, unchanged inputs, independent draws and transform counts. A 600-point strip takes 600 transforms, down from 1,198, without a timing assertion.
  - **The sim's LiDARs** (§8l) are layers named after their part, under the robot in Layers: live scans from each frame message, and during a replay the try's recorded ranges re-placed from the recorded body poses (`dataLayers/simTracks.ts`).
  - **An imported recording's topics** (§8h, Imported tries) are layers named after their topic while its try is open: each drawable topic's message at a time is rebuilt from the try's columns (`src/topics/topicTracks.ts`, eight kept at once), `/tf` and `/tf_static` among them, so its frames resolve to the robot's links by name.
  - **Scan maps** (§8h, Scan maps) add a layer named **Map** (`scene/Map`) from the whole open try, or accumulate live simulated LiDAR hits while the simulation is loaded. Its points are 5 cm voxel centroids, capped at 1,000,000, colored by height on the turbo ramp. The fused cloud uses root-frame ROS meters and draws through `ROS_TO_SCENE` directly, without anchoring it to a moving robot link. The complete map stays fixed while the other data layers follow the playhead. Right-click its row in Layers for **Scan Matching**, on by default for imported data, which rebuilds the map with or without point-to-plane ICP. Matching reuses one neighborhood buffer, point, Jacobian and normal-equation system per sweep across all ICP iterations. Each neighborhood scan reads only its populated cells, and normal equations clear before each iteration. Each equation row accumulates only the upper half of the unweighted normal matrix, which is mirrored before solving. Cached structures retain their own anchors and normals. Simulated hits bypass matching. The layer is on `stage.overlays`, so captures and camera views never include it, and remains separate from saved worlds. For an open recorded ROS try, **Make World** in this menu and ⌘K freezes the try and matching choice, reconstructs 0.1 m occupied surfaces in the mapping worker, and opens **New Case** with the result selected (§8h). The saved world uses the existing world layer and collider rendering, so closing the try removes only the Map overlay. Planar worlds label their estimated floor and 1 m obstacle height. Reconstruction reuses one transformed point per sweep and three ray axes for laser and depth returns (`dataLayers/mapWorldCells.ts`). Each axis retains its origin cell and resets its traversal values for every return. Point clouds share the point workspace without creating free-space rays.
  - `setDataLayers` on the handle takes the store's input and returns each layer's counts (`dataLayerState`: points, shapes, segments, grid, axes, the missing frame and the time of the message shown); `scanPointCount` sums the scan layers. The dev `simState()` reads both, with `dataPlayhead`.
- **The Earth layer** (`three/viewport/earthLayer.ts`, the page's side in `src/earth/`, the main process's in `electron/earth/`): **Place on Map** (⌘K and the empty canvas menu, desktop only, §11) draws Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles of a real place under the scene, through `3d-tiles-renderer` (NASA-AMMOS) on `stage.overlays`, so captures never show it. The place is `project.earth` (`EarthOrigin`: latitude and longitude in degrees, `altitudeM` or null, `offsetM`, `hidden`), saved with the scene and read through `checkedOrigin`, which drops a crafted value.
  - **Placing.** The sheet's one field takes coordinates (`37.7793, -122.4193`, hemisphere letters, an optional third number for the height above the WGS84 ellipsoid) or a Google Maps link (its `!3d…!4d…` pin, a `q`, `query`, `ll`, `center` or `destination` parameter, else its `@lat,lon`), read by `readPlace`; Place stays disabled until it reads. Without a saved key, Place on Map opens Settings ▸ Accounts with the Map Tiles key focused (`openSettings('accounts', 'map-tiles')`).
  - **Frame.** `earthFrame` (`src/earth/enu.ts`) is the inverse of the place's East-North-Up frame, then `ROS_TO_SCENE`, the same mapping as data layers: east is +X, north is −Z, up is +Y, meters become millimeters. The floor is at the height the origin gives, or, when it has none, the tiles' own surface under the spot: after each batch of loads the layer casts a ray down through the loaded tiles at the spot and re-places itself on the hit (`sample`), finer each time a finer tile arrives. Until the first hit the layer is hidden, and a narrow probe camera 50 m above the spot keeps the tiles under it loading. `offsetM` lifts the robot from that floor.
  - **Drawing.** Level of detail is the renderer's screen-space error (target 20 px) for the canvas camera, so tiles near the camera load finer and far ones stay coarse; the canvas's 80 m far plane bounds what draws. Tile materials get a polygon offset, so the grid and the shadow plane draw over a surface at the floor. The tiles' attribution (each glTF's `asset.copyright`, counted over the visible tiles, most frequent first) draws in a glass chip in the canvas's lower left (`[data-testid="map-attribution"]`). **Hide Map** (⌘K) and **Show Map** (canvas menu) set `hidden`, which keeps the place. `earthState` on the handle reports visible tiles, load progress, the sampled surface, the root tile’s center in the scene and the attribution; the dev `__studio.earth()` reads it and `__studio.saveMapKey(key)` saves a key.
  - **Fetching.** `EarthFetchPlugin` (`tileFetch.ts`) turns every URL the renderer asks for into a path under `/v1/3dtiles/` on `tile.googleapis.com` (refusing anything else, §0h), keeps the session the root tileset names, and asks the main process for the bytes; JSON comes back parsed, everything else as an `ArrayBuffer`. Google's tiles are Draco-compressed: `GLTFExtensionsPlugin` gets a `DRACOLoader` whose decoder is three's own, bundled as assets (`src/earth/draco.ts`), so nothing loads from a CDN. A failed tile shows its error once in a toast. The web studio doesn't draw the layer.
  - **Tests** use `src/earth/tileFixture.ts`: a three-level tileset built in code around a spot in San Francisco, flat squares 3 m, 1 m and 0 m above a 16 m ground, served by `e2e/fakeTiles.ts`.
- **Check dots** (`three/viewport/checkDots.ts`): an expanded check with points to show (Payload, §8b) draws them as 5 px dots on `stage.overlays`, so captures never show them, coloured from grey `#8a8a8a` to the accent by each dot's `shade`. They test depth but don't write it. `checkDotCount` on the handle counts them for e2e tests.
- **The measured robot** (`three/viewport/ghostLayer.ts`): while a robot connected from this scene runs in Test or above (§8k), a translucent Figma-blue copy of its moving parts follows the servos' measured positions beside the solid sim robot. `useRobotGhost` poses it with `poseDeltas` from the latest status (`robotGhost` in `src/robot/robotGhost.ts`) and hands it to the handle's `setGhost` once per status event, every 100 ms, then the render is invalidated; there's no loop. Its meshes reuse each part group's geometry with one shared material (no copies) and are rebuilt only when the part is. It lives on `stage.overlays` with raycasting off, so captures, thumbnails and picking never see it. A chip (`[data-testid="ghost-gap"]`) at the joint with the largest gap shows that gap in signed degrees; `ghostState` on the handle reports the posed parts, values and label for tests.
- **Infinite grid** (`three/infiniteGrid.ts`): a camera-following shader plane on y = 0, not a fixed `GridHelper`.
  - 1 / 10 / 100 / 1000 mm lines fade in and out by on-screen density, so there's no moiré when zoomed out or at grazing angles.
  - The red X and blue Z axes run forever, and the grid fades into the distance (the fade distance scales with zoom).
  - `toneMapped: false` keeps line contrast exact; lines sit about 15–25 levels above the background, and the axes are brighter.
- Edges: Figma blue `#0d99ff` for selection, red `#ef4444` for cutouts.
- **Selection outline** (`three/viewport/selectionOutline.ts`): selected parts also get a 2.5px Figma-blue outline around their combined silhouette, drawn over everything (so a part inside a shell still shows), and around a multi-selection or group as one shape. Before each render the selected meshes are drawn into a 4× MSAA single-channel mask (camera layer 7, override material), then a quad in `stage.overlays` (so captures never show it), covering only the selection's screen rectangle, dilates the mask. The dilation loop spans only the outline's radius at the current pixel ratio (`REACH`: 7 × 7 samples at 1×, 13 × 13 at 2×), since every pixel of the rectangle runs it. Nothing is rebuilt; with no selection the pass is skipped.
- **Software GL** (`three/softwareGl.ts`, read once from the unmasked renderer name: SwiftShader, llvmpipe): the canvas, the outline mask and the camera feed drop MSAA, and shader warm-up doesn't run (§0f). With SwiftShader every pixel is drawn on the CPU, and in a Linux container like CI's, MSAA at 1440 × 900 cut the idle canvas from about 28 to 7 frames a second. Nothing changes on a GPU.
- Catalog components (manufacturer meshes) and hardware (fasteners) draw their edge lines only when selected or set as a cutout. Unselected, their dense feature edges showed through enclosing shells as dark specks; the judge read those as overlapping geometry.
- B-rep edges are drawn only at creases (over 20° between the faces on each side). Smooth seams from lofts, splines and tangent fillets aren't outlined, so lofted shells don't get contour stripes. The worker and fallback build share `partMesh/creaseEdges.ts`, which classifies adjacent faces only for supplied B-rep segments. Coordinate keys round to 0.01 mm and are cached by vertex index within each call, so adjacent triangles reuse keys without retaining stale coordinates across mesh edits. Boundary, unmatched, degenerate and non-manifold segments stay in source order, including duplicates.
- The directional light's shadow `normalBias` scales with the shadow frustum (about 1.5 shadow-map texels), so large robots don't get shadow-acne specks.
- Sheet thickness edits (§8b) use local dimensions when the face normal follows a local axis. Tilted profiles use a cumulative `ScenePart.sheetTransform` matrix on the group's direct children, preserving the source mesh and the exact affine matrix, including shear. The transform stays when the part returns to printing. Detection requires a closed mesh with matching parallel cap boundaries, perpendicular through walls, and thickness no greater than the smaller profile extent. Plane and matching-loop tolerances are 0.002 mm, and side-normal dot products tolerate 0.003 to cover agent meshes rounded to 0.001 mm. Measurements snapshot the child matrices, and print shape keys include the transform, so geometry analysis and exports see the changed physical thickness.
- Solid part faces use a small polygon offset, so edge lines always draw cleanly on top. Without it, outlines lying on another surface (a flush panel, a coincident face) flicker as dark speckles.
- Gestures: two-finger orbit (or pan), Space-drag pan, pinch zoom, gizmo drag.
- **Clicks for Sketch** (§8): a click that selects a part also reports the point it hit (`onPickPoint`), which Sketch uses to open on that face; a double-click on a part reports it too (`onOpenPart`), which reopens the sketch of a part Sketch made. Sketch's snap glyphs (coincident, horizontal, vertical) and constraint glyphs draw on the sketch plane in the Inspector, not in 3D.
- **Cursors** (`CURSORS` in `src/design-system/cursors.ts`, Figma-style): the canvas has its own arrow (near-black, white 1.5px stroke, every corner rounded, soft shadow), the same shape as the blue `AgentCursor`, so the two read as a pair. Comment mode shows a bubble in the pin's shape and the arrow's colors; its tip corner is where the pin's corner lands. Sculpt shows an outlined crosshair. Picking a joint axis (§8c) shows the crosshair over a round face of the two parts and the system not-allowed cursor elsewhere. Space-pan keeps the system grab hands.
  - They're CSS cursors drawn by the OS (never a DOM element following the pointer), so they never lag. Each is a 32 × 32 px SVG shipped at 2x in `image-set()` for Retina, with its hotspot on the exact pixel the tip shows (the arrow's stroke tip at (3, 3)).
  - The canvas element inherits its wrapper's cursor (`.canvas-cursor > canvas` in `index.css`), because three.js controls write `cursor: auto` onto the canvas. Comment pins use the arrow too.
- **Zoom range** 25 mm – 12 m camera distance (`MIN_DIST` / `MAX_DIST` in `three/viewport/types.ts`), enough for humanoids.
  - The far plane is 80 m, and the near plane scales with distance.
  - Grid fade, shadow plane and the directional light's shadow frustum all follow the camera distance.
- **The camera can go under the floor** to inspect undersides: orbit (drag and two-finger scroll) is limited only near the poles (`MIN_POLAR` / `MAX_POLAR` in `three/viewport/types.ts`). From below, the grid draws at 60% strength (it never writes depth, so parts on the floor show through it without z-fighting), and the shadow plane faces up, so it's culled and never hides parts.
- **Port snapping** (`three/viewport/portSnap.ts`, logic in `agent/snap.ts`, §9): moving one part with the gizmo, or dropping a Parts or Library tile, snaps it onto another part by their mounting ports, the way the agent's `mate` places parts.
  - While the drag lasts, ports that have a compatible partner nearby show as dots (`portDots.ts`, a `THREE.Points` overlay in `stage.overlays`, so captures never show them): on the dragged part and on parts within 60 mm or the part's own size. The pair about to snap turns Figma blue.
  - A dragged port within the snap distance of a compatible port snaps: the part takes the pose `planMate` gives that pair, live, under the pointer. The snap distance is 8 mm (`SNAP_MM`) out to 400 mm of camera distance and grows with it beyond, so it stays about the same on screen.
  - Releasing commits the pose and the mate (`PartMate`, the newest one replacing the part's older mates) as one undo step (`onPlacePart` → `placePart`). Dragging a mated part without a snap drops its mates.
  - Holding ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows; `runtime.freeHeld`) moves freely; pressing or letting go mid-drag re-solves at once. Grid snapping still applies; Shift turns that off as before.
  - Ports come from part data (`portsAt`), never meshes. The target parts' world ports are gathered once when the drag starts and the dots update only on pointer moves while dragging. Snapping is off while the robot is posed, for multi-part selections and groups (the pivot), and in Rotate and Scale.
- **Shift while using the gizmo** turns off grid, angle and scale snapping for smooth motion. A “Snapping off” glass chip shows while dragging. Commits keep 0.1 mm / 0.1° precision instead of rounding to 0.5 mm / 1°. Shift-dragging a gizmo handle never starts a box select (`transformControls.axis` check).

## Performance: mesh preparation

Shared mesh packing (`three/packedMesh.ts`) retains existing Float32 positions and Uint32 indices, including subarray offsets. Ordinary arrays still convert once per immutable source identity. Camera, thumbnail, dataset, build-guide and Routine scene delivery share this helper with geometry and boolean preparation. Packed buffers are read-only inputs and must not be transferred away or mutated. `packedMesh.test.ts` covers conversion, mixed inputs, cached identities, subarrays and complete crease output after structured cloning. [Evidence](../notes/performance-geometry.md#mesh-packing-retains-already-packed-buffers).

- **Captured-world camera rendering stays in workers** (§8l). Camera tiles, frame streams, agent shots and dataset videos reuse the canvas's Spark/mesh world layer inside their existing OffscreenCanvas stages. The imported asset is sent on scene changes, never with pose frames. Spark updates finish before a capture. [Evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#captured-world-camera-rendering-stays-in-workers).

- **The viewport never rebuilds everything.** `src/three/viewport/` is a plain TypeScript engine behind the thin `Viewport3D` component. `PartLayer.sync` diffs parts by object identity: unchanged parts are kept, a transform-only change moves the group, and only changed or re-selected parts are rebuilt. Parts are immutable; always replace a part object to change it, never mutate one. Builds are time-sliced (about 10 ms per frame), so opening a big scene never freezes; queries that need the whole scene (captures, measurements, picking, framing) finish pending builds and spawn animations first (`NEEDS_WHOLE_SCENE` in `engine.ts`): a part caught mid pop-in is scaled down, and the print report caches what it measures.

- **Parts draw in batches, not one draw call each.** `PartBatches` (`three/viewport/partBatches.ts`) draws every plain part mesh in a three.js `BatchedMesh` per material (`MeshBatch`, one geometry slot shared by every copy of a shape, colour per instance) and every part's edge lines in one `LineBatch` per line material (one buffer, instance index per vertex, matrices in a float texture).
  - Part groups stay exactly as before for picking, snapshots, bounds, the gizmo and the outline; their batched meshes and lines just move to `BATCHED_LAYER` (off layer 0, so the main and shadow passes skip them). Every part raycaster comes from `partRaycaster()`, which enables that layer. Code that hides a part uses `visible`, never layers.
  - `scene.onBeforeRender` syncs matrices, visibility, colours and in-place geometry edits (attribute versions) after three updates world matrices. Materials are replaced, never edited in place: a new material or geometry object re-tracks the mesh.
  - Batchable: an opaque, untextured, non-glowing `MeshStandardMaterial` mesh with position and normal and at most 32k vertices (`batchKey.ts`); selected parts glow, so they draw alone. Bigger meshes draw alone: a draw call is nothing next to their vertices, and copying them would cost more. Lines: plain `LineBasicMaterial` segments.
  - Freed geometry slots are reused best-fit (`setGeometryAt` re-uploads only that range); a batch compacts only when half of it is waste and grows by doubling.
  - Captures that isolate parts, swap materials or draw silhouettes run inside `batches.unbatched(...)`, which puts the originals back on layer 0 for the call; plain captures and screenshots stay batched. Edges that arrive late from the worker announce themselves with `noteLateChildren(group)`.
  - `warmShaders` also compiles the batched program. Deleting a geometry in `BatchedMesh` never frees its space, and growing or compacting re-uploads the whole buffer. [Evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#parts-draw-in-batches-not-one-draw-call-each).

- **Sculpt brushes reuse per-stroke workspaces.** `three/sculptStroke.ts` allocates one brush-state object per stroke and reuses its scratch normal vector for Inflate, Carve, Flatten and Pinch. Smooth and Grab share the same state reuse. Five independent brushes apply during the radius scan. Only Smooth collects affected indices and falloffs to calculate a shared center before moving vertices. [Evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#sculpt-applies-independent-brushes-during-the-radius-scan). Flatten, Smooth, Pinch and Grab prepare their strength multiplier and cap once per stroke. Their vertex operations reuse that blend with the original multiplication order. `sculptStroke.test.ts` covers uncapped and capped displacement, untouched vertices and one clamp for both mesh sizes. [Strength preparation evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#sculpt-prepares-strength-blends-once-per-stroke).

- **Captures encode off the main thread.** `captureViews` and `captureScreenshot` render with the live renderer, copy at the requested size into an `OffscreenCanvas` right after rendering (`transferToImageBitmap`, synchronous: a WebGL canvas has no preserved drawing buffer, and `createImageBitmap` on it reads blank), and encode in the image worker (`utils/image.worker.ts`, `encodeBitmap`). Helpers are restored before encoding finishes. Export Image and Save to Library await a full-size PNG. A scene tab's preview requests its final 560px JPEG directly, without encoding a full-size PNG and decoding it again. `downscaleDataUrl` goes through the same worker. Never `toDataURL` a capture on the main thread.

- **Part-isolated captures index requested ids once.** `three/viewport/captures.ts` shares `hideForViews` across agent views and silhouette fitting. Each call builds one membership set, removing a requested-id scan per scene part. Empty requests keep the whole scene, image planes remain excluded, and restoration preserves previously hidden objects. `captures.test.ts` covers duplicate and missing ids, empty requests, restoration, later request edits and bounded id reads. [Evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#part-isolated-captures-index-requested-ids-once).

- **Export STL never rebuilds a part.** It snapshots the canvas's own rest-pose meshes of the visible solid parts (`exportStl`, `restSolids`) and writes the binary STL in an analysis worker (`stl` task, `sceneStl.ts`, through `StlWriter`), the way 3MF plates are written; `__studio.stlBase64` reads the same file.

- **Thumbnails render in a worker** (`thumbnail.worker.ts`, OffscreenCanvas, encoded with `convertToBlob`): Library, Parts, scene previews and robot previews all go through `renderPartGroups`, which is async. A try's filmstrip draws and encodes in `filmstrip.worker.ts` (§8h), and the agent's camera shots encode in the image worker (`encodeBitmap`). Never `toDataURL` or `convertToBlob` a thumbnail, filmstrip or camera shot on the main thread: reading a canvas back there is a synchronous GPU round trip, which waits behind every frame the GPU process has queued.

- **Shaders are compiled before they're needed.** `warmShaders` compiles the scene (gizmo included) and the part materials with `compileAsync` at startup, finishes one program per idle callback, then renders one hidden shadow pass, so the first part, selection or shadow never stalls. On software GL (SwiftShader, llvmpipe: CI and VMs, `softwareGl()`) it doesn't run. Without it, the first render compiles only the programs it draws. [Evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#shaders-are-compiled-before-theyre-needed).

- **Camera-follow lighting uses no temporary vectors.** `three/viewport/renderLoop.ts` snapshots the key light's three coordinates as scalars and applies the fill offset with `addScaledVector`. Every studio viewport shares this path. Camera up, orbit target, light fitting thresholds and shadow invalidation stay unchanged. `renderLoop.lighting.test.ts` covers exact light positions, unchanged frames, independent viewports, interaction, following, shadow span changes and explicit invalidation. [Allocation evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#camera-follow-lighting-omits-temporary-vectors).

- **Render on demand.** `RenderLoop` draws only when something changed (`invalidate()`), the camera moved, an animation runs, or a texture finished loading, plus a 1 s idle refresh. Code that changes the scene outside the engine's own paths calls `invalidate()`. Never add an always-on `requestAnimationFrame` render. Camera moves call `noteInteraction`, not `invalidate`: the shadow map (`autoUpdate = false`) re-renders only when the scene changed, and the key light refits once the camera settles. Anything that renders a changed scene itself (captures) sets `shadowMap.needsUpdate`. Nothing redraws for a change no one can see:
  - `setPose` returns early when every delta is within 0.01 mm and 1e-5 of the one shown (`samePose.ts`), so a live sim's resting robot doesn't re-render the scene and its shadows at 60 Hz.
  - `update` returns early when no prop changed but callbacks (`shownPropsChanged`), and agent frames rebuilt with the same parts keep their identity (`steadyProps`). A Case try re-renders the workspace ten times a second for its stats; each one used to redraw the canvas.
  - A new LiDAR scan redraws through `redraw()`, which leaves the shadow map alone, and only when a hit moved more than 0.01 mm: `LiveScans` (`src/dataLayers/liveScans.ts`) keeps the layer's last track when no hit moved. The viewport has no `preserveDrawingBuffer`: read the canvas only right after rendering it, in the same task. [Evidence](../notes/performance-viewport.md#render-on-demand).

- **Map tiles stream by distance and draw on demand** (§12). The Earth layer updates the tiles renderer only inside `RenderLoop.beforeRender`, so tiles load and refine only when a frame draws; a tile arriving asks for one redraw (`needs-update`, `needs-render`), and nothing renders once the view and the loads settle. Screen-space error picks each tile's detail from the canvas camera, so a tile near the camera loads finer and the rest stay coarse. Draco decoding runs in `DRACOLoader`'s workers, and `3d-tiles-renderer` loads as its own chunk the first time a scene has a place.

- **No canvas reallocation during panel animations.** `CanvasSizer` keeps the canvas centred in its card, grows the drawing buffer once and shrinks it only after the size settles, and redraws synchronously after a resize so there's never a blank frame.

### Fleet globe and facility views

Fleet's browser map uses the same `WGS84_ELLIPSOID` ECEF and ENU contracts as the studio Earth layer. `fleet/browser/mapStage.ts` draws a local graticule and registered facility geometry, without using the desktop Google tile bridge. Its coordinates remain meters with Z up. The studio Earth layer still maps ENU into scene millimeters with Y up. [Fleet](fleet.md#explicit-registration-and-positions) owns registration, pose freshness, altitude, current-page bounds and browser coverage. Its geometry buffers are prepared in workers and its map renders only on changes.
