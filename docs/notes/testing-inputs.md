## Phone event stream startup

[Nightly Check 43938326448](https://github.com/JamelHammoud/basecase/actions/runs/43938326448), at `fb3bbfdf9134497770b7414be3c19858a0c522c8`, failed only the phone pairing walkthrough in Linux shard 3. The WebSocket upgrade succeeded, but the renderer remained `pairing` through its connected-state poll. The automatic retry then reported `ECONNREFUSED` against the old pairing port. These are separate attempts, not evidence of a TLS failure on the first attempt. The log also reports preview-server teardown delays, including after a passing quarantine observation. Those messages do not identify the phone failure's cause.

Classification: a timing-dependent event-subscription race. The phone service, store, browser client and walkthrough are unchanged from the preceding full green revision `6be4b9dd8d0e4cf9f0d9c7ceef80166365e36d03` in [Check 43864963744](https://github.com/JamelHammoud/basecase/actions/runs/43864963744). That run passed the phone walkthrough in 4.115 seconds on Linux and 9.212 seconds on Windows. No single intervening phone change exists to revert. The renderer creates an EventSource and independently requests pairing. Creating the EventSource does not establish its server subscription. A phone can upgrade before that subscription opens, and the bridge previously delivered only future events. The store then stays in `pairing` even though the service is connected.

The fast bridge regression forces this ordering with a real certificate-pinned TLS phone. Before the fix, its first connected-state assertion failed because the stream had no event. The bridge now subscribes and synchronously sends the current connected state before yielding, covering both delayed startup and stream reconnection. It replays no input samples or pairing credentials. The regression also checks subsequent input, Unpair Phone, closed-stream listener removal and cross-site refusal. No quarantine, retry, timeout or assertion relaxation was added.

The changed-file checks cover the deterministic ordering and existing phone bridge tests. Local browser runs and flake-rate measurements were not performed. The existing browser walkthrough retains full tilt-to-rover and post-unpair refusal coverage in CI. A passing fast regression proves state recovery for the forced ordering, not a measured browser failure rate or preview-server teardown repair.
