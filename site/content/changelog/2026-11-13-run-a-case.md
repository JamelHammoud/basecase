Run Case fills the window with the open Case, robot in the middle. Around it sits whatever that Case needs: camera feeds, live joint plots, a timer, a score or a Reset button.

It's the expand button in the Case's header in the Simulate tab, and Run Case in ⌘K. Done or Esc takes you back with the scene, the selection and the camera as they were.

A Case can carry a console that says how it runs. It names the input sources (keys and a gamepad today) and any signals of its own. It lists panels of eight kinds: camera, value, plot, table, button, list, text and a top-down map. It sets columns of fixed widths around the canvas.

Every panel reads named live signals, such as each joint's target, position and effort, the Case's probes, or the Case's own timers and scores. A block game puts Time and Score on the left and Try It, Reset and the tries on the right.

![A block game console during a try: Time counting down and Score left of the arm, and Try It, Reset and the running try on the right](/changelog/run-a-case/console.png)

A Case without a console runs with its cameras on the left. The brief, failures, servos and tries sit on the right. For now a console is written into the `.basecase` file by hand. The Mac's camera, a phone, MIDI, a leader arm, voice and policies are refused as inputs until they can drive a Case.

Steps are in [Run a Case](/help/run-a-case).
