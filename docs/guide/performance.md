# Performance

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 0f. Performance (mandatory)

- **Strength accumulates stress at shared nodes.** `strength/solve.ts` adds each computed corner stress directly to its node total and divides those totals in place. Cell and corner order, per-cell infill scaling and floating-point addition order stay unchanged. The sampler removes the 48-value Float64 corner buffer per cell, the later buffer traversal and the copied nodal result. `stressSampling.test.ts` compares exact results against corner-buffer averaging across four materials, four layer normals, dense and sparse meshes, changed displacements and shares, and unchanged inputs and earlier results. It also checks constant affine stress, changed strain matrices, empty meshes and nodes without cells. Its 300- and 600-cell fixtures bound explicitly constructed Float64 storage to 3,418 and 5,959 values, including the result and two fixed work arrays. The removed corner buffers alone held 14,400 and 28,800 values. These are allocation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete Strength solve time.

- **Feasibility shares body topology per model.** `skills/feasibilityBodies.ts` prepares subtree intervals, ordered robot members and leaves once when `openKin` compiles a model. Foot discovery no longer rebuilds membership for each possible leaf. Mass calculations, joint chains, link scaling and payload ancestry reuse the index, while positions and masses remain live. `feasibilityBodies.test.ts` compares membership with parent walks across branches, multiple roots and 40 seeded trees, checks fresh preparation after edits, and checks mass, chains and feet on three MuJoCo robots across pose and mass edits. Its 300- and 600-body chain and star fixtures read 300 and 600 parents during preparation, with no parent reads in later membership queries. One full membership scan by parent walks takes 44,850 and 179,700 reads on those chains. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete feasibility time.

- **Recorded signals resolve through indexed topic names.** `topics/topicIndex.ts` checks the complete signal suffix, then its dotted prefixes from longest to shortest through the recording's existing name map. Playback values, series, labels and topic references share this path without scanning every topic or caching resolutions by signal. Exact names, dotted names, field boundaries and first-duplicate ownership stay intact. `topicResolution.test.ts` compares the previous scan across overlapping names and checks numeric, exact-label and ragged signals through forward and backward seeks, source preservation and replacement recordings. Its 256- and 512-topic fixtures reduce topic-name reads across 40 playback queries from 20,500 and 40,980 to zero after preparation. Preparation reads each topic name once. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete playback time.

- **Camera alignment prepares masks once.** `utils/viewFit.ts` shares centroid and occupied-area measurements across every rotation and scale candidate in a scoring call. It overwrites one raster instead of allocating one per candidate. `viewFitAlignment.test.ts` compares fresh-raster scoring on 60 seeded mask pairs, including source edits, empty masks, ties and result ownership. On empty 64 × 48 and 128 × 96 masks with 27 candidates, model pixel reads fall from 86,016 and 344,064 to 3,072 and 12,288. These are operation counts for mask preparation, without timing assertions or a claim about complete camera fitting time.

- **Sketch profile tracing consumes incident lists once.** `utils/sketch/profile.ts` appends to endpoint lists without copying growing arrays and retains a cursor at each endpoint. Preview fills, profile selection and solid edits share this traversal. Branch order, reversed arcs, reference exclusion, open-chain consumption and duplicate-id behavior stay intact. Preparation ends with the call, so later edits and undo read current geometry. `profileTraversal.test.ts` checks these contracts and unchanged inputs. Its 300- and 600-loop shared-vertex fixtures reduce edge-id reads from 92,700 and 365,400 to 3,000 and 6,000. A seeded scratch comparison matched complete loops and profiles across 2,000 mixed sketches. These are operation counts for edge traversal, without timing assertions or a claim about complete Sketch rendering time.

- **Axis discovery indexes candidate connections once.** `joints/jointGraph.ts` orients the kept joint and fixed-pair graph through ordered adjacency lists and a breadth-first array cursor. It removes full connection scans per body, repeated joint searches and copied frontier arrays. Root priority, joint-before-fixed ordering, fixed bridges and first discovery through cycles stay intact. Preparation is local to each query, retaining two adjacency records per connection and one queue entry per reached body. `jointGraph.test.ts` checks those contracts, metadata, unchanged inputs and edits, plus 80 comparisons against full-edge scans. Its 300- and 600-connection chain and star fixtures bound endpoint reads to 1,200 and 2,400. The full-scan reference exceeds 90,000 and 360,000 respectively. These are operation counts for graph orientation, without timing assertions or a claim about complete axis-discovery time. Pairwise cylinder matching remains quadratic in part count.

- **Dated lists prepare timestamps once.** `utils/dateGroups.ts` parses each item's update time once for sorting and labeling in both Scenes and Chat History. It prepares the local midnight and year once per grouping call. Equal timestamps retain source order, input arrays stay unchanged, and later calls read timestamp edits and calendar changes afresh. `dateGroups.test.ts` checks exact recent cutoffs, month and year labels, timezone offsets, calendar transitions, stable ties, source preservation and unreadable timestamps. Its 300- and 600-item fixtures require exactly 300 and 600 parses and one calendar preparation each. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete list rendering time.

- **Analytic arm reach shares elbow-branch calculations.** `utils/armReach.ts` computes the elbow bend once per wrist-center trial and rejects bends outside the elbow limit before calculating shoulders. Both signs reuse one shoulder bearing and offset. Arm sizing and target-pose seeding share this path. `armReach.test.ts` checks branch order, generated tool targets, joint margins, full extension, refusals, source preservation and calculation counts. A seeded scratch comparison matched 12,169 planar results and 300 target-pose results exactly against the previous solver. On its 300- and 600-query fixtures, inverse-cosine calls fell from 13,420 and 22,610 to 6,720 and 11,326. Two-argument arctangent calls fell from 27,140 and 45,820 to 3,088 and 4,938. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete arm-sizing time.

- **Coplanar comparisons reuse sampled surface area.** `three/analysis/shapeSolid.ts` sums each immutable shape's Float32 face weights once, on its first area query. Every later collision pair reuses that total, including transformed instances of the same shape. Replacement geometry receives a new shape and total. `shapeSolid.test.ts` checks subdivision, sampling stride, discarded faces, empty shapes, source preservation and replacement geometry. Its 300- and 600-face fixtures reduce summed entries across repeated queries from 90,000 and 360,000 to 300 and 600. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete collision-check time.

- **Mirror-twin checks count clashes once.** `assembly/twinLint.ts` counts both distinct endpoints in one clash-list traversal, retaining counts only for eligible paired parts. Repeated records still count separately, self clashes count once, and pair order and the strict 10% threshold stay intact. Checks with no eligible pair read no clashes. Each call rebuilds its counts from the current input. `twinLint.test.ts` checks these contracts, geometry reports, later edits and source preservation. Its 150- and 300-pair fixtures reduce endpoint reads from 179,700 and 719,400 to at most 600 and 1,200. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete scene-check time.

- **Robot components receive ordered joints in one pass.** `utils/kinematicsRig.ts` indexes each connected component's joint ids, then distributes the depth-ordered joints into component arrays without filtering the full joint list for every robot or searching member arrays. The index stays within the call. Component order, robot keys, names, pivots and part membership stay unchanged. `kinematicsRobotGroups.test.ts` checks interleaved robots, shared bases, duplicate names, empty input, edits, undo and source preservation. Its 300- and 600-robot fixtures previously required 95,098 and 370,198 joint-id reads. The tests bound those reads below 6,000 and 12,000. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim that the whole rig builder is linear.

- **Build guide modules share geometry per planning call.** `guide/guideModules.ts` retains each reached module's bounds across height comparisons and repeat detection. Relative layouts are computed only for repeated signatures and retained across later copies. Nothing survives the call. `guideModules.test.ts` checks height and name ordering, translated copies, mirrors, missing boxes, small-group folding, empty input, edits, undo and source preservation. Its 100- and 200-module fixtures previously read part boxes 8,250 and 18,678 times. They now require at most 900 and 1,800 reads. These are operation counts for geometry lookup, without timing assertions or a claim that the whole planner is linear.

- **Retargeting prepares landmark carriers once.** `utils/retargetRobot.ts` indexes the first part owned by each link when building robot landmarks. Full-body and upper-body streams reuse their carrier parts and rest points in every target and posed-landmark query, including BVH imports and live retargeting. Preparation preserves mapped-parent rules, inferred wrist and toe ends, deepest-joint ties and source part order. A new retargeter reads edited rigs and mappings afresh. `retargetCarriers.test.ts` checks those rules, missing parts, independent preparation and unchanged carriers through moving frames. A scratch comparison matched 120 complete frame results across full-body, upper-body, in-place and inferred-wrist streams. On the 29-joint fixture, 30 full-body frames reduce ownership-map traversals from 390 to zero, and upper-body frames from 210 to zero. These are traversal counts, without a timing assertion or a claim about total retargeting speed.

- **Battery lead probes request only the nearest hit.** `assembly/fitProbes.ts` uses `raycastFirst` for every obstacle along both pack-end rays, bounded by the nearest distance found so far and the existing 200 mm limit. It no longer collects, maps, sorts or deduplicates all ray hits to read the first. Insert-wall probes retain their crossing lists. `fitProbes.test.ts` compares against all-hit distances for misses, starts inside solids, axis ties, rotated, translated, scaled and mirrored obstacles, boundary clearances, multiple packs and later placement edits. The 300- and 600-layer fixtures previously collected 1,200 and 2,400 hit records from the layered solid. Each fixture now uses four bounded nearest-hit queries across its two obstacles and no all-hit query. These are query and hit counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete check time.

- **Planar hulls share one traversal.** `utils/convexHull.ts` serves Build support polygons, print contact outlines, skill support checks, camera silhouettes and try filmstrips. It sorts one input copy and appends both hull halves to one output array. The upper pass walks the sorted points backwards, without a reversed copy, separate half arrays or sliced halves. Empty, singleton, repeated and collinear inputs retain their caller behavior. `convexHull.test.ts` checks outline order, enclosure, source preservation and degenerate inputs. `supportMargin.test.ts` checks signed distances and the single-contact refusal.

- **Onshape fixed-mate attachment indexes links once.** `onshape/fixedMates.ts` groups note names before attaching them to imported parts. Import and Update from Onshape share this path. Matching stays case-sensitive, note order and duplicates stay intact, and repeated links receive independent arrays. No index survives the call. `fixedMates.test.ts` checks these contracts, source preservation, existing notes and changed source data. Its 300- and 600-part fixtures bound mate-link reads to 600 and 1,200, down from 90,000 and 360,000 in the previous nested scans. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about complete import time.

- **Head gestures share prepared angles.** `cameras/gestures/gestureFrame.ts` computes head pitch and yaw once per accepted frame. Nod and Shake Head read those values throughout their rolling window, removing repeated trigonometry, point tuples and angle records from both detectors. The window retains one angle record per frame. `gestureHead.test.ts` compares both detectors against the previous calculation across nods, head shakes, turned-body waves and walking, checks nonfinite head observations and rejected shoulders, and preserves input landmarks. Its 300- and 600-frame fixtures reduce angle calculations from 49,836 and 104,968 to 600 and 1,200. These are operation counts, without a timing assertion or a claim about total recognition speed.

- **Strength voxel traversals reuse neighbor storage.** `strength/voxelNeighbours.ts` captures grid dimensions and reuses one array per operation across surface detection, depth propagation, component labeling and Lighten's mounting paths. Depth and component traversals test eligibility directly, without filtered neighbor arrays. `voxelTraversal.test.ts` checks boundaries, singleton axes, cavities, disconnected solids, edited grids, equal-cost paths, density-ranked detours and unchanged inputs. Its 300- and 600-cell fixtures require one neighbor buffer per operation and respectively 1,199 and 2,399 total queries across depth, labeling and paths, including the mounting search stopping before expanding its final target. These traversals previously created 1,800 and 3,600 neighbor and filtered arrays, and now create three neighbor buffers. This counts those arrays only, excluding frontiers, stacks and path-queue entries, without a timing assertion or a claim about total solve time.

- **Lighten stops searching once mounting paths are final.** `strength/lightenPaths.ts` counts frozen cells and stops Dijkstra traversal after settling the last one, before expanding its neighbors. Stale queue entries cannot complete a target. Single-cell mounts expand nothing, and disconnected mounts still exhaust the reachable solid before refusal. `lightenPaths.test.ts` compares paths with exhaustive simple-path enumeration across 24 weighted, tied and perforated fixtures. It also checks density edits, duplicate regions, disconnected mounts and unchanged inputs. On 300- and 600-cell bars with mounts in their first three cells, neighbor queries fall from 300 and 600 to two in each case. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about total Lighten time.

- **Ground planning reuses obstacle footprints.** `cases/groundObstacles.ts` prepares each reached obstacle and clearance level once per query. Ground routes share them across waypoints and recursive detours. Room and imported-world start searches share full-clearance footprints across candidate positions. Unvisited obstacles and unused clearance levels create no rectangles. Each planning call owns its query, so edits and clearance changes read fresh geometry. `groundObstacles.test.ts` checks strict boundaries, rotation and parallel thresholds, all four clearance levels, obstacle order, duplicate ids, source preservation, edits and empty or push-only routes. Both 10- and 20-waypoint fixtures prepare one footprint. A seeded scratch comparison matched 300 complete routes, 6,000 occupancy queries and 30 room starts against the previous implementation. A 20-waypoint detour fixture reduced footprint calculations from 320 to one. A blocked room-center search reduced them from 147 to one. These are operation counts, without timing assertions.

- **Body fitting prepares residual windows once.** `reality/bodyResiduals.ts` snapshots selected frame indices and measured joint positions for the fit and check halves. Friction search, solver probes and both held-out scores share that preparation. Each evaluation allocates one result buffer and no per-frame arrays. Servo fitting already prepares its own log windows in `fitServo.ts`. `bodyResiduals.test.ts` checks elapsed-time boundaries, joint order, independent results, changed recordings and 180 replay comparisons. Its 300- and 600-frame fixtures require 301 and 601 timestamp reads, plus two measured-position reads per frame after the initial frame. Twenty later probes of each half read neither timestamps nor recorded positions. Existing body identification tests check payload, mass and floor-friction recovery through the real MuJoCo pipeline. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about total fit time.

- **Policy playback retains actuator addresses.** `sim/engine.ts` resolves each policy output's actuator when its controller starts, alongside its observation addresses. Direct policies and policy-driven routes reuse these addresses on every physics substep. Controller replacement, reset and respawn rebuild the bindings. Passive joints still receive no policy writes, and outputs retain policy order even when model joints are reordered or actuator indices differ. `policyCommand.test.ts` compares controls and poses against equivalent held targets for both controller kinds, checks replacement and missing joints, and bounds joint-id reads across 50 substeps. The 12-joint quadruped fixture falls from 4,500 reads to 600, and the 18-joint hexapod from 9,450 to 900. The remaining reads belong to the ordinary hold pass. These are operation counts, without timing assertions or a claim about total simulation speed.

- **Triangle crossings reuse query storage.** `three/analysis/crossing.ts` prepares two triangle-plane workspaces per BVH query. Corner references, plane distances and line projections are overwritten for each candidate pair. Span bounds and the first crossing edge stay in those workspaces, while returned points and directions remain owned by each result. Projection starts only after both triangles pass the plane tolerance. Each fully crossing pair needs seven dot products, down from thirteen, and no temporary corner, side or span arrays per pair. `crossing.test.ts` checks vertex order, winding, triangle order, tolerance boundaries, result ownership, gathering limits, stride order, moved meshes and source preservation. Its 300- and 600-pair fixtures require 2,100 and 4,200 dot products. These are operation counts, without timing assertions.

- **Elastic solves reuse connectivity.** `strength/elasticPattern.ts` prepares sparse rows, columns and element block destinations once for a mesh. Lighten shares this pattern across load cases and its 14 updates. Strength prepares it for one solve. Numerical stiffness values, pinning, preconditioning and displacement workspaces remain private to each solve. The destination table costs 256 bytes per element, up to 10.24 MB at Lighten's cell limit. `elasticPattern.test.ts` compares assembly against independent dense matrices, checks known displacements with changed supports and densities, and verifies that repeated solves preserve the pattern and previous matrix values. The existing multi-load optimization test checks one preparation for the complete operation. A scratch comparison matched complete matrices and solver results exactly in 24 cases across dense and sparse meshes, four materials and three density scales, plus complete Lighten results for one load and two mixed-material loads. On shared macOS hardware with Node 26.13.0, a batch of 28 assemblies of a 300-cell mesh took a median of 61.35 ms before and 15.24 ms after, including preparation, over nine alternating measurements after three warmups. The retained destination table used 76,800 bytes. This measures assembly alone, without a CI timing assertion.

- **Motion fitting prepares shared channels once.** `utils/motionSkeletonSource.ts` unwraps angles and calculates spans once per visited bone, sharing read-only values across its endpoint chains. The cache ends with the call, and each endpoint retains its own joint records, axis order and static-channel orientation. File import and the agent share this preparation. `motionSkeletonSource.test.ts` checks branching chains, seam crossings, static rotations, limb selection, source preservation and changed samples across calls. The 12- and 24-endpoint fixtures each require nine shared-root rotation lookups. A scratch comparison matched the previous complete chain output exactly in 63 cases across eight BVH fixtures, three scales and endpoint selections. On the nine-frame shared-torso fixture, torso rotation lookups fell from 20 to 10. These are operation counts, without timing assertions.

- **Cable-clearance queries reuse their interval.** `wiring/routeCollision.ts` clips one query-local accumulator against the six box planes, replacing up to six returned interval arrays with one. Connector exits, anchors, detour corners and visibility edges share this path across rest and sampled motion. `routeCollision.test.ts` checks all axes, parallel epsilon thresholds, grazing contact, disjoint axis intervals, growing and shrinking envelopes, nested queries and unchanged inputs. Existing routing tests cover rotated housings, thin obstructions, coupled joints and deterministic detours. A seeded scratch comparison matched the previous result on 50,049 queries, including rotated boxes and displacement envelopes. Those queries created 193,734 interval arrays before and 50,049 after. These are source-level allocation counts, without a claim about garbage collection or end-to-end routing time.

- **Physics latency reuses control snapshots.** `sim/controlDelay.ts` shares one `mj_step` wrapper between live physics settings and Arena. A delay of N steps retains at most N + 1 control arrays, then overwrites them in place without shifting history. Startup holds the first command, and resetting the delay clears its history. The separate per-actuator Laggy fault already reuses its scalar ring. `controlDelay.test.ts` checks every channel through startup, repeated wraps, zero delay, resets and changed control widths. A scratch comparison matched the former controls across 140 runs and 84,000 steps. At eight controls and 20 steps of latency, 1,200 steps allocated 21 snapshots instead of 1,200. `controlDelayWorlds.test.ts` checks 450 real WASM steps in each of the live and Arena paths, including timestep rounding and repeated resets. These are allocation counts, without timing assertions.

- **CAD crease filtering indexes only requested segments.** `three/partMesh/creaseEdges.ts` seeds adjacency from supplied B-rep segments and computes normals only for triangles touching those segments. The worker and fallback build share this path, so unrelated triangulation edges never enter the adjacency map. Each referenced vertex's coordinate key is calculated once per call and reused across adjacent triangles. Keys stay local to the call, so mesh edits cannot reuse stale coordinates. `creaseEdges.test.ts` checks threshold boundaries, welded vertices, source order, duplicates, exceptional topology, packed worker results and fallback mesh lines. Fixtures with 300 and 600 unrelated faces each calculate only two adjacent normals. Indexed grids with 512 and 2,048 triangles read coordinates once per vertex plus 18 reads for the two adjacent normals. Tests also check that changed positions are read afresh on each call. A scratch comparison matched the former output in 120 cases across transformed boxes, spheres and tori with ordinary and packed inputs. A 5,000-triangle plate with three requested segments reduced normal calculations from 5,000 to three. These are operation counts, without CI timing assertions.

- **Nearest joint-face picks reuse a query workspace.** `joints/meshIndex.ts` uses three.js's closest-point routine with one triangle and two query vectors per scan, as Sketch face picking already does. This removes the duplicate closest-point implementation and per-candidate tuple arrays. Welding, the area threshold and first-face tie order stay with `MeshIndex`. `meshIndex.test.ts` covers all seven closest-point regions, ties, degenerate and incomplete faces, welded coordinates, repeated queries and workspace reuse across 300 and 600 faces. A seeded scratch comparison matched 1,000 picks on a 5,000-triangle mesh. On macOS with Node 26.13.0, 20 picks took a median of 11.45 ms before and 5.24 ms after, over nine alternating measurements after three warmups. The former scan allocated 15,000 vertex tuples per pick, plus intermediate arrays. This measures nearest-face selection alone, without a CI timing assertion.

- **Print orientations share triangle calculations.** `three/printability.ts` scores all candidate poses in one triangle pass, reusing its normal, area and centroid across poses. Temporary storage scales with candidate count, never triangle count. Print reports, merge planning, manual faces and fit coupons use this scorer. `analysis/shapeReports.ts` also batches uncached requested faces, preserving rounded cache keys, first matches and request order. `printOrientation.test.ts` checks box contact areas, support hits and misses, angle and contact limits, tilted faces, degenerate triangles, source preservation, cache reuse and coordinate-read bounds for both batch paths. A scratch comparison matched complete reports on 30 transformed meshes and 90 manual-face requests exactly against the previous scorer. With ten candidates, 300- and 1,200-triangle fixtures reduced coordinate reads from 99,900 to 75,600 and from 399,600 to 302,400. Vector cross products fell from 7,602 to 4,902 and from 18,410 to 7,610, including BVH work. These are operation counts, without timing assertions.

- **Dense solvers scan matrix tails directly.** `utils/matrixScans.ts` selects pivot rows and accumulates solved terms without copying array tails. Calibration least squares, skill IK and stance weight sharing use it. - **Cable detours share local point coordinates.** `wiring/routeCollision.ts` prepares each queried point and obstacle pair once during a detour search. Visibility edges and later anchored detours reuse its local coordinates and displacement bound. Direct segments build no point cache. Each route call owns its cache, including each sampled pose, so edits and undo prepare fresh values. `routeClearance.test.ts` checks oriented obstacles, duplicate obstacle ids, growing and shrinking envelopes, epsilon boundaries, lazy preparation, anchored detours, edits and source preservation. - **Cable routing uses the analysis workers** (§8b). `viewport.wiring(scene)` waits for component meshes and refuses measurement if any model fails to load, so placeholders cannot certify cable clearance. After loading it checks that the requested parts are still on the canvas, completes their pending builds and snapshots the solid parts in their rest positions, then `wiringIn` sends the scene with the existing cached shape payload. The rest instance keys and matrices, parts and relevant project values, including scene-carried sourced records, key a shared pending job or completed result. The worker registers those sourced records before reading connector ports. The worker derives world bounds and fastener chains from the placed geometry, assigns fastener followers to joints and returns the measured netlist. Engineering and Build Along share this API, including the same fastener motion. Engineering requests routing even when joint collision sweeps are disabled, and its synchronous report only consumes the returned routes.

- **Captured-world camera rendering stays in workers** (§8l). Camera tiles, frame streams, agent shots and dataset videos reuse the canvas's Spark/mesh world layer inside their existing OffscreenCanvas stages. The imported asset is sent on scene changes, never with pose frames. Spark updates finish before a capture. An unchanged camera, world transform and image size reuse the prepared splats, so robot motion alone never regenerates a fixed view's splat buffers. One feed draw is in flight at a time. On demand, `node tools/perf/worldVideosBench.mjs` measures rendering and encoding a 10 s, 30 fps, two-camera kitchen episode against Open Floor, including stage creation. It prints both times and fails above a 3× ratio. Timing never runs in Check.
- **World-render acceptance excludes live input runtimes.** Case validation and console defaults read `sim/inputRegistry.ts`, rather than loading drive handlers and their inference workers. `worldImportRuntime.test.ts` guards the import boundary. - **Training images stream in workers** (§8h, §8l). `trainingImages.worker.ts` owns scene variation, JPEG encoding, mask extraction, polygon tracing and frame hashes. Generation transfers one JPEG and depth buffer at a time and writes them before requesting another frame. The main thread retains annotation manifests, not a batch of depth images. Frame-set verification hashes one frame at a time to bound temporary copies of JPEG and depth buffers.
- **Training image acceptance budget** (`node tools/perf/trainingImagesBench.mjs`): on demand, 1,000 labelled 640 × 480 frames in under 120 seconds after one warmup capture. The first 500 frames compare every visible block box against independent projected corner bounds within one pixel. The command accepts `--cpu`, `--dist <dir>` and `--no-build`. The 120-second threshold is a contract, not a measured baseline. It includes worker transfers and JPEG encoding but excludes dataset persistence and archive export. Timing never runs in Check. `e2e/trainingImageBounds.e2e.test.ts` checks five seeded placements without a timing assertion. Other camera acceptance tests cover depth within 5 mm, fisheye projection, occlusion and byte-identical seeded captures.

Captured world parsing and V-HACD collision decomposition run in the world import worker. At most 64 hulls with 96 vertices each reach MuJoCo. The scene stores one content-addressed asset and each Case references its hash, so adding a Case does not copy its splat or mesh. Case workers receive collider items only. Spark sorts splats in its bundled worker and requests redraws as work completes through the existing on-demand render loop. `e2e/worldImport.e2e.test.ts` covers attachment of the rendered world.

Saved world assets restore in `worldAssetRestore.worker.ts`: base64 decoding, buffer validation, canonical serialization, hashing and texture or splat payload validation all finish before the page registers any asset. The worker transfers decoded binary buffers and returns cached canonical JSON for autosave, then terminates. An unavailable browser worker refuses the restore. The standard worker client runs the same operation locally only outside the browser for Node and tests. Hashing and serialization live separately from the registry, so neither world worker imports the worker client.

`node tools/perf/worldBench.mjs --dist dist --scene scratch/rover.json` imports a synthetic 1,000,000-point kitchen splat into a saved rover scene, waits for all points to enter Spark's active set, warms one orbit and measures a second four-second orbit at 1440 × 900. It uses the viewport benchmark's WebGL probes and Chrome's GPU-process trace, records CPU work p95, frame interval p95 and average GPU-process work per rendered frame, and fails when CPU work p95 or GPU-process work exceeds 16 ms. `--cpu` selects SwiftShader, `--budget` changes that explicit budget, and `--output` chooses the JSON report (default `scratch/perf/world.json`). No GPU measurement has been recorded for this change. GPU-process work measures Chrome's submission work rather than hardware execution time. The decomposition test’s 200,000-triangle worker budget is 10 seconds. Run `src/cases/worldDecomposition.perf.test.ts` with `vitest.perf.config.ts` for that measurement. It transfers a tessellated kitchen into a bundled worker and checks the gap under its table. The initial direct 200,000-triangle box measurement took 17.95 seconds before boundary-preserving collision simplification was added. This is a local measurement, not a CI result.

The app must feel instant. These rules came out of profiling real scenes (60+ CAD parts, 50+ MB saved files); don't regress them.

- **Containment stops when its ray votes settle.** Collision analysis and assembly probes share `three/analysis/collide.ts`'s containment query. The CAD kernel's `MeshQuery.inside` applies the same early decision to its own directions and crossing rules. Two agreeing rays settle a three-ray query, reducing three BVH traversals to two. Disagreement still casts the third ray. Renderer queries count forward hits without allocating a filtered hit array, retaining the strict 0.0001 mm cutoff. Kernel queries retain sorted, deduplicated crossings. `containment.test.ts` and `meshQuery.test.mjs` check every three-ray vote sequence, real solid and hollow meshes, boundary points and moved assembly frames. Custom renderer direction lists retain the two-vote threshold. A local before-and-after probe of 300 interior box points reduced BVH traversals from 900 to 600 in each of collision analysis, assembly probes and the kernel, with 300 inside results in both versions. These are operation counts, without timing assertions.

- **Study snapshots are immutable** (§8b). Each run owns a worker session that retains immutable geometry by handle and receives changed buffers once. Geometry refresh returns only rebuilt triangle buffers. The session closes after completion or cancellation. Variant geometry, Checks, simulation models and physics run in workers. Named dimensions compile through the existing sandboxed kernel pool, with only numeric literal edits on the captured source. The canvas previews a copied design without changing scene history. Autosave serializes the Study's captured baseline once per baseline object, reusing the scene's cached part JSON (`utils/sceneJson.ts`). Later saves reuse that string and each immutable result row's cached JSON, so completed Case paths and Checks are not serialized again. `sceneDraft.ts` detects a changed Study by identity before comparing the remaining project settings, so receiving a result never stringifies its baseline mesh to check whether the scene changed.

- **Keep views alive.** Panels that switch (sidebar views, inspector tabs) mount on first use and then stay in React's `<Activity>`, so switching back is instant and hidden views run no effects.

- **A Case's code panels cost only what they draw** (§8h). The studio adds no render loop for them: a frame runs its own, out of the studio's renderer process. The bridge sends a frame only the signals it subscribed to, at most 20 times a second (a trailing timer, so the last value always arrives), never the whole bus, and the heartbeat is one small message every 500 ms.
- **Small payloads across IPC and HTTP.** Lists are summaries (`SavedSceneSummary`: counts, size, a small WebP preview); full records load on demand (`platformBridge.getScene`). Library JSON is written compact. Never send a whole library over IPC. Saving, renaming, duplicating or deleting a Library model, a scene in the Scenes menu or a chat changes the list on screen at once, then writes that one record (`withModel`, `sceneStore`); the list is read again only when the write fails. A scene's writes run in order, and opening it waits for them (`queueSceneWrite`). The desktop models-folder watcher sends only the record that changed, and skips the app's own writes (`ModelChanges`).

- **React Compiler friendliness** (§0c) is a performance rule: one rule break makes the compiler skip the whole component, and everything under it re-renders on every change. One that isn't a Rules of React break: the compiler can't lower `try` without `catch`, so a handler in a component that resets a flag after an awaited call chains `.finally(...)` instead (`agent/ConnectionBanner.tsx`).
- **Boot never waits on anything it doesn't need to draw.** The window opens before the agent connects (`connectAcpAgent` runs in parallel; it used to delay the window 0.7–2 s, and status reads `connecting` meanwhile, so nothing says “disconnected”). Only the active tab mounts at first; background tabs mount one per idle callback after its first frame (`useMountedTabs`), since each builds its own WebGL context, environment map and shaders (about 60–250 ms apiece). Events for a tab that hasn't mounted are dropped, not refused: on mount it adopts its runs from the journal and the bridge replays unanswered requests.
