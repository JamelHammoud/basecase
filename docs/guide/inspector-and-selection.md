# Inspector, selection, drag and drop, mode panels

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 7. Inspector (Figma UI3 style)

- Sections are separated by full-width hairline dividers, never boxed cards. Section titles are sentence case and 13px semibold.
- **Notes** (`NotesSection`) is one section for the scene and for a part: a text area with no placeholder, collapsed while empty and open when it has text. `Section` reads `defaultOpen` only on mount, so `InspectTab` keys the Inspector by `sceneId` and `PartInspector` keys Notes by part id: loading a scene into the open tab or picking another part opens Notes again when they hold text.
- Every Inspect view and mode panel scrolls with `useScrollFade`, like Parts: an edge fades only while there's more to scroll past it.
- **No selection:** Notes for the scene (saved with it to `ai-reference/` and matched by Search scenes; Copy as Markdown puts it under Design Intent and leaves that line out when it's empty) and Comments (just the title and + when there are none). Scene stats live in Layers: the header shows parts and cutouts, and a footer shows the overall size.
- **Part selected:**
  - Inline-renamable name, then Duplicate / Save to Library / Delete.
  - Hardware shows the same `HardwareConfigurator` sections as Parts (type tiles, drive, size, length, finish) in place of Size and Shape. Specs and Holes come before Notes, collapsed; Holes carries + (Add clearance hole) in its header. Length typing and scrubbing are live; release commits one undo step. The color follows the finish unless it was set by hand.
  - No Solid · Cutout · Guide control: the part type is set from the right-click menu (one part or several).
  - Size W/H/D (+ wall T), Position X/Y/Z (Floor, Stack and Center actions), Rotation X/Y/Z with a strip of 90° turns (`QuarterTurns`, the same strip as a multi-selection's).
  - **Shape** (Fillet or Chamfer and its size, Taper, Bend, Twist): collapsible, open only when non-zero; Reset shape clears all of them.
    - **Edges** (`EdgesField`, no label of its own) shows on a box, cube, cylinder, tube, hex prism or wedge (`EDGE_KINDS` in `utils/partEdges.ts`): **Fillet** or **Chamfer**, then a size in mm, stored as `edges: { kind, mm }`. The size is clamped to 98% of the largest the shape allows: half its smallest side, half the tube wall, or the hex's or wedge's inradius (`edgeLimitMm`). The shape keeps its size: the edges round or bevel inward.
    - The builders make exact geometry, with no vertex deformer (`partMesh/edgedGeometry.ts`). A box, hex prism or wedge is its outline inset by the size and grown back by a sphere (fillet) or a double pyramid (chamfer), in layers, so every edge and corner is cut. A cylinder or tube is a lathed profile with its rims rounded or beveled; an oval cylinder is a round one scaled. Arcs step at most 11.25°, so a 3 mm fillet on a 40 × 20 × 10 mm box is within 0.2% of its true volume. Results are cached by their parameters and cost about 1 ms uncached. Taper, Bend and Twist still apply on top.
    - Everything that reads the built mesh follows: STL, 3MF and plates, printability, mass, booleans (an edged operand stays editable) and the MJCF hull. STEP falls back to the mesh for an edged part, and a world item with edges uses its hull instead of an MJCF box or cylinder.
    - **Round** (`deformers.roundness`, a 0–100% pull toward an ellipsoid) shows only on a part that already has it, until it's set to 0, so old scenes render as before.
  - **Dimensions** (agent-made OpenSCAD parts, after Size; hidden in the web demo): one row per top-level numeric assignment the code makes once (`wall = 2;`), named as the code names it, with min, step and max from a Customizer comment on the same line (`// [1.2:0.4:4]`, `// [min:max]` or `// [max]`, which starts at 0). Size still scales the mesh; Dimensions change the design. Enter or blur sends the part id and the new number over `cad:tune` (`useCadTune.ts`); the main process rewrites that one literal in the stored code and compiles it in the kernel pool (§9, §0h). The result replaces the part through `applySpec`, keeping the code origin where it was, and `followMates` moves what's mated to it, all as one undo step (`withTunedPart` in `agent/cadTune.ts`). A compile error, or a result that fails any of the part's `cad.expect` requirements, keeps the old part and shows the reason in an error toast. Edits to one part run in order; a result whose part changed meanwhile (the agent rewrote it) is dropped. replicad parts get no Dimensions: once `mirror` or `fasten` wraps their code in a function, their constants aren't at the top level any more.
  - Collapsible sections keep their title where a plain section's sits and put the chevron at the far right, after any header actions.
  - Appearance (`ColorField` + material), comments on this part, Notes (sent to the agent), then Product (maker · part number, specs, mounting, model source, links) for real components.
- **Several parts selected (`MultiInspector`), like a multi-selection in Figma:**
  - Title: the group name (renamed in place, one undo step on Enter or blur), or “N parts”. Under it, the part count and the selection's overall size (`39 × 50 × 39 mm`). Group/Ungroup, Duplicate, Mirror (a menu of Side, Front and Top, below), Save to Library and Delete sit beside it.
  - **Align:** one icon strip per axis (min · center · max) with Spread beside it (`AlignBar`; Spread shows from 3 parts).
  - **Position:** X/Y/Z of the selection's bounds center. Typing, scrubbing and math move every unlocked part together (`handleMoveSelection` takes an absolute target computed from the latest parts, so Enter + blur can't move it twice). Drop to floor and Center on origin in the header.
  - **Rotation:** the single part's `QuarterTurns` strip; X / Y / Z 90° turns the selection as one piece about its center (`handleRotateSelection`).
  - **Combine:** Union / Subtract / Intersect on one strip; Subtract's tooltip names the part it keeps.
  - **Appearance:** `ColorField` and material, each showing “Mixed” when the parts differ.
  - Strips (`StripGroup` + `StripButton` in `AlignBar.tsx`) are the pattern for a row of equal actions: a 32px soft track with 2px inset cells, never a grid of text buttons or tall tiles.
- **`NumberInput`:**
  - Drag the label to scrub (Shift ×10, ⌥ ×0.1). ↑/↓ steps, Enter commits, Esc reverts.
  - Hovering a one-letter label shows what it stands for (W: Width (X)). A field with its name beside it (Wall thickness, Copies, Infill) has no tooltip.
  - Accepts math (`40/2`).
- **Live vs. committed edits:** scrubbing, slider drags and typing call `onChange(value, live=true)`. Those skip undo history until release or blur, which commits one undo step (`commitParts(updater, live)` in `App.tsx`).

## 7a. Selection, Groups & Booleans

- **Selection is an ordered list** (`selectedIds` in `App.tsx`). The first id is the primary, and Subtract keeps that part.
- **Canvas:**
  - Click a part to select it, or its whole group. Clicking again keeps the group selected.
  - Double-click or ⌘-click a grouped part to drill into just that part, or a boolean to drill into the operand under the pointer. Once inside a group (some but not all of its members selected), a single click selects another member.
  - Shift-click works like Figma (`clickSelection` in `workspace/selection.ts`): it toggles what a plain click would pick. From outside a group it adds or removes the whole group; inside one it adds or removes just that member, so members of one group and other groups mix freely. ⇧⌘-click toggles exactly the part under the pointer. Clicking empty space clears the selection.
  - Shift-drag draws a blue selection box and adds every unlocked, visible part it touches, plus their groups. Hit testing uses each part’s real silhouette (projected vertices + a 7×7 ray grid), not its bounding box. It runs in the capture phase and turns OrbitControls off for that drag, so Shift-drag never pans. Shift-scroll still pans.
  - Only primary clicks change selection; right-click never does.
  - Locked parts ignore clicks, like Figma: a click or double-click passes through to the part behind (`raycastUnlocked` in `three/viewport/picking.ts`), and right-click opens the canvas menu, not the part's. ⌘-click (and ⇧⌘-click) still selects a locked part, as does its row in Layers. Measure, Annotate and Mark still land on them.
- **Layers:**
  - A plain click selects that row; Shift/⌘-click toggles exactly that row (a part row never expands to its group; a group row toggles all its members).
  - Group rows are collapsible, double-click to rename, and select every member.
- **Mirror** (right-click ▸ Mirror ▸ Side / Front / Top, ⌘K Mirror, the multi-selection Inspector) copies the selection across one of the three scene planes, named after the views. The plane passes through the selection's centre, except Side for parts of a robot that leave its body out: that one passes through the centre of the robot's body (the parts no joint moves), or x = 0 when the parts are framed to a skeleton. The last plane picked is remembered until the app quits (`mirror/plane.ts`), and ⌘K uses it. The copies are selected, named after their source with left and right, front and rear or top and bottom swapped (else “⟨name⟩ (Mirror)”), put in a new group the same way, and keep `mirrorOf`. Parts and twin joints land as one undo step. The maths is `src/mirror/`, shared with the agent's `mirror_parts` (§9):
  - Basic shapes are placed as the same shape, turned about a local axis they're symmetric about, so a wedge, cone, pyramid or dome lands as its true mirror image; a twist turns the other way. Catalog parts are the same part, placed by datum (`mirrorDatum.ts`), never a flipped mesh. CAD, mesh, sculpted and custom-model parts mirror their geometry, with the winding reversed; a boolean mirrors its operands and recomputes.
  - Mates follow to the port that lands where the mirrored port would (within 1 mm), on the copy or on an unmirrored target; a mate with no such port is dropped.
  - A joint whose children are all mirrored gets a twin: parents and actuators map to their copies (an unmirrored parent, like the body, stays), the origin is reflected, and the range is kept. A hinge's axis is reflected and reversed, so the same angle moves the twin the mirror way; a slider's axis is only reflected.
  - Framed parts (§9) land on the paired skeleton joint (`alignToSkeleton`) when the plane is the skeleton's; on any other plane they lose their frame.
- **Moving several parts:** the gizmo attaches to a pivot at the selection’s center. Move, rotate and scale apply to every unlocked part and commit as one undo step.
- **Groups:** stored on each part as `group: { id, name }`, so they’re undoable and saved with the scene. Groups are flat (no nesting). ⌘G groups, ⇧⌘G ungroups.
- **Booleans** (`src/booleans/`) stay editable:
  - With 2+ parts, Union, Subtract (from the primary) and Intersect replace them with one `boolean` part. With one part, Subtract uses every visible Cutout that overlaps it, and makes the same kind of part.
  - The result keeps the base part's id and name (joints, marks and comments on it stay attached). `boolean: { op, operands }` holds the parts it was made from, base first, each a whole `ScenePart` in world coordinates with its own id; the base takes a fresh id. They're not in the top-level parts list, so Checks, the BOM, printing, plates, STL and the MJCF see only the result.
  - The result's mesh is derived: it lives in `mesh` like an imported mesh (drawn by the same builder, measured the same way) with `boolean.hash`, the operands' hash it was cut from (`hash.ts`), and `boolean.frame`, the result's position, rotation and size at that moment. Scenes save it, so loading never cuts again; a part whose hash no longer matches is cut again on load.
  - The cut runs in the geometry worker (`csg.ts`, op `boolean`) with `manifold-3d`; when Manifold refuses an operand's mesh (open component pieces), the whole cut falls back to three-bvh-csg in the same worker. The main thread never runs CSG. Results are cached by hash (`evaluate.ts`, last 64).
  - `commitParts` lifts every operand into the list the updater sees and folds the edits back (`editWithOperands` in `operands.ts`), so every edit by id (gizmo, Inspector, agent ops, ⌫) reaches an operand. Deleting the base, or all tools but one, dissolves the boolean into what's left. Moving, turning or resizing the result carries its operands with it (`syncFrames` in `frames.ts`); a move alone keeps the hash, since operand positions are hashed relative to the base.
  - After a commit, `useBooleanResults` cuts every stale boolean (innermost first) and writes the result into the present and every undo and redo state whose hash matches (`patchEveryState`), so an operand edit is one undo step and undo shows the old cut at once. An edit that empties the result leaves an empty mesh until the next edit; making a boolean from parts that don't overlap changes nothing and says “Those parts don’t overlap”.
  - Drilling in works like a group (`drillTarget` in `picking.ts`): double-click or ⌘-click the result to select the operand whose surface is under the pointer (the closest of each operand's hits to the result's hit); once inside, a plain click picks another operand. A selected operand draws as a ghost over the result (cutouts in their own red, solids in translucent blue, `drilledOperands`), which the gizmo moves like any part. Layers lists operands under the result (§5).
  - Duplicate and paste give operands fresh ids (`freshOperandIds`). STEP export rebuilds a boolean exactly from its operands when every one can be built exactly (`exactSolid`), and from its mesh otherwise. Old scenes keep their `sculpt_mesh` booleans, which render and export as before (one saved with a `csg` recipe still exports exactly while it keeps the size it was made at).
- **Wrap Robot:** with exactly one visible imported mesh (`mesh.file`) and parts of exactly one jointed robot selected (`wrapTarget`), the several-parts menu starts with Wrap Robot, and ⌘K lists it under Selection. It turns the mesh into one hollow shell per link that clears every joint's range (§8c, `src/wrap/`): a progress toast shows while the wrap worker runs, the canvas stays live, and the shells come back selected, with the mesh hidden. One undo restores the mesh, the robot's joints and its parts. The fit is a starting point: resize a shell with Size like any part.
- **Align (`AlignBar`):**
  - One icon strip per axis: X Left·Center·Right, Y Bottom·Middle·Top, Z Back·Center·Front, with Spread beside each once 3 or more parts are selected (below that the strip takes the row). The right-click Align submenu and the multi-selection Inspector show the same strip; a click in the submenu closes the menu.
  - Several parts align to their shared bounds; one part aligns to the origin (Bottom = floor).
  - Bounds include each part's rotation (`halfExtents` in `utils/selection.ts`, checked against three.js `Box3`), so align, spread, drop to floor and the sizes in Layers and the Inspector stay right for rotated parts.
  - One table names every action, `ALIGN_AXES` in `components/alignActions.ts`: the strip's tooltips and ⌘K read their labels from it, so both say Align Left … Align Front, Align Center (X), Align Middle, Align Center (Z) and Spread Along X, Y and Z, with each action's own icon. With one part the tooltips say where it goes instead: Left to origin, Bottom to floor.
- **Joining two parts:** with exactly two parts selected, Add Hinge and Add Slider (right-click or ⌘K) make the first part the parent and the second the child of a new joint. The axis comes from a shaft in a bore shared by the two parts, or from a round face you click on either one; the selection order is what decides which part moves (§8c).
- **Drop to Floor (F):** loose parts each drop to the floor; when every selected part is grouped, the selection lands as one piece.

## 7a-2. Drag and Drop

- **Draggable onto the canvas:** Parts tiles, Shapes menu tiles, Library model tiles, Robots tiles and SimReady tiles (Objects and Robots). They use `PART_DRAG_TYPE` / `MODEL_DRAG_TYPE` / `ROBOT_DRAG_TYPE` (`{ url, name }`, imported on drop) / `SIMREADY_DRAG_TYPE` (`{ url, name, maker, license }`) from `src/data/dnd.ts`, and the tile’s preview is the drag image.
- **Where it lands:** at the drop point, snapped to the grid step. Dropping on a part stacks it on that surface; otherwise it sits on the floor.
- **Snapping by ports** (§12): a Parts tile, or a Library model that inserts as one part, snaps onto a compatible port within reach of the drop point, with the same dots as a gizmo drag and the mate recorded. The browser hides drag data until the drop, so tiles start their drag with `setSceneDrag` (`data/dnd.ts`), which also keeps the payload for `dragover`; `workspace/dropPreview.ts` builds the part that would land and `previewSnap` on the viewport handle solves and draws it. ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows) drops without snapping. Articulated parts (grippers, hands) and multi-part Library models land as before.
- **Shapes menu:** fades out (stays mounted) while dragging, then closes on drag end. Removing the drag source would cancel the drag.
- **Import** (File ▸ Import, ⌘K, the canvas menu at the clicked point, and Library ▸ New Model) opens one file picker for every type below, and files dropped on the canvas take the same route at the drop point: `routeImports` (`workspace/importRoute.ts`, pure) sorts files by extension, case-insensitively (a file with no known extension but an `image/*` type other than SVG counts as an image, the same test the drop overlay uses for “Drop to add a reference image”), and `useFileImport` hands each kind to its flow. Meshes and CAD become parts; an SVG opens the Import SVG panel (§8) with the file loaded (`openCreator('svg', file)`); a `.urdf`, `.mjcf`, `.xml` or `.zip` (a Robot Package is a zip), and any dropped folder, opens the Import Robot sheet (§8c) with the files loaded, placing the robot at the drop point when Import is clicked; a `.bvh` opens the retarget sheet for the selected robot (§8c), or “Add joints to retarget motion onto” without one; images become reference images (§7b); a `.basecase` opens in a new tab (§5a). Only the first SVG and the first BVH open; any others, and any other file, get one “Couldn’t import ⟨files⟩” toast. Import Robot stays its own command because it also takes links. `__studio.pickFiles` hands the next picker files in tests.
- **Mesh files** (STL, 3MF, OBJ, PLY, FBX, GLB / glTF, DAE) are inserted as one `mesh` part each, standing on the drop point (`workspace/meshImport.ts`). Units and axes follow each format's convention: STL, 3MF and PLY are millimetres with Z up; OBJ millimetres with Y up; GLB, glTF and DAE metres; FBX centimetres times its unit scale. A model under 1 mm across is read as metres. Meshes are welded, never simplified, so prints keep every triangle. `mesh.file` marks an imported mesh, and only those print (`isPrinted`); robot links and SimReady meshes don't. Parsing runs in the geometry worker except 3MF and DAE, which need `DOMParser`. FBX textures are skipped (`NoTextures`), since the worker has no `document`.
- **CAD files** (STEP, STP, IGES, IGS) take the same import path on desktop: drop or Import. They never parse in the renderer or in Node: `platformBridge.openCadFile` hands the bytes to the main process (`cad:open`, or `/cad-open` over the dev bridge), which sends them to the same sandboxed converter as `import_maker_cad` (§8a, §0h) in its bodies mode. OpenCascade reads the units from the file header, so every body arrives in millimetres at true size, Y up. Each body becomes its own `mesh` part (`workspace/cadImport.ts`), named from the file's product names (else “⟨file⟩ 2”), coloured with the face colour covering most of it, and grouped under the file name when there's more than one; the whole file stands on the drop point. The converter simplifies a file past 200,000 triangles in total and refuses one with more than 1,000 bodies. A file it can't read gets the same single “Couldn’t import ⟨file⟩” toast as a broken mesh. The web demo keeps meshes only (`IS_WEB_DEMO`).
- **STEP export** goes the other way: Export ▸ STEP in the title bar, or Export STEP in ⌘K, writes `⟨scene⟩.step` for Fusion, Onshape, SolidWorks and other CAD tools (`workspace/useStepExport.ts`, desktop only, `IS_WEB_DEMO`). It takes the parts STL takes (visible solids, no reference images, `isStepBody`) as they're modelled, never as posed; with none of those in the scene the menu item is disabled and the ⌘K command is hidden. `stepJob` (`workspace/stepExport.ts`) loads catalog meshes, then gives each part one body named after it, with its colour, placed at its position and rotation in millimetres, Z up like STL, inside a sub-assembly named after its group. A replicad part goes as its code, a basic shape as an exact solid (`exactSolid`, `workspace/stepSolids.ts`: box, cube, cylinder, tube and torus always; sphere, cone, capsule and dome when round, and a dome or capsule only when its ends are at least as tall as they are wide, since OpenCascade's reader loses the curved face of a revolved arc of a wide ellipse), and a Union, Subtract or Intersect result as the boolean of its operands while it keeps the size it was made at; everything else (catalog parts, hardware, imports, OpenSCAD, sculpted and deformed parts, wedges, pyramids and hex prisms, whose flat faces the kernel merges back, and a replicad part resized unevenly) goes as its mesh, which the kernel turns into a faceted solid. Code and booleans also carry their mesh, used if the exact build fails. The kernel builds the file in its sandbox (`platformBridge.exportStep`, `cad:export-step` or `/cad-export-step`; §9, §0h). “Exporting STEP…” shows while it runs (a second click meanwhile does nothing) and goes away on a clean export; “Exported STEP without ⟨n⟩ parts” replaces it when a part had no geometry or failed both ways, and a failed export shows the kernel's error (on desktop without Electron's “Error invoking remote method” prefix, stripped by `errorMessage`, §3b-9). If no part ends up with geometry, the progress toast just closes.
- While files are dragged, the overlay reads “Drop to add a reference image” for images alone (SVG excepted), “Drop to import” otherwise.

## 7b. Reference Images

- **Adding:** ⌘V anywhere outside a text field, drop image files on the canvas (at the drop point), ⌘K → “Add Reference Image”, or right-click the canvas → “Add Reference Image”.
- **What they are:**
  - An `image_plane` part with `imageDataUrl` and role `reference`.
  - Unlit, double-sided, with a thin frame that turns blue when selected.
  - Never printed, exported to STL or used in booleans. Listed in the brief as reference photos, not parts.
- **Size:** photos are downscaled to 2048px max (`utils/imageFile.ts`). They start upright with a 120mm long side. Width and height stay locked to the photo’s aspect ratio.
- **Hide all:** the image toggle in the Layers header (shown when the scene has photos), ⇧I, or ⌘K. It’s a view setting, not undoable; hidden photos dim in Layers and aren’t rendered or clickable.
- **Inspector:** an Image section with preview, Opacity, and Stand Up / Lay Flat (backdrop vs. blueprint on the floor), with no tooltips on those two. The header is the name row alone: the Image section already says it's a photo, so there's no caption that it isn't printed. Notes describe what to take from the photo. The Layers row shows the photo as its glyph.

## 8. Mode Panels (Sculpt, Sketch, Import SVG)

There's no Shape tab. These panels appear in place of the Inspector only while they apply (`SculptAndDrawStudio`), with the title and a Done button in their own header.

- **Sculpt:** shown while the Sculpt tool (C) is on. The tool is hidden unless Settings → Canvas → Tools → Sculpt is on; when it's off, C, the toolbar button, the ⌘K entry and the Shortcuts rows (Sculpt, Sculpt inverted) are all gone, and turning it off mid-sculpt switches back to Move. Existing sculpted parts still render and export. Brushes, Size/Strength sliders, Mirror X/Z, Subdivide (only while one sculpted part is selected), Add clay presets. The panel doesn't explain the gestures: ⌥-drag inverts the brush, listed under Shortcuts. Done switches back to Move.
- **Sketch:** opened from Shapes ▾ → Sketch, ⌘K or right-click. Extrude / Revolve / Path, the Line, Rectangle, Circle and Arc tools, Snap to grid and Clear, the sketch plane, then Output (Name, T, colour, Add).
  - **Model** (`src/utils/sketch/`, framework-free): a sketch is points, lines, circles and arcs plus constraints (coincident, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, equal, tangent) and dimensions (length, radius, angle, distance), all in millimetres. `solveSketch` is our own damped least-norm Newton solver on the residuals, with a numeric Jacobian: deterministic, synchronous, so a drag re-solves every pointer move. A set whose Jacobian has fewer independent rows than equations, or that doesn't converge, is reported as `over` and never applied, so an over-constrained edit is refused (the plane's ring flashes red) and the sketch keeps its last solved state. A dragged point is weighted stiff, and a changed dimension holds its line's first point (a circle's centre), so the rest of the shape moves around it. PlaneGCS (FreeCAD's solver, as the `@salusoft89/planegcs` WASM build) was the alternative; it needs an async WASM load and is far larger than the dozen constraint kinds Sketch uses.
  - **Plane** (`SketchCanvas`): an SVG in millimetres, y up. Pinch zooms about the pointer and two-finger scroll pans. The grid step follows the zoom, and snapping uses a step about 1/80 of the view.
  - **Drawing infers** coincident (a point within 9px snaps onto it and reuses it), horizontal and vertical (within 9px of the last point's row or column) like Figma and Shapr3D. Each inference shows as a glyph by the pointer (`CoincidentIcon`, `HorizontalIcon`, `VerticalIcon`) and becomes a constraint when the click lands. A rectangle is four lines with horizontals and verticals. Lines chain until one ends on an existing point.
  - **Typed lengths:** with a shape started, typing a number opens fields by the shape: length, then Tab to the angle for a line; width, then Tab to the height for a rectangle; diameter for a circle. Enter places the shape with those dimensions.
  - **Editing:** with no tool (click the active tool or press Esc), clicking a segment selects it and shows its dimension; constrained dimensions are always shown. Clicking a dimension (or typing a number, or Enter, with one segment selected) edits it. Dragging a point re-solves live and commits on release. Selecting one line, or two entities with ⇧, shows a strip of the constraints that fit (`applicableConstraints`); constraints show as glyphs beside their entities, and clicking a glyph selects it so ⌫ removes it. Each edit is one undo step: a new sketch keeps its own history; a reopened one edits the part, so every edit is a scene undo step.
  - **On a face:** clicking a part on the canvas remembers the face under the pointer; Sketch with that part still selected opens on that face (`faceOf` in `utils/sketch/face.ts` gathers the coplanar triangles of `partTriangles`, its plane and its outline). The face's edges are dashed reference lines with fixed points: they can be snapped to and constrained against but not deleted. With no face, Sketch opens on the ground plane.
  - **Output:** the profile (`sketchProfile`: the largest closed loop, with the loops inside it as holes; circles are loops) becomes one `custom_model` part with a `contour_extrude` node (or `lathe` for Revolve) sized exactly to the profile, standing on the plane (`sketchSolid` in `utils/sketch/placement.ts`). A negative T cuts: the part is a Cutout sunk into the face, for Subtract. Revolve spins the outline around the sketch's vertical axis and stands the result on the plane.
  - **Path** (a new sketch only, the third mode beside Extrude and Revolve): designs a four-bar by the path its coupler point traces. Click points on the plane, or drag a stroke, which is resampled to 12 points by arc length (`useSketchPath`); drag a point to move it, ⌫ removes the last one and the eraser clears them. Every one of those edits, Clear included, is a step in the points' own history (`usePathHistory`, held by the panel), so ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo and redo points and never touch the hidden Extrude/Revolve sketch. The line tools and Output hide. With 5 to 12 points, `synthesizeFourBar` (`utils/linkage.ts`) runs in the linkage worker (`src/linkage/linkage.worker.ts`) 250 ms after the last change. Up to three linkages draw over the plane (`SketchPathLayer`), each tracing its curve and sweeping its crank back and forth as SVG `<animate>` frames, so nothing runs a render loop; the view frames them when they arrive. A row per linkage below the plane shows its largest path error in mm; hovering one draws it in the accent. When nothing fits, the plane's ring turns red. Clicking a row inserts that linkage on the sketch's plane (`insertLinkage` in `useSketchSession`, `linkageParts`, §8c), selects it and closes the panel, as one undo step.
  - **Solver** (`utils/linkage.ts`, `linkageFit.ts`, `linkageKinematics.ts`, framework-free): a multi-start Levenberg–Marquardt fit of the coupler point to the points, 36 starts per crank direction from a fixed seed, so the same points give the same linkages. The unknowns are the crank pivot, the ground's direction, the four link lengths (as logs), the coupler point, the first crank angle and a positive step to each next point, so the points are visited in order within one turn. Each start is a random crank-rocker scaled onto the points by a similarity fit; a start whose cost is still 100 times the best so far after 25 iterations is dropped. Penalties keep the crank the shortest link and the linkage Grashof, with links of 10 to 300 mm. The path error at a point is its distance to the curve the coupler traces over the sweep. A linkage is kept when it's a crank-rocker with a transmission angle of at least 15° and a largest error within 10% of the path's span (at least 1 mm). Ranking: largest error in 0.25 mm steps, then a transmission angle of at least 30°, then how far the curve strays from the drawn polyline plus 0.02 × the linkage's size, so compact linkages that follow the drawing win ties. 5 to 12 points take 0.1 to 0.5 s on an M-series Mac, too long for one frame, hence the worker.
  - **Editable afterwards:** the sketch, mode, T and the profile's anchor are stored on the part (`ScenePart.sketch`). Double-clicking the part reopens its sketch; each change re-extrudes it in place (`partPlane` finds the plane again from the part's pose, so a moved part keeps its sketch), and Output then holds only T.
- **Import SVG:** opened from Shapes ▾, or by Import or a drop with an SVG, which loads it. Drop zone, samples, Paste Code, then Output: the preview with its shape count (and hole count when there are holes), then the Output fields (Name, W, T, colour, Bevel). The name lives only in the Name field, which starts as the file's name, so the preview row doesn't repeat it. “Edit in Sketch”, beside the preview, hands the outline to Sketch as lines at W wide.
- Adding a part from Sketch or SVG closes the panel and shows the new part in the Inspector.
- Both end in one Add button. To keep the result, use Save to Library on the new part (the bookmark in its Inspector header, or right-click); the model it saves inserts that same part again.
- Combine lives in the multi-selection Inspector, the right-click menu and ⌘K; nothing floats over the canvas for a multi-selection.
