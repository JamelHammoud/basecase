Run Case fills the window with the open Case, robot in the middle. Around it sits whatever that Case needs: camera feeds, live joint plots, a timer, a score or a Reset button.

It's the expand button in the Case's header in the Simulate tab, and Run Case in ⌘K. Done or Esc takes you back with the scene, the selection and the camera as they were.

A Case can carry a console that says how it runs. It names the input sources, such as keys, a gamepad, a phone, the computer's camera or a code panel, and any signals of its own. It lists panels of nine kinds: camera, value, plot, table, button, list, text, a top-down map and code. It sets columns of fixed widths around the canvas.

Every panel reads named live signals, such as joint efforts, timers and scores. A block game puts Time and Score on the left and Try It, Reset and the tries on the right.

![A block game console during a try: Time counting down and Score left of the arm, and Try It, Reset and the running try on the right](/changelog/run-a-case/console.png)

A code panel is the Case's own web page, such as a joystick drawn on a canvas or a speed readout over the robot. It drives the robot like a gamepad and sends events to the Case's logic. It runs sandboxed, with no network and no storage. One that stops answering for 2 seconds is closed and says so.

![A rover Case running with a joystick in the right column and its speed and tilt over the canvas](/changelog/run-a-case/code.png)

A Case without a console runs with its cameras on the left. The brief, failures, servos and tries sit on the right. Ask the agent in words, such as a rover on a gamepad with its front camera, and it opens the Case running. MIDI, a leader arm, voice and policies are refused as inputs until they can drive a Case.

Steps are in [Run a Case](/help/run-a-case).
