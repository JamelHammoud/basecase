# Bottom toolbar and Settings

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 6. Bottom Toolbar

- **Toolbar (`ViewportToolbar`):**
  - Tools: Move V · Rotate E · Scale R · Comment M. Sculpt C appears between Scale and Comment only when Settings ▸ Canvas ▸ Tools ▸ Sculpt is on (off by default).
  - Then a divider, then **Shapes ▾** (A): a 4-column grid of basic-shape thumbnails (Plate, Cube, Shaft, Tube, Wedge, Sphere, Cone, Pyramid, Hex Prism, Capsule, Dome, Ring), then Sketch, Measure, Annotate and Import SVG.
  - **Measure (D):** also in ⌘K and the right-click menus (Measure from Here, which starts at the clicked point). Click two points; a blue line joins them with a matte label at its middle: the distance alone, units muted, scaled to read naturally (`formatLength` in `measureMath.ts`: metric is mm, then m from 1 m; imperial is inches to the nearest ¹⁄₁₆, then feet and inches from 1 ft). Points snap to a part's crease corners, then its edges (the hit part's `cad-edges`; meshes without them snap to the hit triangle's corners), within about 9 px; ⇧ places a free surface point. ⌥ locks the second point straight above or below the first (to a snapped corner's height, or the height under the pointer). Empty space measures on the floor. A third click starts a new measurement; Esc clears it, a second Esc leaves the tool. The unit system is a preference (Settings ▸ Canvas ▸ Measure: Metric / Imperial; older mm/cm/m/in values load as Metric, or Imperial for in).
  - **Annotate (N):** also in ⌘K and the empty-canvas right-click menu. Drag to draw coloured marks for the agent (circle a part, underline an edge), each with an optional label, so one message can say “yellow is right, orange is wrong”. A stroke lies on one camera-facing plane at the depth of the nearest part it crosses (the orbit target when it crosses none), so it stays put in 3D, and it's drawn over everything. On release the mark records the parts it circles (part centres inside a closed loop, `markMath.ts`), the parts it crosses, its colour and the view it was drawn from (`view`, a `look` view like `az:30,el:20`).
    - **Marks pill** (`MarkBar`, above the toolbar while the tool is on): Marker (P) and Eraser (E), then, with the Marker, four colour swatches (Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, `utils/markColors.ts`) and Custom color (the `ColorPickerPopover`, opening above), then Delete marks once there are marks (Delete mark while one is selected; both ask first, “Delete 3 marks?”, and follow with an Undo toast), then Done (Esc). No tool name or mark count: the toolbar and the canvas already show them. The colour applies to new marks and to the selected one. P and E mean Marker and Eraser only while the tool is on (otherwise Pose and Rotate); leaving the tool goes back to the Marker.
    - **Eraser:** click a mark, or drag across several, to erase them (`markEraser.ts`, using the same 10 px reach as selecting, and the cursor is a ring that size). Marks under the eraser fade while the button is down and go on release as one undo step, with an Undo toast; it asks nothing, since erasing is the tool's whole action. ⌘K lists it as Eraser too, under Tools beside Annotate, while there are marks.
    - **Selecting and moving:** pressing on a stroke or its label (`MarkLayer.hitTest`, within 10 px) selects that mark, and dragging moves it (`markMover.ts`) on a camera-facing plane through its centre. The cursor turns to move over a mark. On release the mark keeps its new place as one undo step, and the parts it circles and crosses are worked out again (`markParts.ts`, shared with drawing), so the agent sees what it now points at. A click on empty canvas deselects; a new mark is selected as it's drawn and its label field takes focus, so you can type a label, draw the next mark (pressing on the canvas saves the label) or press Esc. The label field sits where the label goes, at the top centre of the mark, in its colour (`MarkLabelField`, rendered into an anchor that `markFieldAnchor.ts` moves after each render), and the mark's label sprite hides while it's open. Enter saves and closes it, Esc reverts and closes it; another Esc leaves the tool. While a mark is selected the pill shows Delete mark (⌫). Deleting one mark or clearing all toasts with Undo.
    - **Drawing:** `three/viewport/markLayer.ts` draws marks outside `stage.overlays`, so the agent's `look` / `compare` renders show them, and frames those renders to include them (not isolated or silhouette views). Labels are sprites (`markLabel.ts`: a pill in the mark's colour, constant pixel size, above the top centre of the stroke on screen, `topCenterOnScreen`), so they're in the renders too. The selection outline (Figma blue) is in `stage.overlays`, so renders never show it.
    - Marks are scene data (`project.marks`, saved with the scene) on the undo timeline; Delete Marks is also in ⌘K and the canvas menu. The agent sees them in `get_scene` (`marks`: colour name, label, parts circled, bounds, `seenFrom`), and a message sent while marks exist lists each one's colour, label and parts (`agentMarks.ts`). Custom colours are named by hue for the agent (“purple”).
  - Measurements are a view overlay (`three/viewport/measureTool.ts`, pure math in `measureMath.ts`): not parts, not undoable, never saved, exported or captured (they live in `stage.overlays`, which captures hide). Hover picking runs once per frame; the label is positioned after each render (`RenderLoop.afterRender`); the gizmo detaches while measuring.
  - **Measure toolbar** (`MeasureToolbar`): while measuring, the bottom toolbar is swapped for one with **Ruler** (the default), **Human** and **Dog**, then **Done** on the right (`Esc`), the same exit as Annotate's bar and Print mode. State lives in `workspace/useMeasure.ts`; leaving Measure resets it to Ruler and clears the figures.
    - **Human / Dog** are scale figures: a light-blue ghost follows the pointer (on a part's surface or the floor) and a click places it there, facing front (+Z); clicking again moves it. One of each. The ruler line stays while they're placed.
    - Once the human is placed, their height joins the toolbar, with no label or tooltip since the units beside the selected Human say what it is: two fields (ft and in) where people say heights in feet (US, UK, Liberia, Myanmar, from `navigator.language`) or with Imperial units, otherwise cm (`heightsInFeet`). A US engineer measures parts in mm but still says a person is 5 ft 9 in, so this follows the region, not the Measure units. Extra or negative inches carry into feet (`toFeetInches` / `fromFeetInches` in `measureMath.ts`). It defaults to 175.3 cm, 5 ft 9 in (US adult men, CDC NHANES 2015–2018). The dog is a Labrador Retriever, 57 cm at the withers, and keeps its real size.
    - Figures are view overlays like measurements (`three/viewport/scaleFigures.ts`). They're drawn as one clean translucent layer: a depth-only pass first, then the fill with `depthWrite` off, so overlapping limbs never stack up brighter.
    - **The models are sculpted by the Basecase agent**, never hand-built from primitives: `node tools/figures/generate.mjs human dog` runs the real app headless (the same harness as §8a, `tools/parts/studioRun.mjs`) with the briefs in `tools/figures/briefs.mjs` (subject, pose, anthropometric or breed-standard proportions, find references, sculpt with replicad lofts, then a review pass against the references), and writes `public/figures/<kind>.glb` plus renders and the agent's notes in `tools/figures/generated/<kind>/`. Check the renders before shipping, and rerun when the agent gets better. `figureModels.ts` loads a GLB once per app, merges it into one smooth-shaded geometry and stands it on the floor; the human is normalized to unit height, so its scale is its height. `tools/figures/figures.test.mjs` checks the shipped files.
  - Active tool: solid blue fill.
  - Keep it to tools and creation only. Placement actions (Drop to Floor, Stack) live in the right-click menu, the Inspector and shortcuts (F, S).
- **No view-controls cluster on the canvas.** Snap, trackpad mode and shortcuts live in Settings (§6a); the grid shows and hides from View ▸ Show Grid, ⌘K and the canvas right-click menu; camera views live in View ▸ Camera, ⌘K, the canvas right-click menu and keys 1–4 / Z.

## 6a. Settings (`SettingsWindow`)

- Opens from the app menu ▸ **Settings** (⌘,), ⌘K, or Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts / `?` (opens the Shortcuts pane). It remembers the last pane. ⌘K also opens each pane by its own name, “⟨pane⟩ Settings” (Canvas Settings, Build Settings…), from one list, `SETTINGS_PANES` in `src/app/settingsPanes.ts`, which the window's sidebar reads too; the Shortcuts pane keeps Keyboard Shortcuts, as in the Help menu.
- A sheet with a sidebar of panes, like macOS System Settings. There's no Layout pane: the sidebar and inspector show and hide from the title bar, ⌘B / ⌘I and ⌘K, and double-clicking a panel's edge resets its width (§5), so a pane would only repeat them.
  - **Canvas:** Trackpad (Mouse and touchpad on Windows: Two-finger scroll, or Scroll, Orbit / Pan / Zoom), Snapping (Move Off/1/2/5/10 mm, Rotate Off/5/15/45/90°), Measure (Units Metric / Imperial), Tools ▸ Sculpt (off by default). The group is named for what the switch adds, never Experimental (§3c). There's no Display group: the grid shows and hides from the View menu, ⌘K and the canvas right-click menu, and reference images from the View menu, ⌘K, ⇧I and the Layers header (§5), so a switch here would only repeat them. Zoom makes the wheel zoom (`zoomScroll` in `wheel.ts`, about 16% a notch) for mouse users; it's the default on Windows, Orbit on macOS. Middle-drag pans in every mode. Scroll, pinch and drag gestures change with this setting, so its hint describes them for the chosen mode and Shortcuts doesn't list them; pan and camera keys, and the modifiers that change a tool (⇧ to move, rotate or measure without snapping, ⌥ to measure vertically or invert the sculpt brush), are listed once, under Shortcuts, never repeated beside a setting or in a tool's panel.
  - **Agent:** one untitled card, since the pane title already names it: Run with Claude / Codex / Gemini / Offline, the command (status under it, or where to sign in). `SettingsGroup`'s `title` is optional for a card like this.
  - **Build** (`components/settings/BuildPane.tsx`), in the Build tab's order: Checks ▸ Have the agent fix new blockers (up to 2 follow-ups per message), Prices ▸ Currency (US Dollar by default; “Build Settings” in ⌘K, found by “currency” too), and Build guide ▸ Update automatically; both switches on by default (§8b). Currency lives here, not in a pane of its own, because every price it changes is in the Build tab. Each row's label leaves out what its group title already says.
  - **Printers** (`components/settings/PrintersPane.tsx`, `PrinterEditor.tsx`): the printers in a first card left untitled, since the pane title names it (name, model, nozzle, how prints are sent, address, serial, access code or API key, Test Connection, Make Default, Delete), with Find on Network and Add Printer in its last row (labelled No printers when the list is empty, unlabelled otherwise, since the buttons say what it's for), then the slicers Basecase found (Get Bambu Studio when there's none). Deleting a printer asks first, like every delete. §8f.
  - **Accounts** (`components/settings/AccountsPane.tsx`, desktop only): password fields like a printer's access code (`settings/SecretField.tsx`, shared with Printers), one group per service. Each key is sealed with `safeStorage` in `library/agent-config/`, beside the agent keys, and never comes back to the page, a scene or an export. A saved key shows as dots in its empty field, so the hint under it is left for what the dots can't say, the account it signed in to or an error, never “Saved”. A refused key stays in its field beside the error, so it can be fixed instead of pasted again; `SecretField` clears its draft only when `onSecret` resolves. An error shown here drops its own “in Settings ▸ Accounts”; the same error in a toast elsewhere carries an Open button to this pane (§3b-9).
    - **Hugging Face** ▸ Access token. Saving checks the token with the Hub (`whoami-v2`) in the main process and refuses one that can only read; the hint then shows the account name. Saving another token replaces it; there's no sign-out, so revoking a token is done on huggingface.co. Kept in `hub-keys.json`. Publish to Hugging Face opens this pane when no one is signed in (§8h).
    - **Onshape** ▸ Access key and Secret key. Each saves when the field loses focus; once both are in, the main process checks them with Onshape (`users/sessioninfo`) and the hint under Secret key shows the account name, or Onshape's refusal shows under the key just saved. A refusal is remembered (`refused` in the key file, cleared by the next save) and comes back as `error` from `account()`, so it shows under Secret key whenever the pane opens. Kept in `onshape-keys.json`; the page only learns which are saved. Import Robot uses them for Onshape links (§8c).
    - **Google Maps** ▸ Map Tiles key. Saving checks the key in the main process by fetching the root tileset with it, and refuses one Google turns down; there's no account name to show. Kept in `earth-keys.json`. Place on Map opens this pane with the field focused when no key is saved (`openSettings(pane, field)`, whose `field` names the one field to focus, §12).
  - **Shortcuts:** every keyboard shortcut, named as in the toolbar, menus and ⌘K, from one list, `SHORTCUT_GROUPS` in `src/components/shortcutGroups.ts` (`ShortcutsList` renders it; the Agent group is desktop only). Each row names a command in the shortcut table, `electron/shortcuts.json`, and shows its keys from there, so the pane lists only keys a handler runs (§11). A shortcut that works across the workspace or in the composer gets a row there and in the [Keyboard shortcuts](../../site/content/help/keyboard-shortcuts.md) help article, whose `##` groups and Action column `shortcutGroups.test.ts` holds to the list.
- **Sheet:** 860 × 620, 18px corners, matte (dark 0.5px keyline + drop shadow, no light ring). The sidebar (224px, `#1c1c1c`) sits on the `#222222` sheet by lightness alone, with no border. Pane items are 34px with a plain 16px icon (`white/40`); the open pane gets a soft fill and `white/90` icon and label, nothing else. The sidebar title and the open pane's title (20px semibold, 64px header) both center on y = 32. Close (Esc) sits at the header's right, its glyph in line with the cards' right edge. No line under the header; the content fades under it instead (`useScrollFade`). Content scrolls vertically only (`overflow-x-hidden`), with 28px side padding.
- **Groups and rows** (`SettingsGroup` / `SettingsRow` in `src/components/SettingsRows.tsx`): a sentence-case group label (12.5px medium, `white/50`) above a rounded inset card; the pane title, group labels and cards share one left edge, and row content sits 16px inside the card (12px corners, `white/[0.035]`), rows separated by full-width hairlines. Rows are at least 52px with 16px side padding: label (13.5px, `white/90`) with an optional hint (12.5px, `white/50`) on the left, the control on the right. `stacked` rows put a text field under the label and the hint under the field. Units are a muted suffix on the label (“Move mm”). `SettingsRow` doesn't wire its label to the control, so every control takes the row's label as its `aria-label` (Currency, Move mm, Run with…), or a screen reader hears a bare “combobox” or tab list. A row with several fields names each one (Width, Depth, Height). `SettingsPanes.test.ts` renders the Canvas, Agent and Build panes and fails on an unnamed control. Shortcuts use the same cards with 40px rows.
- Grid, snap, trackpad mode, the Sculpt tool and Build pane settings persist in `localStorage` (`preferences`); the currency in `currency`, rates in `exchange-rates`.
