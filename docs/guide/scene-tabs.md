# Scene tabs

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 5a. Scene Tabs

Several scenes stay open at once, each with its own parts, undo history, selection, comments and agent conversation.

- **Architecture:** `App.tsx` is the tab host; each tab is a `SceneWorkspace` (`src/SceneWorkspace.tsx`).
  - Every tab stays mounted (background tabs mount shortly after launch, §0f), so a background agent keeps building and can render its review shots. Background tabs are `invisible` + `inert`, and their `Viewport3D` is `paused` (no render loop; captures still render on demand). Closing a tab frees its WebGL context (`forceContextLoss`).
  - Up to 8 tabs (browsers cap WebGL contexts near 16). A ninth says “Close a scene to open another. Up to 8 stay open.”
  - App-wide state lives in the host and reaches scenes as `studio` (`StudioShared`): preferences, panel open state and widths, libraries, chat list, the agent connection, Settings. Per scene: everything about the scene, Left/Right panel tabs, the palette, toasts.
  - **Reading another tab's robot:** `studio.robotsBeside(tabId)` returns every other open tab's robot (`sceneRobots` in `src/app/tabModel.ts`: the scene's name, parts and joints, from the tab's live snapshot, or its seed while unmounted; tabs without joints are skipped). It's a call, not state, so a scene reads it in a handler (opening the Case builder, opening a Case, starting a try) and never during render. A Case's extra robot slots are filled this way (§8h).
  - **Running another tab's robot:** `studio.handleOf(tabId)` returns that tab's `WorkspaceHandle`. `caseRobot()` measures its parts in its own viewport (paused or not) and returns them with its project and triangles; `fingerprint()` is its readiness fingerprint; `openCase(kase)` opens that Case there (or adds it) in the Simulate tab. Compare With uses all three (§8h); like `robotsBeside`, they're called from handlers and effects, never during render.
  - Only the scene in front listens to the keyboard, paste, the menu bar and the dev `__studio` hook. Background scenes skip re-renders for app-wide changes (custom `memo` comparator) and catch up when brought to front.
- **Where:** in the title bar (§2), ending 8px before the scene's actions. With the sidebar open, the first tab starts over the canvas card's left edge (rail + sidebar width + 8px gap), so the strip lines up with the canvas, and slides with it as the sidebar resizes or toggles; with the sidebar closed (or too narrow to clear the title bar controls) it starts 8px after the Scenes menu. `App.tsx` lays the sidebar toggle and `SceneTabs` over each scene's title bar drag region. No glass: the tabs are part of the window chrome.
- **Tabs (`SceneTabs`):** 28px rounded pills (8px corners), 180px wide, shrinking to 96px, 6px apart, on the title bar's centerline.
  - The active tab sits on a flat `#262626` fill (no edge or highlight); its label is `white/90`, 13px medium, and its × always shows.
  - Background tabs are labels (`white/50`, `white/90` on hover) in a faint `white/[0.07]` inset ring (box-shadow, so it takes no space), with a soft fill on hover and × on hover.
  - Nothing else: no icons or separators. While a scene's agent works, a 12px white spinner sits before its name.
  - Past their minimum width they scroll sideways (the wheel scrolls; an edge fades only while tabs are hidden past it, measured from layout so sliding tabs don't trigger it).
  - New tabs grow in (`animate-tab-in`); tabs slide into place after insert, close and drop (FLIP, 220ms).
  - Press selects. Drag past 4px reorders. Double-click renames inline. Middle-click closes. × closes (`Close  ⌘W`). It shows on the active tab and on hover; elsewhere it takes no room and no clicks, so a background tab's name runs to its right edge, and on hover the × fades in over the name's faded end (§4). It's a 28px mouse target hidden from the accessibility tree, since a button inside a tab breaks the tab role, so keyboard and screen reader users close with ⌘W or the tab's menu. Right-click: Rename, Duplicate, New Scene, Reopen Closed Scene, Close, Close Other Scenes, Close Scenes to the Right.
  - Resting 800ms on a background tab opens a small preview card under it: a fresh frame of that scene, its name, and its part count in the Scenes list's words (“0 parts”, “1 part”, `scenes/partCount.ts`), or the agent's live activity while it works. The frame is copied at a maximum side of 560px and encoded once as JPEG in the image worker (§0f).
  - + (28px) 2px after the last tab (its glyph carries 6px of air, so it reads about as far out as the tabs are apart): `New scene  ⌘T`.
- **Opening and closing:**
  - **Browser storage:** `utils/platform/web/webScenes.ts` lists the `sceneSummaries` IndexedDB store, without reading full saved scenes. Saves preserve the previous preview and dimensions from that summary too. Database version 4 builds those summaries from existing scenes with a cursor during the upgrade. Saving, updating a preview and deleting write the scene and its summary in one transaction and return only after it commits. Full records load for opening a scene or generating and updating a missing preview. `webScenes.test.ts` covers each write path, `idbWrite.test.ts` covers transaction completion and aborts, and `e2e/webScenes.e2e.test.ts` checks the version 3 upgrade in the browser.
  - New scenes are numbered so open tabs never share a name: Untitled, Untitled 2…
  - **First launch** (`launchTabs` in `app/openScenes.ts`, `app/firstLaunch.ts`): the window restores the tabs it had open. When none of them loads and the Library holds no saved scene, the first tab is the gallery's `rover-reach-the-flag` bundle instead of a blank scene: fetched from `/gallery/`, checked by `parseGalleryBundle` and turned into a scene by `bundleScene`, the same path Open Scene takes (§8h). It's unsaved under a fresh id, and its seed's `playCase` opens the Case and plays its reference (`useOpeningCase`). Autosave writes nothing until it's edited, so a person who closes it untouched sees it again next launch; once any scene is saved, launches restore tabs or open a blank one. When the Library can't be read, the tab is blank. The web studio does the same from IndexedDB. The e2e harness lists one saved scene by default, so suites start on a blank canvas; `e2e/firstLaunch.e2e.test.ts` passes an empty Library.
  - Library → a scene: shows its tab if it's open, reuses the tab in front if it's untouched (blank, unrenamed, unused), otherwise opens a new tab after it.
  - Closing a tab stops its agent, saves its chat and ends its session. The toast reads “Closed ⟨name⟩” + [Undo] (“…and stopped its agent” when one was running); Undo reopens it in place with its chat. Untouched tabs close silently. Closing the last tab leaves a blank one, which Undo removes again.
  - ⇧⌘T reopens the last 10 closed scenes. Duplicate (the tab's menu, ⌘K, or the Scenes menu) copies the scene, not the chat, and names it “Sprout copy”, then “Sprout copy 2”, skipping any name an open tab or a saved scene already has (`sceneCopyName`).
  - When a background agent finishes: “Agent finished in ⟨name⟩” + [Show].
- **Sharing scenes** (`utils/sceneFile.ts`): Export ▸ Scene (also File ▸ Export Scene and ⌘K) downloads `<name>.basecase`, gzipped JSON `{ format: 'basecase-scene', version, scene }` holding the name, description, parts, comments, project settings and preview, built from the scene in memory. It leaves out what belongs to the sender's machine: the chat, `prints` (so Skip N printed parts doesn't skip the sender's prints), `policies` and `buildGuide` (their files live in the sender's `library/`). File ▸ Open Scene File, ⌘K, or dropping the file on the canvas saves it to the Library under a new id and opens it in a tab (`importSceneFile` in `useSceneLibrary`). The file is untrusted: it must match the format, parts need an id, kind and position, images must be `data:image/` URLs, and a newer `version` says “Made with a newer Basecase. Update to open it.” Bump `VERSION` only when an older app can't read the new shape. A robot imported from Onshape keeps its link as ids only, never the account's keys: `group.onshape` on each of its parts (document, workspace or version, element), `robotLink.onshape` (its Onshape occurrences) and `onshape` on each joint (the mate's id, and `gone` once the mate is deleted in Onshape), which Update from Onshape uses (§8c). A shared scene keeps them, so whoever opens it can update the robot with their own keys if their account can open the document.
- **Shortcuts:** ⌘T new, ⌘W close, ⇧⌘T reopen, ⌃Tab / ⌃⇧Tab and ⇧⌘] / ⇧⌘[ next / previous, ⌘1–⌘8 a tab, ⌘9 the last. They work while typing (except with Settings open). ⌘K lists every other open scene under Open Scenes, plus New / Rename / Duplicate / Close / Reopen Scene. Rename Scene opens the tab's rename field, the same one double-click opens; the field's tab lives in `useTabActions` (`renamingTab`), so ⌘K can open it.
- **Sheet imports:** scene files and gallery bundles validate optional sheet fields through `src/sheet/validation.ts`, including boolean operands and custom model nodes. A sheet needs a supported material and positive finite thickness. Its transform must contain 16 finite numbers, the affine bottom row `[0, 0, 0, 1]`, and a finite positive determinant. Missing fields keep older scenes readable. `src/sheet/importValidation.test.ts` checks valid round trips and malformed settings, nonfinite values, perspective, singular and reflected transforms at both import boundaries.
- **Imported world assets:** a scene's `worldAssets` table stores each imported world's visual mesh, materials, texture and splat bytes, and collision hulls once under the SHA-256 of its canonical serialized content. Cases carry only the hash and placement. `src/cases/worldAssets.ts` registers the typed geometry in memory, restores and validates saved tables before a scene opens, and collects the worlds referenced by its Cases for autosave and export. The binary codec writes little-endian numeric buffers and byte payloads as base64. Scene imports check finite coordinates, buffer sizes, vertex indices, material groups, transforms and at most 64 collision hulls, then verify every content hash before registration. Each asset is capped at 128 MiB of decoded binary data, including at most 64 MiB of textures and splat bytes. The same budgets apply when the worker prepares an asset, so accepted worlds remain readable after saving. A scene with missing world content is refused even if another open scene already registered the same hash. Desktop scene JSON, browser IndexedDB and portable `.basecase` files carry the same table, while Library summaries leave it out. Autosave reuses each immutable asset's cached JSON across project edits.
