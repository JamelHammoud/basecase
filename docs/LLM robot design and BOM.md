# Catalogs, checklists and slicers make LLM robots buildable

Language models can now write robot CAD that compiles, but only four LLM-driven robot designs have been publicly built, and every one needed human fixes before or after printing. The best end-to-end result, ShanghaiTech's October 2026 "Agentic Robot Creation" Gorilla, passed only **15 of 23 pre-fabrication checks** with the strongest model tested and was hand-corrected before it was printed on a Bambu printer ([arXiv 2610.17881](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17881)). No paper reports measured hardware performance. The literature does agree on what works. Fix the actuator and electronics kit from a catalog with exact geometry. Generate parametric code in an execute–render–measure loop. Prefer numeric acceptance tests to vision-model opinion (VLM self-checks are only about 65% accurate). Gate printing on an explicit checklist covering mounting, interference, fastener chains, torque and build volume. For a desktop app targeting a Bambu Lab P1S, the remaining pieces are mostly deterministic engineering, not more model intelligence. That means joint-sweep collision checks, torque and tipping budgets, FDM design-rule checks, and a headless Bambu Studio/OrcaSlicer call that returns exact print time and grams. One finding reshapes the BOM goal: Amazon retired PA-API 5, bars Associates content in installable client software, and blocks the Claude-User agent in robots.txt. "Confirmed working links" therefore have to come from a maintained catalog of verified ASINs, an optional third-party check with the user's own API key, and a final check in the user's own browser, never from the agent fetching Amazon.

## Four robots built, zero hardware metrics published

The honest state of the art is thin. **Text2Robot** (Duke, ICRA 2025) is the most cited "words to walking robot" pipeline, but the language step is a Meshy text-to-mesh call, not an LLM writing CAD. Candidate meshes were filtered **by hand** for symmetry and four legs. Fusion 360 repaired them, and joints were placed at cross-section minima and maxima. Evolution plus PPO in IsaacGym (4,096 environments, 2.46×10⁷ steps per robot) then picked the morphology. Four quadrupeds were printed in PLA with eight Hiwonder HTD-45H servos, a Raspberry Pi 4B and a 3S LiPo each, in "approximately a day." The hardware test was open-loop playback at 0.1 m/s, described only as "sufficient," and the authors call their sim-to-real "primitive" ([arXiv 2406.19963](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19963v3)). The 2023 TU Delft/EPFL tomato harvester in *Nature Machine Intelligence* used ChatGPT for the concept (food supply, a soft gripper, a Dynamixel motor). Humans did all the CAD and fabrication and reported only "promising" tests ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/researchers-use-chatgpt-to-make-a-tomato-picking-robot/); [EPFL Infoscience](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/303686?ln=en)). AID-SR built some LLM-designed continuum robots, after only **26.7%** of designs met its simulated success criterion ([arXiv 2609.08220](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08220)).

The ShanghaiTech paper is the closest analogue to the app in question. Its agent receives a reference image, a task, CAD assets and specs for candidate actuators, and **official assembly examples** showing how motors, links and fasteners join. It outputs linked STEP, STL and URDF. A 23-item checklist covers valid solids, watertightness, build-volume fit, tool access, tolerances, interference, load paths, mass reconciliation, fastener length chains, joint-limit margins, cable clearance, torque saturation and collision-free motion. The authors label the mechanical items "conditional calculations, not measured strength." On the same Gorilla design, GPT6-Astra passed 15/23, GPT5.6-Terra 6/23, GPT5.6-Sol 5/23 and GPT-5.5 4/23. The remaining "missing mounting features, self-intersections between links, and insufficient clearance around actuators" were **fixed by hand**. The result was an 8-DoF robot on eight Dynamixel XL330-M288-T servos, printed in PLA on a Bambu X1E at 0.2 mm layers and 15% infill. Its behavior agent scored 3.97/5 with 57 human raters, against 3.60 for one-shot motion, but again with no quantitative hardware metrics ([arXiv 2610.17881](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17881)). Most other "LLM robot design" work (RoboMorph, LASeR, RoboMoRe, Debate2Create, BesiegeField) runs entirely in simulation over grammars, voxels or parametric URDFs, not fabrication-ready CAD. BesiegeField reports that strong models struggle with "spatially precise assembly" and with turning feedback into useful structural edits ([arXiv 2407.08626](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08626v2); [arXiv 2510.14980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14980)).

| System | LLM role | Built? | Measured result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agentic Robot Creation (ShanghaiTech, Oct 2026) | Designs STEP/STL/URDF around catalog XL330s | Yes, 1 robot, Bambu X1E | 15/23 checks best model; hand-fixed; no hardware metrics |
| Text2Robot (Duke, 2024–25) | None in CAD (Meshy mesh + evolution + RL) | Yes, 4 quadrupeds | "Sufficient" open-loop walking; no numbers |
| ChatGPT tomato gripper (NMI 2023) | Concept and component advice | Yes, human-designed CAD | "Promising" tests |
| AID-SR continuum robots (2026) | Designs, MuJoCo-screened | Some | 26.7% met sim criterion |
| RoboMorph, LASeR, RoboMoRe, Debate2Create | Mutation or proposal operator | No | Sim only; e.g. +73% distance (Debate2Create) |

Builder write-ups show the failure modes at hobby scale. A Claude Code + FreeCAD 5-DOF arm with MG996R servos took about 30 hours to design, print and assemble on the author's first printer. Two servos weren't retained by their tabs and were taped in. A lid was glued because the screws were too short. A wall blocked rotation and was cut. The turntable shaft was reprinted, and a print-in-place hinge fused solid ([hiitzroman](https://hiitzroman.com/blog/robotic-arm-with-claude-code-and-freecad)). XDA's June 2026 OpenSCAD test is more damning about self-verification. Claude invented a 35 mm mic radius when the real one is 46.67 mm, and claimed a maker published no dimensions when they sat in its FAQ. GPT-5.5 left an anti-tip rib floating 2 mm above the base while the export reported a clean manifold ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/asked-claude-gpt-design-3d-prints-failed-opposite-ways/)). Commercial tools don't close this gap. Zoo's Zookeeper agent plans constraints before modeling and exposes inspect/snapshot tools over MCP, but it targets single parts ([Digital Engineering 24/7](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/zoo-introduces-zookeeper-agent-for-cad-design/products)). Flux and Diode do PCBs, and Diode puts every design through human EE review before production ([diode.computer](https://www.diode.computer/)). Nobody sells end-to-end robot design.

Two conclusions follow. Every built design fixed the actuator kit first and shaped links around known parts, which validates a catalog-first app. And every one needed a human, so a structured checklist plus a human sign-off before printing is the state of the art, not a crutch.

## Execution feedback beats vision critique in CAD-as-code

The CAD-generation literature gives a clear ranking of what improves geometry. Ordered by measured reliability:

1. Compile-error feedback.
2. Best-of-N selection by a geometric score.
3. Numeric geometric feedback.
4. VLM render critique.
5. A bigger reasoning budget.

**Execution feedback produced the largest single jump.** In Query2CAD, GPT-4 Turbo went from 53.6% to 73.2% success after one error-refinement round, then plateaued at 76.7% ([arXiv 2406.00144](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00144v1)). **Best-of-N with a geometric selector** drove CAD-Recode's IoU from 77.6% to 92.0% on DeepCAD by executing 10 candidates and keeping the lowest-Chamfer one ([ICCV 2025](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/67036/1/CADRecode_ICCV_2025.pdf)). **Render-and-critique helps modestly.** CADCodeVerify has the VLM write yes/no questions, render four views and fix only failed answers. That cut GPT-4's point-cloud distance from 0.155 to 0.127, but the VLM's answers to its own questions were only **64.6–68.2% accurate**, and nothing improved beyond round two ([arXiv 2410.05340](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v2)). P3D-Bench found that 10 turns of multi-view feedback lifted Gemini 3.1 Pro on OpenSCAD from 0.715 to 0.745, while GPT-5.5 stopped after one turn 62% of the time and gained almost nothing. **Feedback helps only if the harness forces the model to keep revising** ([arXiv 2606.11152](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11152v1)). The same benchmark found that maximum "thinking" added just +0.034 on average and reduced CadQuery validity for four of five models.

Tools matter as much as models. Wrapping the same model in build123d-mcp lifted its CADGenBench score from **0.360 to 0.457** and validity from 88% to 100%. That server exposes execute, validate-before-export, volume, bounding box, center of mass, hole and boss detection, verified feature edits and shape comparison ([GitHub](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)). CADGenBench itself scores 0.4 × shape + 0.4 × interface fit in a pin-and-slot fixture + 0.2 × topology (Betti numbers for solids, through-holes and cavities), behind a watertight-B-rep gate. On that functional metric Claude Opus 5 scored 0.351 and GPT-5.6 Sol 0.405, and agents asked to edit often rebuilt the whole part and broke things they weren't asked to touch ([CADGenBench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench); [primer](https://lehuili-cad-agent-intro.static.hf.space/index.html)). ICML 2026's CAD-bench is starker. Agents built a right-angle gearbox in **96.9%** of attempts but averaged **4.0%** on its functional score: executable is not the same as mechanically meaningful ([ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/77860)). Simple parts are a solved problem for frontier models, though. On CadQueryEval (watertight, single component, bbox within 1 mm, volume within 2%), claude-opus-5.5 scored 100% where claude-opus-4.7 scored 60% ([GitHub](https://github.com/danwahl/cadqueryeval)). Difficulty, not syntax, is now the wall. On Text2CAD-Bench, invalidity rises from about 15% on primitives to **70–90% on sweeps, lofts, shells and freeform** (Claude 4.5 Sonnet: 70% invalid, IoU 0.25 at L3). Fine-tuned domain models that look superb in distribution collapse to IoU 0.02–0.08 out of distribution ([arXiv 2605.18430](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)).

On representation, the evidence supports the app's existing OpenSCAD + replicad split. In P3D-Bench, OpenSCAD was **100% valid versus 91–94% for CadQuery** with comparable or better geometry, and it was better on assemblies (GPT-5.5 0.603 vs 0.570). CadQuery's failures were parameter and kernel errors, not parse errors ([arXiv 2606.11152](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11152v1)). An informal same-model test logged about 0.4 code errors per OpenSCAD generation against 1.4–1.7 for build123d, mostly from API hallucination ([grandpacad](https://grandpacad.com/en/blog/openscad-vs-cadquery-vs-build123d)). B-rep kernels remain necessary for true fillets, shells, lofts and feature queries. replicad shares OpenCascade with CadQuery, so it likely shares CadQuery's failure classes (an inference, not measured).

For assemblies, the 2026 systems converge on one design. Declare typed interfaces (connector frames, joints with motion type and limits) before generating parts, then let a deterministic solver place parts rather than LLM arithmetic. ArtiCAD's ablation shows the payoff: connectors decided at design time gave **100% versus 89.2%** success ([alphaxiv](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.10992)). AssemCAD adds geometry-backed ports and mates checked against B-rep evidence ([takara](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2607.05123)). Planning first pays off too. CAD-Coder's chain-of-thought step halved mean Chamfer distance (17.34 → 6.54), and ProCAD's clarify-first agent cut invalidity from 4.8% to 0.9% against Claude Sonnet 4.5 ([arXiv 2505.19713](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19713v3); [arXiv 2602.03045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03045)).

## Professional robot CAD is defined by load paths, budgets and serviceability

No published critique of amateur versus professional robot CAD exists. The well-regarded printable robots define the standard by example, and toy-like demo geometry is what you get when their practices are missing. The first marker is that **servo output shafts never carry bending loads alone.** Lynxmotion puts an idler horn on a 3 × 8 mm flanged bearing coaxial with the output ([Lynxmotion](https://wiki.lynxmotion.com/info/wiki/lynxmotion/view/ses-v2/ses-v2-system/ses-v2-gearing-horn/?rev=36.1)), and ROBOTIS sells idler horn sets for hinge frames ([RobotShop](https://ca.robotshop.com/products/robotis-idler-horn-set-hn12-i101-dynamixel-x-series-430-models)). ToddlerBot (Stanford, 30 DoF, 3.4 kg, under $6,000) goes further. A 1:1 spur-gear set moves hip-yaw loads onto a secondary axis with planar bearings and metal shafts, so the XC330's bushed shaft sees no transverse force. Knee and neck pitch use parallel linkages on ball bearings, and the knee motor is raised to cut leg inertia ([arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893)).

The second marker is that **actuators are sized from computed torques, with stated margins.** ToddlerBot estimated knee 2.35 Nm and ankle pitch 2.66 Nm and chose the XM430 (3.0 Nm stall), a thin 1.1–1.3× margin that works because of short duty cycles ([arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893)). Berkeley Humanoid Lite's locomotion policy used only about 30% of actuator torque ([arXiv 2504.17249](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17249)). Disney sized BDX's 34 Nm and 24 Nm actuators from animation-derived joint torques and velocities ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)). The STS3215, the default hobby bus servo, is rated about 6.5 kg·cm continuous against 19.5 kg·cm peak at 6 V ([Core Electronics](https://core-electronics.com.au/feetech-sts3215-smart-servo.html.plain.md)). A design that compares holding torque to peak torque is quietly wrong.

The third marker is that **printed structure is designed around layer-line weakness.** Berkeley Humanoid Lite uses through-screws and embedded brass hex standoffs, merges subcomponents into single prints to avoid fastener stress concentrations, and keeps every part inside a 200 mm cube. It measured the cost of PLA honestly: its printed cycloidal reducer reached 319 Nm/rad of stiffness against 1,468 Nm/rad for a PA-CF reference ([arXiv 2504.17249](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17249)). CNC Kitchen's hook tests quantify the anisotropy. Parts loaded across layers failed at **55% of flat strength for PLA, 46% for PETG and 29% for ASA** ([CNC Kitchen](https://cnckitchen.com/blog/comparing-pla-petg-amp-asa-feat-prusament?format=amp)). ToddlerBot's authors argue that "design has a greater impact than material choice" for strength, because deflection scales with L² at small scale.

The fourth marker is that **serviceability and reproducibility are designed in.** ToddlerBot minimized screw types, kept tool access clear, and used color-coded printed jigs that set joint zeros in under a minute. A broken part was reprinted in 21 minutes and swapped in 14 ([arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893)). SO-101 ships "Gauge Zero" and "Gauge Tight" fit prints for the STS3215 pocket before the full set, and forbids supports inside horizontal screw holes ([SO-ARM100](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100)). Power is a designed part as well. ToddlerBot's board carries XT30 connectors, an E-stop terminal, JST-EH bus outlets and buck converters. Finally, real projects publish cost, servo count and build time. SO-101 is $121.94 for one follower arm. Berkeley Humanoid Lite is $4,312 in the US, of which only **$200 is printed parts**, and motors plus compute are about 90% of ToddlerBot's cost.

Industrial design evidence is thinner but consistent. Disney aimed for "a simple design which satisfies the creative intent," with modular show functions and rounded foam soles that give passive ankle roll ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)). Spot's smooth yellow cover is credited with changing how the robot was perceived ([Ceramics.org](https://ceramics.org/ceramic-tech-today/ceramic-video/video-novel-uses-of-robots-during-the-covid-19-pandemic/)), and the Anki Vector teardown shows professional products spending heavily on multi-material housings ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/teardowns/anki-vector-robot-teardown)). Taken together, these define the amateur tells an agent should be scored against:

- a single-sided horn mount
- no visible fasteners or inserts
- no wiring path or power board
- no torque table
- parts that exceed a 200 mm bed, or need supports in their screw holes
- exposed actuators with no shells
- proportions that put the battery high
- no calibration, assembly or repair provisions

The professional versions are a coaxial idler bearing on every revolute joint, one or two screw sizes throughout, heat-set M3 inserts in proper bosses, screwed-on shells with deliberate reveals over a structural frame, a limited color palette, mass low and central, and ranges of motion checked with hard stops.

## A layered verification stack ends at the slicer

Because VLM judgment is the least reliable verifier, the stack should run from cheap and exact to expensive and fuzzy. The research supports six layers, and most already have precedents in the app's existing `look`, `compare`, `check_scene` and `check_skeleton` tools.

**Layer one is a per-part acceptance test** modeled on CadQueryEval and CADGenBench. The agent states the intended envelope, and optionally a volume and a hole count. The kernel then returns pass or fail for watertight/manifold, single component, bounding box within ±1 mm, volume within ±2%, and through-hole count (Betti b1) ([CadQueryEval](https://github.com/danwahl/cadqueryeval); [CADGenBench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench)). This costs nothing and would have caught XDA's floating rib, which a plain manifold check missed. A feature query that finds cylindrical holes with diameter and axis turns "4× M3 on a 31 mm pattern" into a checkable claim, as build123d-mcp's `find_candidates` does. An edit diff guard that reports bbox, volume and feature deltas after every change addresses CADGenBench's rebuild-everything failure.

**Layer two is a joint-sweep interference check, reported the SolidWorks way.** SolidWorks lists the frame, the parts involved and the interference volume, and clicking an entry poses the assembly there. Clearance Verification flags gaps below a set minimum ([SolidWorks](https://help.solidworks.com/2019/english/SolidWorks/motionstudies/t_detecting_interference_motion.htm); [Clearance Verification](https://help.solidworks.com/2020/English/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Detecting_Problems_SWassy.htm)). The app checks interference only at the zero pose today. Stepping each joint 2–5° through its range and testing exact triangle meshes with the existing BVH code would yield a usable range per joint ("left knee hits thigh shell at −62°; usable −58…+90°"). MuJoCo should not do this job: it replaces mesh geoms with convex hulls and excludes parent–child pairs by default, so it reports false contacts on concave brackets and hides real clashes between adjacent links ([MuJoCo docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)).

**Layer three is torque, mass and stability budgets.** Static torque at joint j is τ = Σ m_i·g·r⊥_i over all distal mass plus payload, which is MuJoCo's `qfrc_bias` at zero velocity. The research notes give a worked example. A 30 cm arm with a 0.5 kg payload needs about 1.77 Nm (18 kg·cm) at the shoulder, so an MG996R (about 9–11 kg·cm stall) fails and an STS3215 at peak barely passes. Published margin advice ranges from 1.2× to 5× and comes mostly from vendors and forums ([Kpower](https://www.kpower.com/insight_servo/6656.html); [RobotShop forum](https://community.robotshop.com/forum/t/robot-arm-torque-tutorial/31349/60)). No peer-reviewed source sets the factor, so a defensible default is required torque ≤ 50% of stall at the actual supply voltage, tightening for legged joints. The mass model should use datasheet masses for catalog parts and shell-plus-infill estimates for prints, and flag any link under 1 g, which is the near-zero-mass trap reported for Onshape exports ([wikidocs](https://wikidocs.net/384466)). Stability follows from projecting the center of mass onto the support polygon: the tipping angle about each edge is atan(d/h), checked across arm poses or lift heights ([UTS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/27997)).

**Layer four is structure.** No mature 3D FEA runs in the browser. NGSolve via Pyodide is the only full WASM FEM found, and Stabileo and Feasium cover frames and 2D plane stress only ([Stabileo](https://github.com/lambdaclass/stabileo/wiki); [NGSolve](https://docu.ngsolve.org/ngs24/myaddons/lite.html)). For hobby-servo brackets, closed-form cantilever checks are enough: σ = 6FL/(bh²) and δ = FL³/(3EI), using the bending moduli (PLA 3.3 GPa, PETG 1.9 GPa) and the anisotropy knock-downs from CNC Kitchen, with stiffness checked alongside strength. A sandboxed CalculiX subprocess is the escalation path for parts left with a safety factor under 3. Dynamic checks are optional and immature. Google DeepMind's official `@mujoco/mujoco` npm bindings load MJCF and step a simulation, but they are explicitly "still a WIP" ([MuJoCo wasm README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/wasm/README.md)). They suit a settle-and-tip test of a generated MJCF, not a primary gate.

**Layer five is FDM design-for-manufacture against the P1S.** The printer's slicer profile is the ground truth, not the marketing spec. Bambu Studio's P1S profile allows XY 0–256 mm with an **18 × 28 mm excluded corner** at the front-left origin, and inherits `printable_height` of **250 mm**, not 256 ([P1S profile](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/master/resources/profiles/BBL/machine/Bambu%20Lab%20P1S%200.4%20nozzle.json); [fdm_machine_common](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/master/resources/profiles/BBL/machine/fdm_machine_common.json)). The stock 0.4 mm stainless nozzle handles PLA, PETG, TPU, ABS and ASA. Carbon- and glass-fibre grades need a hardened-steel hotend, which the BOM should list as an upgrade ([Bambu wiki](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/p1/manual/unboxing-p1s)). The numeric rules are well established:

Overhangs, thin walls and bed contact can all be computed from face normals and ray casts with the three-mesh-bvh the app already ships. Tweaker-3 offers evolutionary-trained auto-orientation, but it is GPL-3.0, so it should only be called as a separate process ([Tweaker-3](https://github.com/ChristophSchranz/Tweaker-3)). Every source agrees on one more rule: print a fit gauge first, because no single tolerance number works across printers and filaments ([Printables 608 kit](https://www.printables.com/model/276409-ultimate-608-bearing-with-calibration-kit-v2)).

**Layer six is the slicer itself, which is both the final printability judge and the only exact source of time and filament.** Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer share a CLI:

```
BambuStudio --orient 1 --arrange 1 --load-settings "machine.json;process.json" --load-filaments "filament.json" --slice 0 --outputdir out --export-3mf out.gcode.3mf parts.stl
```

It writes `result.json` with a `return_code`, a per-plate `total_predication` (print time in seconds) and per-filament `total_used_g` ([BambuStudio.cpp](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/master/src/BambuStudio.cpp)). Its return codes map directly to user-facing errors, among them −52 objects partly outside the plate, −102 path in the unprintable area and −104 path outside ([Utils.hpp](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/master/src/libslic3r/Utils.hpp)). The main trap: `--load-settings` needs fully flattened configs. Stock profiles use `inherits`, and when presets fail to apply, the CLI silently reports 0 g and a wrong time ([BambuStudio wiki](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/wiki/Command-Line-Usage); [Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/cli-cannot-apply-external-presets/197842)). Thumbnails are blank headless, and each job uses 200–500 MB of temp files ([Printago](https://printago.io/blog/orca-slicer-cli-reference)). All three major slicers are AGPL-3.0. Calling the user's installed copy as a separate process over files and arguments is the conventional "separate programs" pattern, while bundling a binary brings source-distribution duties ([GNU GPL FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#MereAggregation)). This needs legal review before shipping. Material cost is grams × $/kg ÷ 1000. Bambu's US store lists PLA, PETG HF and ABS at $19.99–22.99 per kg with multi-roll discounts to $13.99, and TPU 95A HF at about $38–42 ([Bambu US store](https://us.store.bambulab.com/pages/bambu-filament); [TPU](https://us.store.bambulab.com/collections/tpu)). A 40 g PLA bracket costs under a dollar, which is why printed parts are a rounding error in real robot budgets.

## Amazon's rules force a catalog-first BOM with browser confirmation

The BOM must be derived from scene parts, never from agent free text. The general hallucination evidence is sobering: about **19.7%** of package names suggested by 16 code LLMs did not exist ([arXiv 2406.10279](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2406.10279v1)), and the XDA test shows the same invention habit applied to physical dimensions. Electronics tools already ground every line in a database. Flux uses retrieval over live supply data ([Flux](https://www.flux.ai/p/blog/design-real-time-supply-chain-visibility-2026)), Digi-Key's ProductDetails endpoint returns real-time price and stock while keyword search can be 24 hours stale ([Digi-Key](https://developer.digikey.com/faq/products-plans-and-apis)), and Nexar/Octopart has a free evaluation tier ([Nexar](https://nexar.com/compare-plans)). The app already restricts the agent to catalog keys through `find_parts` and `add_parts`. The BOM should be a pure function of those keys, hardware spec keys and printed-part grams. No published product or paper generates robot BOMs with verified Amazon links, so this is open territory.

"Confirmed working links" collides with Amazon's rules on three fronts:

- **The official API is gone and the replacement is gated.** PA-API 5's documentation now redirects to a deprecation notice, and calls return HTTP 403 `AccessDenied` ([Creators API deprecation notice](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/paapiv5-deprecation)). Its replacement, the Creators API, keeps GetItems, SearchItems and GetVariations with OffersV2 and adds OAuth 2.0. Secondary sources report that access requires **10 qualifying referred sales in the last 30 days** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/th3nate/amazon-pa-api-v5-is-shutting-down-april-30-2026-here-is-what-changes-at-the-auth-layer-22ek); [Keywordrush](https://www.keywordrush.com/blog/amazon-creator-api-what-changed-and-how-to-switch/)), which almost no hobby user meets.
- **The Associates terms bar installable apps.** They forbid using Associates content or Special Links in "any client-side software application… executable or installable by an end user" other than approved mobile apps. They also forbid client applications from caching API content, and require a timestamp and disclaimer beside any price refreshed less often than hourly ([Participation Requirements](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/gp/associates/help/operating/participation/); [Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)). Amazon enforced this against the Delicious Library macOS app in 2017 ([Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=19354)).
- **Amazon's robots.txt blocks AI agents.** It disallows ClaudeBot, Claude-User, GPTBot and other AI agents from the whole site, and blocks the `/dp/twister-update/` and `/dp/product-availability/` endpoints for everyone ([amazon.com/robots.txt](https://www.amazon.com/robots.txt)). An agent WebFetch of Amazon pages to "verify" a link conflicts with Amazon's stated crawler policy.

The compliant architecture follows from those constraints. Each catalog record stores a child ASIN (never a parent variation) with `verifiedAt` and `verifiedBy` from a maintainer check, plus the maker part number used to match it. The app opens plain, untagged `amazon.com/dp/<ASIN>` links in the system browser, where the user sees live price and stock. An optional "Check prices" action uses the user's own third-party key. Keepa costs about 1 token per ASIN with plans from about €49/month ([Keepa](https://keepa.com/api-docs/plans-tokens.html); [RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/keepa-pricing/)). Rainforest starts at $23/month for 500 credits and openly sells "web-scraped data" ([Traject](https://trajectdata.com/ecommerce/rainforest-api/pricing/)). Canopy reportedly offers 100 free requests a month ([mybesh](https://mybesh.com/tool/canopy-api)). With a user key, the user carries that vendor's terms. Electronics get a Digi-Key or Mouser second source with licensed live data. Link states are Verified, Unavailable (no new offer), Dead (`ItemNotAccessible`), Changed (title or manufacturer info no longer matches the stored part number or spec) and Unchecked, each shown with its date ([Creators API GetItems](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/api-reference/operations/get-items)). Fastener listings need parsing for thread, length, head and pack count, and a "kit" or "assortment" title should count as a mismatch.

The budget model should fix what hand-made robot BOMs get wrong. SO-101's README mixes per-unit servos with per-pack cables at Qty 1, prices the same pack inconsistently between two tables, and states CN totals that don't match their line items ([SO-ARM100](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100)). Open Duck Mini states only "under $400" ([Open_Duck_Mini](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini)). Each BOM line should carry quantity needed, pack count, pack price, packs to buy, leftover, price date and source. The model should consolidate identical fastener specs across the scene before rounding up to packs, and report both cash to spend (whole packs) and cost of what's used. Shipping and tax should be user-set lines, because no API returns tax. Showing a direct or AliExpress tier beside Amazon matters: Berkeley Humanoid Lite costs about 25% less sourced in China ($3,236 vs $4,312) ([R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/berkeley-debuts-5000-open-source-humanoid-built-with-desktop-3d-printers/)). The budget can then act as a design constraint the agent checks while choosing servos, not a report produced afterwards.

## An end-to-end pipeline for the P1S studio

Mapping the evidence onto the app gives a concrete stage order:

1. A requirements table with a budget and target size.
2. A sourced-dimensions table with confidence, required before modeling, to stop invented dimensions.
3. A kinematic skeleton with declared connector frames (servo horn axis, idler axis, mounting-hole pattern), so mirroring and attachment become deterministic transforms, as ArtiCAD showed.
4. Actuator sizing from a torque table.
5. A block-out with catalog parts and assembly examples in the prompt.
6. Detailed CAD with per-part acceptance tests.
7. The full verification stack.
8. Slicing and the BOM.
9. A human sign-off.

| Stage | Evidence it works | App addition |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed actuator kit + assembly examples | Every built LLM robot; ShanghaiTech prompt design | Catalog "kits" per robot class (XL330 / STS3215 / XM430) with idler and bracket examples |
| Connectors declared before parts | ArtiCAD 100% vs 89.2% | Typed ports on catalog parts and `model_part` outputs |
| Per-part acceptance + feature query | CadQueryEval rule; build123d-mcp 0.360 → 0.457 | bbox / volume / b1 / hole-pattern pass-fail in `model_part` |
| Best-of-N on hard parts | CAD-Recode 77.6 → 92.0% IoU | Compile 2–3 variants in the 3-process kernel pool |
| Forced revision rounds | P3D-Bench early-stopping finding | Done only when acceptance tests and checklist pass |
| Joint-sweep interference | SolidWorks motion interference | Sweep each joint with BVH; report usable range |
| Torque / mass / tipping | ToddlerBot, BHL budgets | `verify_design` table with exact vs estimated labels |
| FDM DFM + slicer | Bambu profile, CLI `result.json` | Rules engine + spawn installed Bambu Studio / Orca |
| Grounded BOM | 19.7% hallucination rate; Amazon ToS | BOM from catalog keys, dated ASIN checks, browser links |
| Human gate | Diode EE review; ShanghaiTech hand fixes | Checklist screen before print export |

Fidelity also needs a stated finish standard, because "professional" is mostly the presence of things toys omit. The checklist that encodes it is the same one the agent is judged against: an idler on every joint, fasteners shown, inserts sized correctly, shells over the frame, cable paths and a power board, parts within a 200–220 mm cube for portability even though the P1S takes 256 mm, and a fit gauge shipped with the files. The open-source projects target 200–220 mm precisely so others can reproduce them ([SO-ARM100](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100); [arXiv 2504.17249](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17249)).

## Conclusion

Model capability is no longer the bottleneck. Frontier models now ace simple CAD unit tests, yet they still fail at interfaces, mechanisms and self-verification. The gap between a compiling robot and a buildable one sits in checks that are deterministic, cheap and largely unbuilt in public tools: joint sweeps, torque and tipping budgets, fastener chains, design-for-manufacture rules and slicer return codes. An app that makes these checks first-class tools, and forces the agent to iterate until they pass, would go beyond the published state of the art, where even the best system hand-fixed 8 of 23 failed checks. The research also flips the usual priority. The marginal value of better vision critique is small and noisy. The marginal value of letting the agent ask the geometry precise questions (where are the holes, what is the usable range, what does the slicer say) is large and measured.

Two uncertainties should shape the roadmap. First, nobody has measured hardware outcomes for LLM-designed robots: first-print fit rate, failures per build, or assembly hours. A studio that logs whether the fit gauge, the first assembly and the first motion succeeded would hold the only real dataset in the field. Second, "confirmed working links" cannot be fully automated within Amazon's terms for a desktop app. The defensible promise is links checked by a person on a stated date, rechecked on demand with the user's own key, and confirmed in the user's own browser. Saying exactly that, with dates beside every price, is itself a professional-grade feature that no published robot BOM offers today.
