# Build: BOM, budget, printability and checks

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 8b. Build: BOM, Budget, Printability and Engineering Checks

The Build tab (right panel) and the agent's build tools turn a model into something you can print and buy. Everything here is computed from the scene; nothing comes from agent text. The rules come from docs/research/Printable humanoid engineering (cited there), never from other local projects.

- **Checks** (`src/readiness/`, `workspace/runReadiness.ts`, `useReadiness.ts`, `build/ChecksSection.tsx`): the one answer to “can this be built?”, so nobody has to ask. `runReadiness` waits for component meshes, runs every check, in workers wherever they measure geometry, about 1.5 s after the last change (`projectFingerprint`), and merges them into findings, each a **blocker** or a **note**. The fingerprint covers everything a reading depends on: geometry (hashed once per array, `utils/geometryHash.ts`), each part's material, role, visibility, tag, name, note, weight and print settings, the project's infill, gaits and ranges, saved prices and the currency. A newer run's result always wins over an older one (`newestRuns`). A run that throws or stops at the time limit is a blocker (`unfinished`), never the last verdict left standing, and its fingerprint stays empty so asking again measures again. Status: Ready to build, or how many are left to fix (a block-out while stand-ins remain, for the agent). The Build tab shows it first, and the tab carries a red dot, named “⟨n⟩ to fix” in its tooltip, only while something is left to fix: Ready to build needs no dot (§3b-19). Agent text never changes it.
  - **Blockers:** `check_assembly` chain problems, unheld parts and anything stuck in every order (`isBlockingRank`); engineering FAILs; real clashes and undersized insert holes; parts off the plate or with thin panels; stand-ins and parts with no source; anything printed that's marked clear or glass (the name ends in it, “Front Glass”, or the note opens with it, “Clear acrylic disc”; a note that only mentions glass isn't enough); inserts with under 0.8 mm of wall; a battery with under 10 mm free past both ends for its leads (bare cells have none); screws off every hole in a catalog part or deeper than its `depthMm`; sourced items that aren't referenceable. A row's `items` may each carry their own `status`, so one failing servo doesn't turn its neighbours' warnings into blockers; a row with `itemsAreContext` (Budget) is one finding.
  - **Fasteners into hardware:** standoffs and T-nuts are receivers like nuts, needing about one thread diameter of engagement; a screw into a rail carriage, extrusion, collar or pulley threads into it. A standoff joins whatever the screws on both its ends clamp, so a board on a female-female standoff needs both. A set screw holds its hub when it also touches something past its tip. A head may rest on any hardware. A screw whose driver paths weren't measured reads `not run`, never stuck.
  - **Straight moves and drivers:** moves sample part faces, not only vertices (`gridSamples`), so a low-poly bar is blocked by a bar it crosses. Driver and L-key paths are ray segments (`driverAccess.ts`), so no wall is thin enough to fall between samples.
  - **Notes:** build order, drivers, service depth, enclosures, knife edges, support, WARN rows, unpriced lines, hidden parts, which the viewport checks leave out, Fixed Mates: Onshape mates that couldn't become a joint, read from `robotLink.fixedMates` (`fixedMateFindings`, §8c), and Missing Mates: joints whose Onshape mate was deleted, found by Update from Onshape (`onshape.gone`, `goneMateFindings`; the joint ids join the fingerprint as `goneMates`). They turn a check amber and read when it's expanded; they never affect the verdict.
  - **Cases** (`readiness/caseChecks.ts`, `workspace/caseChecks.ts`, §8h): a robot's Cases are its tests, so every Case in `project.cases` is a row of its own, titled with the Case's name. Each runs 20 times with seeds 1 to 20 (`runCaseTimes`, § Seeded runs in [Cases](cases.md)), driven by the scene's ready Walk when it has one, else the Case's reference. The worker picks the Walk from every `velocity-tracking` record with `readyWalkRecord` (`skills/skillRows.ts`), the rule Drive's `readyVelocity` uses: records fresh for the current model before stale ones, then the newest, and only a fresh one with Arena evidence drives. A row passes at 18 of 20 or better, warns from 10 to 17 and fails below 10; its detail is the count (“17 of 20”) and its one note names the most common failure (the contract's label, capitalized, or Missed). Each row runs the robot's fit first and fails with the first thing that stops it, in order: the Case's slot doesn't fit (`slotRefusal`'s words, with New Case on the note: a `new-case` fix, offered when the robot can try some goal), the model doesn't compile (MuJoCo's error), it falls in 1.5 s on hold (“Fell at 0.4 s”), or a walker of four or more legs with no ready Walk fails the 5-second fall check Drive uses (“Falls on its walk”). The fit is the robot's, not the Case's, so the worker's model job runs it once per model (`checkFit`, `cases/fitCheck.ts`) and every Case's runs job carries its `stop`; the slot is checked per Case in `runSeeds`. A robot whose model won't build is keyed by its parts and joints. Measured in Node on the fixtures, the fit costs 10 to 30 ms for an arm or a rover and 200 to 400 ms for a walker, whose fall check bakes its gait. Tests: `fitCheck.test.ts` runs the worker session on fixtures (the biped on the rover's Reach the Flag, an arm with a zero joint axis, the weak quadruped) and checks each row names the first stop. Rows are notes, never blockers: a Case is the person's own bar, so Ready to build doesn't wait on it, and Ignore or Ignore All Cases clears it like any note. A failing row stays red with only notes in it (`rowStatus`). MuJoCo never runs on the main thread: the main thread sends the parts, joints, measures and triangles to a pool of workers (`cases/caseChecks.worker.ts`, `caseChecksClient.ts`: half the cores, at most four, closed after 30 s idle) and splits the seeds across them (`seedBatches`). Each worker gets one job at a time from its own queue, so the pool can still change what waits: the model job goes first, a newer check drops the queued runs of Cases it no longer needs (`keepOnly`, only from the newest check by `nextCaseTurn`, which `runReadiness` takes before its first await), and a Case already running for an older check is shared, not run twice. A worker keeps the last robot it was sent (`CaseCheckSession`), so the parts and triangles cross to it once per model, not once per Case and batch. A Case whose runs throw (a contract the crate refuses, a worker that dies) fails with “The runs stopped.” and is still a note: `caseCheckRows` never rejects, so one bad Case never takes the other findings or Ready to build with it, and since that verdict isn't kept, the next check tries again. A check a newer one has overtaken queues nothing: it takes a Case's kept or in-flight verdict when the newer check shares it, and otherwise a `not run` row (`replacedRow`) whose agent text says a newer check replaced it, never that it errored. Case names aren't unique, so a Case's finding carries its `caseId`, and a Case row owns only the findings with its id (`ownedBy`); other rows own the findings with their name and no `caseId`. A verdict is kept by the Case's hash, the model's physics and actuator hashes and the Walk skill's hash, so a Case runs again only when the robot or the Case changes; a rename, a price or a print setting reuses it. The worker builds the robot's model to hash it only when the parts, the joints or the Walk records changed since the last run, and the newest check drops every queued run before it queues that model job, so no batch for the old model starts while it builds. A Walk whose skill file the bridge couldn't read isn't remembered, so the next check asks again. Checks land together: `runReadiness` waits for the Case rows with the other findings, so after an edit that changes the model's physics or actuators, every row waits for the Cases' runs; edits that keep those hashes reuse the kept verdicts and don't wait.
    - **Budget:** 20 runs of a gallery Case within 10 s. Measured on one thread in Node (`runCaseTimes`, Apple M-series with other work running): Touch the Dots on the six-joint arm 0.5 s, Reach the Flag on the rover 0.7 s and on the hexapod 4.9 s. The hexapod was 35 s until the legs drive stopped baking its gait on every controller change (`bakeCycles` keeps one bake per setup) and the seeds started sharing one setup (`caseRunner`). In the app the seeds split across the pool, so each worker runs 5 to 10 of them.
  - **Wording:** every finding has two texts. `text` is for the person: one plain sentence, no tool names, no ids, no thresholds they'd have to interpret (`RULES` and `PLAIN_ENGINEERING` in `readiness/wording.ts`). `agentText` keeps the measurements and the instruction, for `get_readiness`, the edit-result delta, follow-ups and Fix. Engineering findings also keep their numbers in `detail`, shown under the sentence. A new check needs a rule there; `ReportLine.code` names it.
  - **Decisions:** only the user clears a blocker, and the project remembers (`project.readiness`). **Ignore** drops one finding (`ignored`, by key); **Ignore All ⟨name⟩** drops a whole kind, like Thin Walls, for parts not made yet too (`ignoredRules`, by rule). Neither comes back when the parts change: it's an opinion, not a measurement. They aren't part of the fingerprint and apply to the last result at once (`withDecisions`), with nothing measured again. Ignored, under the list, restores them. The agent sees what's ignored and leaves it alone; it can only `dispute_finding`, which shows its reason on the row and clears nothing.
  - **What the build needs goes on the list by itself** (`utils/bomNeeds.ts`, `buildBom`): a battery brings a switch, fuse, connector, wire and charger; servos bring cables; sensors, a Pi camera, a Pi, a Pi 5, inserts and screws bring theirs. Each is one pack, unless something on the list already covers it, so they count toward the budget and are never a finding.
  - **Cost:** about 5 s from an edit to a verdict on a 225-part robot, model rebuild included, all but about 100 ms in workers (`perf:agent` phases `get_readiness`, `edit_then_readiness`).
  - **Clashes** (`three/analysis/collide.ts`, `crossing.ts`): two parts clash when their surfaces cross, triangle against triangle through the BVH, or when enough sampled points of one sit inside the other, and the overlap is deeper than the tolerance. Crossing finds what vertices can't: a rod through an uncut plate, crossed bars. Depth comes from probes inside both solids around each crossing. A pair that already overlaps at rest (a pose or a sweep) reports again only once it's over 2 mm deeper than at rest (`DEEPER_THAN_REST_MM`). Calibrated against exact Manifold volumes on saved scenes: every new pair that could be tested was a real overlap.
  - **New check rule:** when a person or an audit finds something the verdict missed, add the check and a seeded fault test in the same change. Don't add a check that cries wolf: a false blocker teaches people and agents to ignore all of them. Calibrate on a real scene (`scratch/`) first.
- **Thin walls** (`thinWallOf`, `printReport.ts`): a *panel* (100 mm² or more of surface under 0.84 mm, both faces counted, or a tenth of the part) must be thickened. A smaller area is a *knife edge*, usually a grazing cut, which the slicer drops: a note. Only panels are issues. Area comes from 4,000 to 20,000 rays spread by surface area (`wallThickness`), so a big triangle counts at its full size; calibrated against a dense ground truth on real scenes, where verdicts only disagree near 100 mm².
- **Project settings** (`ProjectSettings` in `types/robotics.ts`, saved with the scene): printer (a My printers id, a catalog id, or `default`; old `bambu-p1s` maps to the P1S), print quality (`printProcess`), default filament, infill, budget, purchase-only extras, declared joints.
- **BOM** (`utils/bom.ts`): components counted by part (and version), hardware by exact normalized spec (`hardware:type|size|length|finish[|drive]`), parts with `supplyId` as that supply, printed parts as filament grams per material (shell + infill model, Bambu densities) plus estimated supports, then spools with a 15% margin; extras by key. Stand-ins (block-out kinds) and parts tagged CNC, laser-cut or hand-modeled COTS are listed as needing a source.
- **Prices:**
  - **Checked:** `library/purchases.json` (app-wide, keyed by catalog key) holds the price the user saw, with pack size and local date; the listing (link, title, note) is optional. Amazon links are saved as canonical `/dp/ASIN` URLs, other store links as typed. Over 60 days old is Stale. The app never fetches Amazon, and the agent never states a price that isn't in the BOM.
  - **On hand** (`PurchaseRecord.onHand`, `utils/purchases.ts`): the same store holds how many units of each key the person owns, beside the price or alone (`{ onHand: 6 }` for a part owned without a checked price); old records load unchanged. There's no second inventory file. `electron/purchaseRecord.mjs` checks each saved record on desktop and web alike: a count is a whole number from 0 to 100,000, 0 clears it, and a record left with neither a price nor a count is deleted. Each BOM line splits into `onHand` and `toBuy` (`ownedSplit`), and packs, the line price, the totals, the Budget check, Buy All's cart and the agent's `get_bom` count only the units to buy. A fully owned line stays in its section at $0 and never counts as unpriced. Saving or deleting a price keeps the count (`withPrice`), and setting a count keeps the price (`withOnHand`).
  - **Currency** (`utils/currency.ts`, `currencyStore.ts`, `useMoney`): every amount is stored and computed in US dollars and converted only when shown, so the BOM, checks, budget and agent tools never mix currencies. The 30 ECB currencies; rates come from Frankfurter (`RATES_SOURCE`) once a day at startup, else the bundled `BUNDLED_RATES` (`data/exchangeRates.ts`; refresh them when you touch it). A price or budget typed in another currency keeps what was typed (`PurchaseRecord.entered`, `ProjectSettings.budgetEntered`) beside its dollar value, so it reads back exactly. Components format with `useMoney()` (never a module-level helper, or the React Compiler caches the old currency); plain text for the agent uses `formatMoney` / `formatWhole`, adds `currencyNote` when it isn't USD, and `set_build` takes `budget` in the user's currency. Checks rerun when the currency or rates change. Amazon links stay on amazon.com.
  - **Estimated:** `data/listPrices.ts` holds list prices from maker and US distributor stores (never Amazon), each with store, URL and date. Shown as "≈ $" and counted only in the estimated total.
- **BOM sections:** Components, Hardware, Added by Agent, Supplies, Materials, Filament, Equipment. Equipment (the Tools supply group and sourced equipment) stays out of the robot's cost.
- **Sourced items** (`data/sourcedItems.ts`, `electron/backend/sourced.mjs`, §8a): what the agent found outside the catalog. Prices are estimates; only the user's Add Price makes one checked. **The finished robot uses only referenceable parts**: a confirmed link, a source for the numbers it relies on, and real geometry if it takes up space (`unreferencedReason`). Anything less is a blocker.
- **Supplies** (`data/supplies.ts`): generic bought goods (wiring, chargers, filament, tools, and the generic batteries and buck converter older scenes use) with an Amazon search for the spec. Batteries and regulators with exact shapes are Power components (§8a).
- **Printability** (`three/printability.ts`, `utils/printReport.ts`): each printed part in its own frame tries the 6 axis orientations plus up to 6 large flat faces it can rest on (or the face the user picked); the scene's printer bed and keep-outs come from the printer catalog (§8f), long parts across the diagonal; overhangs past 45°, support volume under faces within 30° of horizontal (the stock profile's threshold, Morgan 2016 drop method); walls under 0.84 mm by inward rays; time from extruded volume; MaxRects-packed plates (§8f). Labelled estimates until the plates are sliced.
- **Engineering checks** (`utils/engineering.ts`), each Pass / Warn / Fail / Not run with a one-line summary (`detail`) and, when something's wrong, one line per problem (`items`, each selecting its parts). A pass's detail carries a measurement or a name (“Worst: Knee, 0.35 of stall holding”); a pass with nothing to add has none, never a sentence that restates the tick (“Every servo has a measured model”), so its row doesn't open. Name joints and parts as people read them (“Left shoulder roll: clear to −75° of −100°; at −100° the left hand meets the hip pitch left”), never by id; summarize repetitive results (“Hip l, Knee l, Ankle l and 3 more”, `listIds`) instead of listing them:
  - **Which parts are the robot** (`engineeringBody.ts`): each robot from `buildRig`, plus any solid within 50 mm of it (box to box, repeated, so an unnamed battery or torso still counts). The wide margin is deliberate: dropping a real part hides load, counting a neighbour only overstates it. Anything further away, and every `physics.dynamic` object, is scenery: left out of mass, leg loads and footprint, and named in the Weight row. Each robot has its own mass, centre of mass, legs and feet, so a second robot or a table changes nothing. A leg joint is `limb: 'leg'`, or no limb and a leg word in its name (`isLegJoint`); `limb: 'other'` stays out. Feet of a legged robot are the parts within 2 mm of its own lowest point, so soles a few mm above the floor don't switch off leg loads; other robots and scenes use the floor itself. Legs with no foot at the lowest point warn in Servo torque.
  - Weight: total mass and centre of mass from printed mass, catalog weights, fastener volume × finish density, supply weights; estimates flagged.
  - Servo torque (`actuatorRules.ts`, `engineeringCrouch.ts`): stall and rated torque at the servo supply voltage (a regulator's output, else the battery's nominal), interpolated over the datasheet's voltage points and scaled down below the lowest, never rated at more than the supply can reach. Leg pitch joints (the largest set of parallel, level axes in a leg, whichever way the robot faces; a knee settles a tie) carry the whole robot on one leg (two for 3+ legs, for stance as well as crouch) at the gait's crouch, at least 30°: τ = m·g·lever·sin(θ/2). **Stance** PASS ≤ 0.40 of stall, WARN to 0.60, FAIL above; **Heat** 0.67 × τ ÷ rated PASS ≤ 1.0, WARN to 1.6, FAIL above. Other joints hold their limb across the range: PASS ≤ 0.40 of stall and ≤ rated, FAIL above 0.60. Rated falls back to ¼ of stall. A joint's rating sums the catalog actuators on it and skips the rest, as the MJCF writer does (`jointServos`). Joints with neither a catalog actuator nor a published limit aren't graded here (all of them: Not run); Torque estimates names them. **Peak** is Not run until a trained walk policy is simulated with force limits at 0.7 × stall. The thresholds come from robots that walk (docs/research/Agent harness/actuator_sizing.md): no walker meets the old 1/5-of-stall rule at the knee. Actuator numbers per version in `ComponentInfo.actuator` / `variants`, with sources.
  - Peak torque (`engineeringPeak.ts`, `agentPeak.ts`, `utils/peakTorque.ts`): `verify_design` with `peak: true` walks the latest walk policy in MuJoCo for 14 s with two shoves of 0.3 × weight, every force limit at 0.7 × stall at the supply voltage. Per leg joint, whichever load grades it in Servo torque: the 99th percentile (25 ms windows) of the torque the joint *asks for* (the position servo's command before its force limit; the delivered force can never pass the limit) over stall. PASS ≤ 0.8, WARN ≤ 1.0, FAIL above stall for more than 50 ms or a fall. Speed counts too (`engineeringEnvelope.ts`): the recorder keeps each window's mean signed torque demand and joint speed (`actuator_velocity`, `jointSpeeds` in `sim/simForces.ts`), stored per joint for every second window after the first second as `WalkJoint.samples`, `[rpm, N·m]` pairs (about 260 a joint, so the saved scene stays small). A servo's envelope is the straight line from stall at 0 rpm to its no-load rpm at the supply voltage (`ratingAt`, which reads `rpm` from the catalog's points; a joint with several servos adds their stall and takes the slowest rpm, and has no rpm unless every servo on it has one). A window falls outside it when the servo is driving (torque and speed share a sign) and asks for more than the line allows at that speed (`outsideEnvelope`); braking windows don't count, since a back-driven servo isn't held to its motoring line. More than 5% of a joint's windows outside makes it WARN, and its item names the joint and the share, so the agent's `check` output does too. A joint with samples, an rpm and a catalog servo carries a `JointEnvelope` on the row (`CheckRow.envelopes`, passed to `CheckSummary`); the item that names a joint carries its id (`joint`, passed on to its `Finding`), so opening Peak torque plots a flagged joint's windows under its own item, and lists the other joints as rows (`DisclosureRow`) that open to the same plot (`build/EnvelopeRows.tsx`), each joint once. The plot is `TracePlot`'s points mode: driving windows light, braking ones grey, the servo's line in the accent, its rated torque dashed, and the next servo up (`servoUp` in `actuatorSizing.ts`: the weakest servo on the same bus that suits the voltage, has an rpm and beats the joint's stall by over 5%, the margin `pickFor` uses for `up`) as a grey line, each named under the plot. The walk's RMS replaces 0.67 × stance in Heat, and its measured current feeds Current and runtime. Not run without a walk policy. A walk that ran is stored on the project (`project.walk`, `storedWalk.ts`) under a key of the parts, joints and latest walk policy (`walkKey`); the live Checks pass it to the engineering run while the key still matches (`freshWalk`), so Peak torque, Heat and Current and runtime keep the measured numbers until the design changes, and fall back to their estimates after. Building the MuJoCo model still runs on the main thread (about 0.5 s the first time, hulls cached per part after, `mjcfHull.ts`), as the live sim does.
  - Torque estimates and Servo models (`engineeringSimModel.ts`): what the simulation has to guess, as notes. Torque estimates names the movable joints with no catalog actuator and no published limit, whose force limit the MJCF writer estimates from the weight they hold (§8d), and selects their child parts. An estimated stand-in servo (a block-out with no actuator spec) counts as no servo there, as it does for the MJCF writer, so its joint reads “No servo on …”. Servo models names the Servos-group parts on joints without a Measured calibration (`measuredJoints`, §8k: the joint's active calibration is measured and fits its servo), whose response comes from the catalog preset or an estimate, and selects them. A catalog fit (STS3215 C001, XL330) is a published model of another unit, so its joints count until they're calibrated. When any of them can be calibrated (`calibratable` in `src/reality/servoPart.ts`: a catalog servo on a Feetech or Dynamixel bus, so not a PWM servo), the finding carries a `calibrate` fix with those joints: Fix selects their servos, opens Operate and, when the robot is connected from this scene in Test, calibrates those joints alone (`calibrateJoints`); without one, Fix asks the agent as for any note. A rollback to the preset brings the joint back into the row, and the seeded fault (`engineeringSimModel.test.ts`) is a six-joint biped with one joint rolled back. It grades only revolute joints that aren't wheels: the simulation never applies a measured model to wheels or slides, and gearmotors and steppers have none, so those are left out rather than warned about. Each is one finding, never one per joint, naming at most three joints and three servos and counting the rest; every part stays selected. Neither runs without movable joints, and Servo models not without a servo it grades. They replace the estimate notes the Simulate tab used to carry.
  - Payload (`engineeringPayload.ts`, `payload.ts`): how much a fixed-base arm's hand holds (`classifyRobot`, `sim/morphology.ts`; robots on legs or wheels get no row). The chain runs from the base to the deepest joint whose name isn't a gripper's (grip, jaw, finger, claw, thumb), and the hand is the far end of what that joint carries. Holding torque is linear in the load, τ = τ₀ + m·τ₁ (`holdingTorque.ts`, which the skills' feasibility checks share), so each of 4,096 poses sampled inside the joint ranges (fixed seed, hand above the floor) lifts the least over the chain's joints of (0.60 × stall at the supply voltage − τ₀) ÷ τ₁; a published joint's `effortNm` stands in for stall, and a chain joint with neither leaves the row out. The detail is the least at full reach (past 90% of the furthest the hand gets, measured level from the first joint) and close in (within half of it). It's always a pass: information, never a finding. The samples run in the analysis workers with the sweeps (`payloadMapsIn`); without a viewport they run inline.
  - Budget (`engineeringBudget.ts`): the BOM estimate of the units to buy (checked plus list prices, and support filament when `supportG` is passed in; the row says when it isn't) against the budget. FAIL over, WARN above 90%.
  - Range changes (`engineeringRanges.ts`, WARN): a joint narrower than its first definition (`project.firstRanges`, set once by `define_joints`), with the old value. The agent can't clear it.
  - Servo alignment: each servo's output on its joint axis, the output read from the servo mesh's top cap; any other declared `output` port also counts (`output-b` of a 2XL430). Motors use their declared output port. An actuator with no known output is Not run, or named in the summary, never a pass.
  - Joint support: each joint held on both sides by a deep-groove or flanged bearing on the axis (a thrust bearing takes no bending load), a second servo, or a link that reaches past both ends of a dual-shaft servo. The link is on the output side: a servo riding the child is straddled by the parent, and never by itself.
  - Range of motion: the distal parts swept through the range against the rest and the floor, with mesh interference, at every 15° inside the range plus both ends (30° for published joints, §0f); reports how far each joint end is clear. A clash thinner than the step can fall between samples. Slides (`prismatic`) can't be swept, and the row says how many it skipped. Fails for designed joints. Published robots' joints (`RobotJoint.published`) only warn: their limits are the motor's, reached with other joints moved, and contact under 5 mm is links meeting at a hard stop.
    - A servo's output turns with its joint: its horns, spline and output gear (the mesh bodies centred on its output line, `three/analysis/rotorSplit.ts`) rotate with the link they drive, so screws seated in a horn never “hit” their own servo. Pose and animation checks test the parts riding an output against the servo's case alone (`outputRiders.ts`). Meshes whose horn shares vertices with the case (DS3218, XM430, XM540, the XL430's front horn) can't be split and fall back to one rigid body.
  - Published robots: link masses (`ScenePart.massG`) and centres of mass (`ScenePart.com`, in the part's frame), both from `<inertial>`, count as known weights, and each joint's torque limit (`effortNm`: URDF `effort`, MJCF `actuatorfrcrange` / actuator `forcerange`) stands in for servo data: Pass ≤ half of it, Warn ≤ it. Servo alignment and joint support only run when a joint has servos.
  - Battery (`utils/powerSources.ts`): Power packs in the scene, else a holder with matching cells (series multiplies volts, parallel multiplies capacity), else a lone cell, plus any battery supply (older scenes). With several, the checks use the one that suits the actuators best (`servoSource`), so part order never changes a verdict. Every component with an `actuator` counts, motors as well as servos. The parts decide the power source, never a setting: a battery in the build means it runs on the battery; a build with actuators and no battery runs on a tether (`sceneTether`). The tether's volts and amps are `project.tether` (from the fields in the power check rows or `set_build`), else `suggestedTether`; a stored tether is ignored while a battery is in the build. On a tether, Servo voltage and Regulator input read its voltage, and Supply current (in place of Current and runtime) FAILs when the estimated peak passes its amps. A tether puts a DC Power Supply, fuse, switch, XT60 leads and wire on the BOM (`bomNeeds.ts`).
  - Servo voltage: battery full-charge voltage against each servo's listed range (an STS3215 C001 shuts down above 7.4 V). FAIL when it can't reach the minimum or passes the maximum; WARN when it sits under the minimum at nominal. When it's out of range but a regulator in the scene outputs inside every servo's range (and takes the battery as input), it passes and names the regulator to wire them to; with several, the highest voltage. Current and runtime: pack current against the battery's C rating, plus fuse (1.4 × peak) and runtime (0.8 × capacity ÷ average). Its source is the simulated walk when one exists for the current model and the robot stayed up (`walkedCurrent` in `engineeringPower.ts`): average and peak servo amps from the walk (`WalkSim.power`, §8d), plus idle for servos the walk didn't drive, and the joint with the highest average is named in the detail and as the row's item (context, so a FAIL still reads the detail). Without one it estimates (`servoCurrent`): leg servos at 0.45 × rated amps on average and rated at peak, the rest idle (twice idle at peak), and says “(estimate)”. Either way, servo amps behind a regulator are converted to pack amps (Vout ÷ (Vbatt × 0.9)), compute boards add their watts, and the row WARNs when servo amps pass the regulator's `ioutA`.
  - Regulator input: with regulators in the scene, the battery's nominal and full-charge voltage against each regulator's input range.
  - Standing stability (`engineeringStability.ts`): each robot's centre of mass over the outline of its feet, with tipping angle; the worst robot reports. A foot touches where its mesh does: the workers measure each part's `base`, the outline of its vertices within 1 mm of its lowest point (`shapeReports.ts`), so a ball touches at a point, an unjointed wheel along a strip across its tread and a tilted foot along its low edge. A wheel (a round part turning about a level axle through its centre) touches on a line under the axle, and two with no third contact WARN that it must balance itself. Only measures without a `base` fall back to the part's box.
  - Heat near servos (`engineeringHeat.ts`): PLA whose mesh comes within 1 mm of an actuator's mesh (PLA softens at 54 °C; servos run at 60–70 °C). `engineeringInputs` sends the printed parts whose boxes come that close to the workers (`meshGaps`, `analysis/gaps.ts`: a BVH-to-BVH closest distance), and the row passes `servoTouches` on. Without a viewport it falls back to boxes, reading a part whose box stands more than 10 mm clear on every side as a shell around the servo.
  - Strength (`engineeringStrength.ts`, solver in `src/strength/`): every printed part (PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA) on the load path from a servo's horn to the next joint: the parts the horn screws hold (screws parallel to the output and within 12 mm of it), then up to three hops along screws shared with other printed parts, never through a component. A part counts only when screws also tie it to something past it; an end link (a foot, a gripper finger) has nothing to carry. The load is the joint's torque: the larger of Servo torque's load (crouch or stance for leg joints, hold for the rest) and the walk's p99 once Peak torque has run, split between a joint's servos. In an analysis worker (`strength` task, `three/analysis/strengthPart.ts`) the part is voxelised in its own frame at about 1.5 mm (coarser past 9,000 cells), clamped at its horn screws (the aluminium horn holds it) and loaded at the screws that carry the next link with forces at right angles to the joint axis that add up to the torque (`strength/loads.ts`), then solved as linear elasticity on 8-node bricks with incompatible modes (exact in pure bending), assembled into 3×3 blocks, by block-Jacobi preconditioned conjugate gradients. Stiffness and strength are transversely isotropic about the layer normal, which is the chosen print face's up direction (`print.down`, else Printing's pick): PLA 50 MPa along the layers, 25 across, 25 in shear between them; PETG 45, 30, 28; ABS 36, 20, 20; ASA 38, 22, 22 (`LAYERED` in `strength/material.ts`, typical published FDM values). Infill and walls (the stock profiles' 2 walls of 0.42 mm lines, at least 0.8 mm of skin) scale each cell's stiffness by how much of it is solid; stress in a surface cell counts the walls at their bending lever. Stress is read at nodes, averaged over the cells that share them, with a quadratic criterion: in-plane von Mises over the in-layer strength, tension across the layers over the across strength, shear between layers over the shear strength. Two cells (3 mm) around every loaded screw and the nodes on sharp inside edges aren't read: the voxel model can't resolve how a screw head or horn spreads its load, and a voxel corner's peak depends only on the cell size. The factor is 1 ÷ √(the worst index): PASS ≥ 2, WARN ≥ 1.3, FAIL below; the row is a verdict, so a FAIL is a blocker. The row's detail and each item are numbers only (“L Bracket: 1.2×, 0.77 N·m (Shoulder pitch)”); a finding has no sentence of its own. Each candidate print face (Printing's six axes and up to six flat faces) is graded on the same stress field; when another face beats the chosen one by over 10%, the finding carries it (`Finding.fix`) and **Fix** sets that face (`print.down`) directly, with no agent. Opening the row paints each solved part's field on the canvas (`three/viewport/stressOverlay.ts`, one instanced box per cell, from blue through amber at 2 to red at 1.3), following the part's frame, and nothing else rebuilds; closing it or leaving the tab clears it (`strength/fields.ts`). The worker caches readings by shape, frame and load, so an edit elsewhere doesn't solve the part again.
    - **Calibration** (`strength/calibration.test.ts`, scenes built in code with real screws and an STS3215, at 0.4 × stall, the most torque Servo torque passes): quadruped thigh plate (4 mm) 23.4, quadruped hip U-bracket (3 mm) 3.8, SO-101 upper arm (hollow box, 2.5 mm walls, C018) 34.3, SO-101 shoulder U-bracket (4 mm, C018) 3.9, each at the face Printing picks. The seeded fault (`strength/fixtures/lBracket.ts`, `engineeringStrength.test.ts`): a 3 mm L-bracket whose wall stands 40 mm from the horn plate, under 0.77 N·m, is 1.27 with the horn plate on the bed (layers across the bend, FAIL), 2.09 on its side (PASS), and 10.9 at 10 mm. A cantilever matches beam theory within 2% at a third, half and two thirds of its span (`strength/solve.test.ts`). Solve time: 0.2 s for the bracket, 0.9 s for the upper arm (§0f).
- **Joints** (`RobotJoint`): parent and child parts, actuators, range, limb. With an actuator, the pivot and axis come from the servo's real output. Downstream parts follow from the joint tree.
- **Library joint modules** (`vendor/robotics/robotics.scad`): `STS3215` follows the Feetech drawing: axis 12.5 mm from the case center, Ø19.95 aluminium horns with 4 × M3 on Ø14 on both faces, 1.4 mm beyond the case. `XL330` follows the ROBOTIS mesh in the catalog: case 34 × 20 × 23 mm, axis 9.5 mm from the case end, a Ø16 horn 3 mm proud with 4 × M2 on Ø12 and a rear boss 3 mm proud; its joint modules take `m = 2` (`servo_link` and `servo_base` pass `m` to the U-link and cradle). A cradle's bridge reaches at least 3 mm past its cable slot, so short cases like the XL330 keep no sliver beside it. `servo_u_bracket` bolts to both horns. `heatset_hole` sizes include the ~0.25 mm printed-hole shrinkage and 1 mm extra depth. Whether a printer actually hits them is what Print Fit Test checks: it plates one coupon per kind of fit (each insert, servo pocket, bearing seat and screw size), cut from the design's own parts (§8f).
- **UI:** the tab runs in the order a person asks: is it ready (Checks), what to buy (Bill of materials), how to build it (Build guide), then the plates (Printing). Four sections at most, each saying one thing; a control lives in the row it changes (the budget beside the total, the supply beside the checks that read it), never in a section of its own.
  - **Checks:** first, live, with no button. Every check is listed with a green check, amber warning or red error, worst first, each collapsed behind a chevron with its count, so the list stays short. On the first run each shows a spinner; after that the last result stays until the new one lands, with Checking… in the header in place of the verdict. An empty scene has no section. Expanding a check (`CheckItem`) shows its one-line result, then its blockers and notes. The result is left out (`shownDetail`) when a finding says it word for word, or when the row sets `detailInItems` because its result is only a count or a figure its items list (“1 joint without a servo”, Strength’s “1.2×”); a result that adds a fact stays, such as Peak torque’s fall or Servo torque’s missing foot. Each finding shows the sentence, the numbers if it has any, links to the parts (`PartLinks`: same-named parts collapse to “6 × M3 Screw”, a click selects them, hover previews them), Fix and a ⋯ menu with Ignore, Ignore All ⟨name⟩ and Quote. On a note, Fix and ⋯ float over the row on hover.
    - **Payload on the canvas:** expanding Payload draws its samples where the hand gets to, grey to accent by grams (`CheckRow.dots`, `CheckDots`, §12); collapsing the row or leaving Build clears them (`showDots` in `useReadiness`).
    - **Supply** (`TetherFields`, `tetherRows.ts`): while the build runs on a tether (actuators, no battery), the power rows that read it carry its fields when expanded, as their fix: Servo voltage and Regulator input the volts (V), Supply current the amps (A). The fields start at `suggestedTether` (the highest of 48, 24, 12, 7.4, 6 and 5 V inside the actuators' common range, 10 A). There's no Battery / Tether switch: adding a battery to the scene is the switch.
    - **Case rows** (`CaseCheckItem`) show the count on the row itself and their note under it, with no chevron; clicking the row opens the Case in Simulate (`onOpenCase`: `cases.open`, then the Simulate tab). The open Case's header in Simulate shows the row's status icon and opens the row (§8h). A misfit's note offers New Case in place of Fix.
    - **Fix** sends `fixPrompt` to the current agent chat and opens it (queued if the agent is busy). A finding that carries its own fix (`Finding.fix`: Strength's `print-face`, Servo models' `calibrate`) applies it instead, with no agent, through `onApplyFix`'s handler for its kind; Fix shows only when that kind has a handler. The print face applies in the web demo too; the web demo has no Operate, so it has no `calibrate` handler. **Quote** opens the agent with the problem in a quote card above the composer for the person to add to (`findingQuote`, `tool.requestQuote`; the card is in [Agent](agent.md)). The web demo has neither.
    - **Ignored**, collapsed under the list, restores what's ignored (`IgnoredChecks`). Settings → Build → Checks → Have the agent fix new blockers.
  - **Bill of materials** (`BuyList`, `BuyGroup`, `BuyRow`): built like Checks. Each BOM section is one row, collapsed behind a chevron, with its line count and subtotal (≈ unless every line has a checked price), so the list stays short; the only section opens by itself. An amber warning on the row says a line inside has a warning or a stale price. Equipment's subtotal is left out of the budget, and its tooltip says so.
    - **Total and budget** (`BuyTotal`, `budget.ts`), first in the section: the estimated total (checked prices, plus list prices for the rest, marked ≈) with the budget field beside it, then a bar (solid for checked prices, light for the estimate) with “$40 left” or “$12 over budget” against the estimate. No price counts (“3 to price”, “2 of 5 checked”): the bar and the ≈ already say how much is estimated. No stale-price notice either: a stale price is amber in the lines, its section row carries the warning icon, and the price's tooltip gives the date. An empty list shows the $0 total and the budget field, so a budget can be set before the first part.
    - **Lines:** quantity first in its own column (spools for filament, with the grams below), then name, part number or spec (wrapping, never truncated) and packs, then the price. Prices line up under the subtotals: checked in white, ≈ list price muted, stale in amber, otherwise a quiet Add Price. All three open the Add Price sheet (`CheckSheet`, a `Modal`: price first, typed freely and shown as `$1,200.50`, Enter saves; the listing fields fold under Listing; a saved price's Delete Price asks first, like every delete, and the line falls back to its list price or Add Price). Open listing (Search Amazon when there's no product link) floats in the right gutter on hover, under the chevron column, so it covers nothing, and sits at the right of the sheet's header, so checking a price never means unfolding Listing first.     - **Parts:** a line with parts in the scene selects them all on click and previews one of them on hover, never the whole set; filament has no preview, since it isn't one part.

Filament isn't offered: it comes from the printed parts, in grams. On every line counted in pieces the quantity is a button (`QtyEditor`) showing the units left to buy; it opens On Hand, plus Quantity and Delete (which asks first) on a line you added. Filament lines added by older scenes get Delete only. A count on hand shows beside the line's spec (“6 on hand”). The agent's note on an extra shows beside the spec.

    - **Buy All** (`cart.ts`, `bomCart`) fills one Amazon cart from the list: `/gp/aws/cart/add.html` with numbered `ASIN.n` / `Quantity.n` pairs, in line order, for every line with an ASIN (a checked listing's, else a catalog or sourced product link on amazon.com). Quantity is packs of the units to buy, rounded up, and a fully owned line stays out: the checked listing's pack size, else one piece (a 1,000 g spool for filament), so 6 servos sold in pairs are 3 and 13 screws sold by the hundred are 1. A line with no ASIN goes to its store instead: a checked or sourced listing elsewhere, else the list price's store page, grouped under the store's host name below Buy All, one item per line, since stores take no shared cart link. Only https links count, an ASIN must pass `parseAsin`, and the cart is always built on amazon.com, so a forged ASIN or a lookalike host never reaches it (§0h). Lines left with no link keep their row link (an Amazon search). Supplies join the cart only when they have a product link (the ones the agent sources); a catalog supply's search stays a search. Buy All is in ⌘K too (listed while the cart has an ASIN). It opens through `openLink` (`utils/openLink.ts`), the app's one external-link path: the system browser on desktop, a new tab in the web demo. No account and no affiliate tag.
  - **Build guide** (`GuideSection`, `src/guide/`), after the bill of materials: a step-by-step assembly PDF, made with Make Guide (off until the scene has built parts). Making one runs in a worker (`guide.worker.ts`; `generateGuide` packs the parts, the worker measures contacts, plans, draws and writes the HTML, the main thread saves the PDF), so the app stays responsive and you can keep working; a toast with Open says when it's done. While it runs, the card keeps its cover (the new cover replaces it as soon as it's drawn), shows the step being drawn, the time left once it can be estimated, and Stop. A made guide offers Open; an out-of-date one says so in amber and adds Update. There's no Regenerate: a guide reads Out of date whenever a redraw could come back different. That's when its fingerprint changes (`guideFingerprint`: the title, material, infill as printed on the front pages, built parts, joints and `GUIDE_VERSION`), or when its order search stopped at the 20 s limit (`orderTimedOut` on the record), so a later run may check the steps that say “Order not checked”. Bump `GUIDE_VERSION` in any change to the planner, the drawings or item names that changes what a made guide shows, so every guide redraws. Every scene with built parts keeps a current guide (`useGuideAutoUpdate`, Settings → Build → Build guide → Update automatically, on by default): 15 s after the guide goes out of date, while no agent in the scene is working, it makes or updates the guide quietly, with no toast on success or failure. Background runs go one at a time across tabs. Stop, a failed run or another timed-out order search leaves that version alone until the scene changes again; opening another scene in the tab cancels a run. Steps follow the order `check_assembly` proves (`provenOrder.ts`); parts the planner couldn't place come first by the heuristic, and their steps say “Order not checked”.
    - **Open** (the cover, the Open button, the toast, the agent's guide link) shows the guide in the app (`GuideViewer`), not the system PDF viewer. The HTML the PDF is printed from is kept beside it (`library/guides/<id>.html`, read with `guide:html`), parsed into pages, each in its own shadow root with one shared stylesheet (`guide/viewer/`), and prefetched at idle. A guide with no saved HTML is regenerated, then opened; Open PDF and Show in Finder regenerate a PDF that was moved or deleted the same way (`withPdf`, which checks `guide:exists` first), so no error sends anyone to a button; if the system still can't open or show the PDF, a toast says so. The cover thumbnail flies into the first page on a spring (`springEasing.ts` turns a spring into a CSS `linear()` easing), keeping the thumbnail's tilt, while the next pages rise in behind it; closing flies the page nearest the centre back into the thumbnail. Pages fit the window height; pinch, ⌘-scroll or − / + / 0 zoom about the pointer, ← / → and the toolbar's arrows turn pages, Esc or the × at the top right closes. The toolbar floats at the bottom: Previous page, “3 of 60”, Next page, Zoom out, Zoom in, Build Along, Show in Finder, Open PDF.
    - **Build Along** (`useBuildAlong`, `guide/buildAlong.ts`, `build/BuildAlongBar.tsx`) plays the guide on the canvas one step at a time. It opens from the viewer's toolbar and ⌘K (listed while the scene has built parts). It reads the plan the PDF was drawn from: `useBuildGuide` keeps the last generated `GuidePlan` with its `guideFingerprint`, and `currentPlan` regenerates first when the scene has changed since (the bar shows the run's progress meanwhile). There is no second planner. `buildAlongSteps` turns each step into what the canvas shows: every part placed so far plus the step's context (so the shown set only grows and ends at every built part), the step's moves and arrows, its callout counts with the PDF's item names (“4 × M3 × 6 Socket Head Screw”), and a label on each servo the step adds, with its joint's name and bus ID from `servoLayout` (“Shoulder lift, ID 2”), the numbering the joint map and Operate's Connect use. ← / → step, orbit stays free, Esc closes and puts the camera back where it was; parts, visibility and selection are never edited. While it's open the workspace's other keys are off.
    - Regenerating is incremental: the worker keeps the guide's renderer for 2 minutes after a run (at most 2), syncing only parts whose identity changed, and caches contacts and every drawing (cover, item thumbnails, modules, steps) by the identities of the parts it shows (`GuideCache`, keys sent from the main thread's `WeakMap`). An unchanged scene regenerates in about 2 s; an edit redraws only the steps that show the changed parts.
