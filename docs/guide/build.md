# Build: BOM, budget, printability and checks

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 8b. Build: BOM, Budget, Printability and Engineering Checks

Sheet cap boundaries and closed-solid validation use `electron/cad-kernel/pairKey.mjs` for undirected edge keys. Both retain lexical endpoint order and the existing separator, without sorting temporary pairs (§0f).

The Build tab (right panel) and the agent's build tools turn a model into something you can print and buy. Everything here is computed from the scene; nothing comes from agent text. The rules come from docs/research/Printable humanoid engineering (cited there), never from other local projects.

- **Checks** (`src/readiness/`, `workspace/runReadiness.ts`, `useReadiness.ts`, `build/ChecksSection.tsx`): the one answer to “can this be built?”, so nobody has to ask. `runReadiness` waits for component meshes, runs every check, in workers wherever they measure geometry, about 1.5 s after the last change (`projectFingerprint`), and merges them into findings, each a **blocker** or a **note**. The fingerprint covers everything a reading depends on: geometry (hashed once per array, `utils/geometryHash.ts`), each part's material, sheet setting and affine transform, role, visibility, tag, name, note, weight and print settings, the project's infill, gaits and ranges, saved prices and the currency. A newer run's result always wins over an older one (`newestRuns`). A run that throws or stops at the time limit is a blocker (`unfinished`), never the last verdict left standing, and its fingerprint stays empty so asking again measures again. Status: Ready to build, or how many are left to fix (a block-out while stand-ins remain, for the agent). The Build tab shows it first, and the tab carries a red dot, named “⟨n⟩ to fix” in its tooltip, only while something is left to fix: Ready to build needs no dot (§3b-19). Agent text never changes it.
  - **Planar outlines:** Build support polygons and print contact outlines share `utils/convexHull.ts` with skill support checks, silhouettes and filmstrips. The hull keeps counterclockwise boundary points and removes collinear interior points without changing the input.
  - **Blockers:** `check_assembly` chain problems, unheld parts and anything stuck in every order (`isBlockingRank`); engineering FAILs; real clashes and undersized insert holes; parts off the plate or with thin panels; stand-ins and parts with no source; anything printed that's marked clear or glass (the name ends in it, “Front Glass”, or the note opens with it, “Clear acrylic disc”; a note that only mentions glass isn't enough); inserts with under 0.8 mm of wall; a battery with under 10 mm free past both ends for its leads (bare cells have none); screws off every hole in a catalog part or deeper than its `depthMm`; sourced items that aren't referenceable. A row's `items` may each carry their own `status`, so one failing servo doesn't turn its neighbours' warnings into blockers; a row with `itemsAreContext` (Budget) is one finding.
  - **Fasteners into hardware:** standoffs and T-nuts are receivers like nuts, needing about one thread diameter of engagement; a screw into a rail carriage, extrusion, collar or pulley threads into it. A standoff joins whatever the screws on both its ends clamp, so a board on a female-female standoff needs both. A set screw holds its hub when it also touches something past its tip. A head may rest on any hardware. A screw whose driver paths weren't measured reads `not run`, never stuck.
  - **Straight moves and drivers:** moves sample part faces, not only vertices (`gridSamples`), so a low-poly bar is blocked by a bar it crosses. Grid-cell selection uses scalar coordinates and squared distances, without temporary coordinate arrays or vector-component calls per point. Triangle-interior weights and selected output coordinates also use scalar writes. Straight moves and enclosure split planes share this sampler, retaining first-point ties, source order and per-solid resolution caches. Each move query reuses its offset origin and prepares grazing offsets only after the first central hit in each direction. Local removal, pocket capture and service-exit probes share this workspace, which ends with the query. Driver and L-key paths are ray segments (`driverAccess.ts`), so no wall is thin enough to fall between samples.
  - **Containment probes:** `assembly/probe.ts` keeps its world and local bounds checks, then uses collision analysis's shared ray voting with the assembly directions. Two agreeing votes end a three-ray probe. Driver access, move blockers, split tests and fastener seating share this path (§0f).
  - **Battery lead clearance:** `assembly/fitProbes.ts` uses bounded nearest-hit BVH rays from both ends of the pack's longest bounds axis. Each obstacle query stops at the nearest distance already found, up to 200 mm. The freer end supplies the clearance. Insert-wall measurements collect crossings only within their existing 25 mm local-ray reach because they need both sides of a wall.
  - **Fastener lookups:** removal order, service depth, minimum teardown and repair reassembly use `assembly/fastenerIndex.ts` for screw ids and clamped parts. Each operation builds its own index from the current chains. Clamping queries deduplicate shared stacks and retain screw order. Repair arrows and replacement-insert preparation use the same indexed id lookup within a repair plan.
  - **Removal units:** `assembly/removalUnits.ts` indexes local blockers once when a removal plan first searches for a unit. It finds mutually blocked pairs through those neighbors and retains the previous source order, lexical pair direction and group priority. Later searches filter out removed members without rescanning every possible pair. Assembly reports, proven build order, repair and print-merge planning share `planRemoval`. The index ends with the plan, so geometry edits rebuild it.
  - **Diagnostic choices:** `assembly/blockerChoice.ts` scans candidates once for stuck-part blockers, blocked service exits, enclosure splits and lip warnings. Equal counts retain the first direction or plane. Lip warnings minimize solid blockers before lip counts. Split scores sum both halves without flattening their blocker lists. Inputs stay unchanged and every report reads current geometry. Service dependency searches still order every alternative because the first path can fail.
  - **Notes:** build order, drivers, service depth, enclosures, knife edges, support, WARN rows, unpriced lines, hidden parts, which the viewport checks leave out, Fixed Mates: Onshape mates that couldn't become a joint and the dropped slide of cylindrical mates, read from `robotLink.fixedMates` (`fixedMateFindings`, §8c), and Missing Mates: joints whose Onshape mate was deleted, found by Update from Onshape (`onshape.gone`, `goneMateFindings`; the joint ids join the fingerprint as `goneMates`). They turn a check amber and read when it's expanded; they never affect the verdict.
  - **Cases** (`readiness/caseChecks.ts`, `workspace/caseChecks.ts`, §8h): a robot's Cases are its tests, so every Case in `project.cases` is a row of its own, titled with the Case's name. Each runs 20 times with seeds 1 to 20 (`runCaseTimes`, § Seeded runs in [Cases](cases.md)), driven by the scene's ready Walk when it has one, else the Case's reference. The worker picks the Walk from every `velocity-tracking` record with `readyWalkRecord` (`skills/skillRows.ts`), the rule Drive's `readyVelocity` uses: records fresh for the current model before stale ones, then the newest, and only a fresh one with Arena evidence drives. A row passes at 18 of 20 or better, warns from 10 to 17 and fails below 10; its detail is the count (“17 of 20”) and its one note names the most common failure (the contract's label, capitalized, or Missed). Each row runs the robot's fit first and fails with the first thing that stops it, in order: the Case's slot doesn't fit (`slotRefusal`'s words, with New Case on the note: a `new-case` fix, offered when the robot can try some goal), the model doesn't compile (MuJoCo's error), it falls in 1.5 s on hold (“Fell at 0.4 s”), or a walker of four or more legs with no ready Walk fails the 5-second fall check Drive uses (“Falls on its walk”). The fit is the robot's, not the Case's, so the worker's model job runs it once per model (`checkFit`, `cases/fitCheck.ts`) and every Case's runs job carries its `stop`; the slot is checked per Case in `runSeeds`. A robot whose model won't build is keyed by its parts and joints. Measured in Node on the fixtures, the fit costs 10 to 30 ms for an arm or a rover and 200 to 400 ms for a walker, whose fall check bakes its gait. Tests: `fitCheck.test.ts` runs the worker session on fixtures (the biped on the rover's Reach the Flag, an arm with a zero joint axis, the weak quadruped) and checks each row names the first stop. Rows are notes, never blockers: a Case is the person's own bar, so Ready to build doesn't wait on it, and Ignore or Ignore All Cases clears it like any note. A failing row stays red with only notes in it (`rowStatus`). MuJoCo never runs on the main thread: the main thread sends the parts, joints, measures and triangles to a pool of workers (`cases/caseChecks.worker.ts`, `caseChecksClient.ts`: half the cores, at most four, closed after 30 s idle) and splits the seeds across them (`seedBatches`). Each worker gets one job at a time from its own queue, so the pool can still change what waits: the model job goes first, a newer check drops the queued runs of Cases it no longer needs (`keepOnly`, only from the newest check by `nextCaseTurn`, which `runReadiness` takes before its first await), and a Case already running for an older check is shared, not run twice. A worker keeps the last robot it was sent (`CaseCheckSession`), so the parts and triangles cross to it once per model, not once per Case and batch. A Case whose runs throw (a contract the crate refuses, a worker that dies) fails with “The runs stopped.” and is still a note: `caseCheckRows` never rejects, so one bad Case never takes the other findings or Ready to build with it, and since that verdict isn't kept, the next check tries again. A check a newer one has overtaken queues nothing: it takes a Case's kept or in-flight verdict when the newer check shares it, and otherwise a `not run` row (`replacedRow`) whose agent text says a newer check replaced it, never that it errored. Case names aren't unique, so a Case's finding carries its `caseId`, and a Case row owns only the findings with its id (`ownedBy`); other rows own the findings with their name and no `caseId`. A verdict is kept by the Case's hash, the model's physics and actuator hashes and the Walk skill's hash, so a Case runs again only when the robot or the Case changes; a rename, a price or a print setting reuses it. The worker builds the robot's model to hash it only when the parts, the joints or the Walk records changed since the last run, and the newest check drops every queued run before it queues that model job, so no batch for the old model starts while it builds. A Walk whose skill file the bridge couldn't read isn't remembered, so the next check asks again. Checks land together: `runReadiness` waits for the Case rows with the other findings, so after an edit that changes the model's physics or actuators, every row waits for the Cases' runs; edits that keep those hashes reuse the kept verdicts and don't wait.
    - **Budget:** 20 runs of a gallery Case within 10 s. Measured on one thread in Node (`runCaseTimes`, Apple M-series with other work running): Touch the Dots on the six-joint arm 0.5 s, Reach the Flag on the rover 0.7 s and on the hexapod 4.9 s. The hexapod was 35 s until the legs drive stopped baking its gait on every controller change (`bakeCycles` keeps one bake per setup) and the seeds started sharing one setup (`caseRunner`). In the app the seeds split across the pool, so each worker runs 5 to 10 of them.
  - **Wording:** every finding has two texts. `text` is for the person: one plain sentence, no tool names, no ids, no thresholds they'd have to interpret (`RULES` and `PLAIN_ENGINEERING` in `readiness/wording.ts`). `agentText` keeps the measurements and the instruction, for `get_readiness`, the edit-result delta, follow-ups and Fix. Engineering findings also keep their numbers in `detail`, shown under the sentence. A new check needs a rule there; `ReportLine.code` names it.
  - **Decisions:** only the user clears a blocker, and the project remembers (`project.readiness`). **Ignore** drops one finding (`ignored`, by key); **Ignore All ⟨name⟩** drops a whole kind, like Thin Walls, for parts not made yet too (`ignoredRules`, by rule). Neither comes back when the parts change: it's an opinion, not a measurement. They aren't part of the fingerprint and apply to the last result at once (`withDecisions`), with nothing measured again. Ignored, under the list, restores them. The agent sees what's ignored and leaves it alone; it can only `dispute_finding`, which shows its reason on the row and clears nothing.
  - **What the build needs goes on the list by itself** (`utils/bomNeeds.ts`, `buildBom`): a battery or a tether brings a switch, fuse and wire, and a battery a charger; sensors, a Pi camera, a Pi, a Pi 5, inserts and screws bring theirs. Each is one pack, unless something on the list already covers it, so they count toward the budget and are never a finding. Cables and power pigtails come from the wiring netlist instead (below), which adds only what the list doesn't already hold under the same product and length key. Extras on a different cable key do not reduce the selected stock quantity. A servo without connector ports (LX-16A, SCS0009, PWM servos) and a battery without one (an agent-sourced pack) keep the one-pack rule. The Build panel requests routing independently of readiness (`build/useBomWiring.ts`), through the same worker cache. Its cable choices survive unrelated price, budget and Case checks. A changed part, joint, routing choice, extra, power supply or sourced record requires a new result. Export and cart menus wait for that result and stay closed while it is pending or failed, so a schematic cable choice cannot be exported or bought from that menu. Only the newest request publishes a result, and a routing failure uses the existing error toast.
  - **Wiring netlist** (`src/wiring/`, pure): `wiringNetlist` reads the connector ports (§8a) and returns the servo buses, the power tree and the servos no adapter takes. Servos are those with a `bus-in` port; adapters (U2D2, Waveshare Bus Servo Adapter (A), OpenRB-150) have a `bus-out` and no `bus-in`. Servos join the bus whose connector matches theirs and daisy-chain in `servoLayout` order (`busOrder`), the numbering Operate and `joint_map.json` use, then any servo on no joint. Several adapters of one connector split the chain into runs in that order that differ by at most one servo, so a hexapod's 18 servos on two adapters are 9 and 9, and 4 servos on three are 1, 2 and 1. The power tree runs battery (or the tether's DC Power Supply) → Inline Fuse Holder → Power Switch → each regulator, and feeds each adapter with a `power-in` from the regulator `servoRegulator` picks for the servo rail, else from the switch. The battery is the one the voltage checks pick (`servoSource`), among packs and holders with a `power-out` port and battery supplies on a part or in `extras`, else the first of them; `power.battery` says it's a battery rather than a holder or tether, and `bomNeeds` reads it, so a supply battery brings the fuse, switch and charger its power tree routes through. Each net carries its connector type and exact local port at both ends. `wiringSupplies` counts each routed bus hop under its selected stock cable key, grouping the same product and length. Before routing is measured, matching connectors retain the existing cable keys (`5264-3` → Bus Servo Cable, `jst-eh-3` → ROBOTIS Robot Cable-X3P). A routed hop without selected stock contributes no bus cable line and stays unresolved in Cable reach. A selected power cable contributes one cable line, otherwise each XT60, EC5 or barrel end contributes its pigtail. A tethered arm gets a barrel pigtail and no XT60.
  - **Servo bus** (`wiring/wiringChecks.ts`): a blocker row, shown only when it fails, when the build has an adapter but none takes a servo's connector (an XL430 beside a Waveshare adapter). A build with no adapter at all isn't flagged.
  - **Cable reach** (`wiring/routeAnalysis.ts`, `routeMotion.ts`, `routeGeometry.ts`): routing extends those same nets in the analysis worker. Each endpoint retains a copy of its named local connector port, including position and axis. `partMatrix`, `resolveJoints`, `buildRig` and `forwardKinematics` place the connectors and link anchors. Routes leave each connector along its axis for at least 8 mm, extending straight to its own expanded housing boundary when needed, and keep 3 mm clear of conservative part bounds. Every neighbouring solid is checked along the full exit. Clear segments route directly. A deterministic corner visibility graph is built only when a segment needs a detour, and its corners are shared across the remaining ordered anchors. Only a connector's exit can cross its own housing. Each bend adds 3 mm of allowance, then the complete length receives 15% slack and rounds upward to millimeters. These allowances are planning values, not maker bend-radius certification. Bounds can reject a path through an actual opening because they enclose the whole solid.
    - Joint endpoints, rest and the existing range samples form a Cartesian product, including simultaneous shoulder and wrist motion. Each sample covers half the largest adjacent sampling gap on each joint. Revolute displacement is bounded by `2 × radius × sin(half-gap / 2)`, and prismatic displacement by the half-gap. The radius includes every downstream pivot offset and maximum downstream slide travel. Shared ancestor motion cancels between each route node and obstacle. The remaining chains bound each connector, exit, anchor and detour corner relative to that obstacle. Routing works in the source connector’s moving frame and retains each obstacle’s orientation. Segment clearance uses linearly interpolated displacement envelopes between its nodes, so a connector exit stays fixed relative to its own housing. Segment lengths receive their relative displacement allowance. A changed detour topology between samples is unresolved. A blocker retains its limiting sampled pose, with joint names in Checks, while Build Along draws the rest route.
    - Motion bounds (`wiring/routeBound.ts`) prepare owner chains, joint range factors and downstream pivot allowances lazily for each ordered owner pair. One query serves every bus and power net, including all sampled poses. Point distances still use the current rest-frame coordinates. Shared-owner pairs return zero without preparing chains. Preparation ends with the netlist analysis, so later edits and undo build fresh bounds.
    - Clearance intersections (`wiring/routeCollision.ts`) reuse one interval accumulator across all six box planes. Connector exits, anchors, detour corners and visibility edges share this query. Each query owns its accumulator, retaining the strict epsilon boundary and linearly varying displacement envelope. Detour searches prepare each visited point in each tested obstacle’s local frame once, retaining its displacement bound across visibility edges and ordered anchors. Preparation starts only when a segment needs a detour and ends with that route call, so direct routes build no point cache and later poses or edits prepare fresh coordinates.
    - Detour labels (`wiring/routeGeometry.ts`) skip obstacle corner construction when a path has no interior points between its two connector exits. Every sampled bus and power route shares this check. Paths of four points or fewer return the same empty labels without expanding bounds, transforming corners or creating corner records. Paths with anchors or detours keep their existing matching and label order. `routeTopology.test.ts` checks the four-to-five-point boundary, duplicate corners, obstacle order, later edits and source preservation. On direct paths among 300 and 600 transformed obstacles, corner transforms fall from 2,400 and 4,800 to zero. These are operation counts without timing assertions or a claim about complete wiring-analysis time.
    - Stock selection (`wiring/cableStock.ts`) scans compatible supplies once without sorting or temporary candidate arrays. It retains the first shortest fitting cable, the last longest confirmed cable when none fits, or the first compatible unknown-length cable when no confirmed lengths exist. Automatic and explicitly selected stock share this helper. Selection retains no state between calls, so catalog edits are read afresh. `cableStockSelection.test.ts` compares 1,600 mixed-stock queries against sorted selection, checks ties and source edits, and requires one length validation per compatible cable on 300- and 600-cable fixtures. These are operation counts without timing assertions.
    - No range, unsupported loop-closure motion, missing connector geometry, blocked exits, changed detours and more than 4,096 coupled samples remain unresolved. Unresolved routes fail Cable reach, as do referenceable stock cables shorter than the required length. Unknown cable lengths remain unknown. `project.wiring` can retain a stock supply and ordered link-local anchors for an existing from-part/port to-part/port pair. It does not define another bus or numbering scheme. Power nets with a virtual fuse, switch or supply endpoint remain schematic and are excluded from Cable reach. They stay in the wiring list and BOM. Power nets with two scene endpoints are measured and still fail for missing port geometry or compatible stock. From-to text reads the same routed nets and names the exact ports, measured requirement and selected stock.
  - **Cost:** about 5 s from an edit to a verdict on a 225-part robot, model rebuild included, all but about 100 ms in workers (`perf:agent` phases `get_readiness`, `edit_then_readiness`).
  - **Clashes** (`three/analysis/collide.ts`, `crossing.ts`): two parts clash when their surfaces cross, triangle against triangle through the BVH, or when enough sampled points of one sit inside the other, and the overlap is deeper than the tolerance. Crossing finds what vertices can't: a rod through an uncut plate, crossed bars. Depth comes from probes inside both solids around each crossing. A pair that already overlaps at rest (a pose or a sweep) reports again only once it's over 2 mm deeper than at rest (`DEEPER_THAN_REST_MM`). Calibrated against exact Manifold volumes on saved scenes: every new pair that could be tested was a real overlap.
  - **New check rule:** when a person or an audit finds something the verdict missed, add the check and a seeded fault test in the same change. Don't add a check that cries wolf: a false blocker teaches people and agents to ignore all of them. Calibrate on a real scene (`scratch/`) first.
- **Thin walls** (`thinWallOf`, `printReport.ts`): a *panel* (100 mm² or more of surface under 0.84 mm, both faces counted, or a tenth of the part) must be thickened. A smaller area is a *knife edge*, usually a grazing cut, which the slicer drops: a note. Only panels are issues. Area comes from 4,000 to 20,000 rays spread by surface area (`wallThickness`), so a big triangle counts at its full size; calibrated against a dense ground truth on real scenes, where verdicts only disagree near 100 mm².
- **Study** (`src/study/`, §8h): one engine compares combinations of the robot's design choices for both the Simulate tab and `study_design`. Knobs select catalog servo models and versions per joint group, supply voltage, battery, battery position, foot length and width scales, or an agent-made part's named dimension. `studyScene` composes every knob on copies, keeping the saved parts untouched. Explicit foot and battery-offset targets use their part ids, while omitted targets use the scene’s foot names or battery types. Named dimensions recompile together per part through the kernel pool before grading, and catalog swaps load their meshes before geometry is measured. Named dimensions compile before foot scales so both changes apply when they share a part. Named-dimension compilation needs the desktop kernel, and the web bridge refuses it.
  - `enumerateStudy` takes the complete cross product while it fits the cap, otherwise samples unique selections with a seeded generator. The default cap is 64 and the default seed is 1. A current-design row is measured separately from those combinations.
  - `createStudyGrader` runs the assembly, geometry, printability, fits, sourcing and engineering checks against each candidate's meshes, then combines them through `readinessOf`. A timed-out assembly check fails its row. Cost comes from the candidate's `buildBom`, with support estimates, and weight and runtime reuse `robotWeight` and `batteryRuntimeMinutes`. Every row retains each check's status, detail, finding items and agent measurements, cost in USD, weight, available runtime and the prior sweep's torque, stability and sizing metrics. Geometry-only checks keep the findings in their detail too, so the table tooltip explains the failure. A Case adds seeded passes and margins (§8h). A design with failing checks remains in the table and plot. A compilation or measurement error remains in the table with no plotted measurements and no Apply.
  - `StudyGradeCache` keeps exact solids, world measures, placements, assembly, rest clashes, sweeps and servo touch pairs for one run. Equal triangle hashes still need equal float values before reuse. Placements include every part id, solid and matrix, while assembly and sweep keys include their inputs. A timed-out assembly is never cached. Each candidate recalculates engineering, print reports and the BOM from its own materials, settings and purchases. `gradeStudyDetached` uses the same grader for one disposable call.
  - `paretoFrontier` excludes rows with a failing check or a Case refusal, then keeps only rows no other eligible row dominates. Lower cost and weight, longer runtime, higher Case pass rate and higher median Case margin are better. Equal rows stay on the frontier. The frontier never changes Checks' Ready to build verdict.
- **Project settings** (`ProjectSettings` in `types/robotics.ts`, saved with the scene): printer (a My printers id, a catalog id, or `default`; old `bambu-p1s` maps to the P1S), print quality (`printProcess`), default filament, infill, budget, purchase-only extras, declared joints.
- **Time** (`utils/engineeringTime.ts`): the Time field below the money budget in `BuyTotal` stores `project.budgetHours`, with hours as its unit. Zero clears it. The Time row fails above the budget, warns above 90%, and passes otherwise. With no budget its total is a note, never a blocker. Print hours come from `printReport`, with matching sliced plates replacing only those plates' estimates by number and hash when the job's label key is current, using the same `currentSlices` filter as Print mode. Both paths index slice records once through `sliceLookup`, retaining the first matching record. Time accepts only finite, nonnegative seconds, including zero, before retaining a duplicate key. Parts that do not fit a plate remain in the estimate. Divide print hours by the number in My printers, at least one, without assigning plates or modeling printer availability. Assembly follows `guideStepParts`, the saved proven guide while current or the same approximate planner used by printing. `runEngineeringNow` chooses these steps from the saved scene, so adding fastener followers to the physical model's joints does not invalidate a current guide. `assemblyTimes` exports per-step minutes for consumers. Planning allowances (`TIME_MINUTES`) are 1 minute per step, 0.5 per fastener, 1.5 per insert and 2 per other part. Each part counts once, even when inserts recur in later steps. Servo setup and calibration add 5 minutes per movable joint in `servoLayout`. These allowances are estimates, not observed build durations. Delivery, failed prints and service lead times are excluded. The total stays marked ≈ even after slicing because assembly and setup are estimates. `agentText` names the longest plate, the step with the most fasteners and the servo count, then suggests compatible Merge operations or catalog replacements. Infill is a project-wide setting with no agent tool, so Fix does not ask the agent to change it on individual parts. Fix uses the existing quote and undo flows. The readiness fingerprint includes the budget, printer count, nozzle, bed geometry, plate placement and rotation, saved guide steps and current sliced times so each updates the row.
- **Merging printed parts** (§8f): Print mode offers to union printed parts of one material that are bolted together on one rigid link, only when the assembly order still holds, no servo is sealed in, and the union fits the bed without new support. The BOM loses the screws and inserts that only joined them, and the guide loses their steps; nothing here keeps its own count.
- **BOM** (`utils/bom.ts`): components counted by part (and version), hardware by exact normalized spec (`hardware:type|size|length|finish[|drive]`), parts with `supplyId` as that supply, printed parts as filament grams per material (shell + infill model, Bambu densities) plus estimated supports, then spools with a 15% margin; extras by key. With **Made by a Service** chosen in Print mode (`project.madeBy`, §8f), the printed parts are a **Printed parts** section instead of filament (`utils/bomPrinted.ts`): one line per printed part (the tracker's shape key includes the part, so mirrored left and right parts are two lines), its copies as the count, its material as the spec, and the service's quote as the price, with its check date like a purchase. Quotes live in the scene (`project.quotes`, one per part, keyed by `quoteKey`: shape key, chosen face, material and copies), so the BOM, Budget, the agent's `bom` tool and every other `buildBom` caller read them unchanged. A line whose part changed since its quote keeps the old price in amber (Stale) until the new one arrives; a part the service refused shows its reply as the line's warning and no price, so Budget counts it under “no price on N lines”. Switching back to a printer drops the section and brings back Filament exactly as it was; the quotes stay in the scene for the next switch. Storing new quotes drops those of parts no longer in the scene (`withQuotes`). Stand-ins (block-out kinds) and hand-modeled COTS are listed as needing a source. Sheet parts have their own material and stock thickness and do not need a catalog source.
- **Sheet parts** (`ScenePart.sheet`, `sheet/materials.ts`, `utils/bomSheet.ts`): aluminum 6061, carbon fiber and acrylic are counted in **Sheet**, grouped by material and stock thickness (`sheet:<material>|<thicknessMm>`). They leave printed parts, filament and printer plates, including Made by a Service quotes. Legacy CNC Aluminum and Laser-Cut Acrylic/Carbon tags join Sheet only when the worker detects a constant-thickness profile, mapping to aluminum and carbon fiber respectively with the measured thickness. Shapes without that profile keep their source blocker. Material lookup without a measurement falls back to the smallest dimension for older scenes. Prices stay unpriced until entered. The build guide groups sheet pieces separately from printed copies by material, thickness and shape, and a sheet-only build does not list a 3D printer. Material and affine thickness changes invalidate the guide. Solid mesh volume × material density supplies Weight, Servo torque and MJCF, so simulated Peak torque uses the same mass. Densities are 2.7, 1.6 and 1.19 g/cm³ respectively. Material and affine thickness edits invalidate Checks and stored walk and fall evidence, including material changes that keep the same dimensions. Shop-rule checks and sheet pricing remain tracked in #263, ahead of bends and press hardware.
  - **Material sources, checked 2026-11-14:** stock thicknesses follow [SendCutSend’s materials catalog](https://sendcutsend.com/materials/). Nominal densities come from [The Aluminum Association’s December 2025 alloy tables](https://www.aluminum.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/Teal%20Sheets%20December%202025.pdf) for 6061 aluminum, [Rock West Composites’ carbon sheet data](https://www.rockwestcomposites.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-rwc-master-catalog/default/dw48c50396/wysiwyg/Elevated-Materials-Sheet-Product-Data-Sheet.pdf) for carbon fiber plate and [Plaskolite’s OPTIX sheet data, page 3](https://plaskolite.com/docs/default-source/bro/bro084_opx_eu.pdf) for general acrylic. These are nominal material values, not a guarantee of a particular shop’s sheet manufacturer or layup.
  - **Profile detection** (`sheet/detectSheet.ts`): geometry-worker requests and analysis reports share the same detector. Each candidate normal projects every face vertex once, retaining the face's minimum and maximum depth in a `Float64Array`. Overall thickness, side validation and both cap boundaries reuse those intervals. Side validation finishes before cap edges are built. Both caps share one planar frame. `sheet/capBoundary.ts` counts cap edges as projected triangles arrive, preparing each vertex key once per triangle without collecting a full edge list. Boundary edges retain their first occurrence's direction, point references and order. Intervals and edge counts belong to the current candidate only, so moved meshes measure afresh.   - Motion-aware servo picks (`utils/actuatorSizing.ts`): the shared picker accepts optional peak joint speed in degrees per second, peak angular acceleration in degrees per second squared and inertia in kg·m². Acceleration adds `I × |alpha|` to the existing hold and stance loads. The picker keeps their stall and heat margins and requires the larger load to fit the straight torque-speed line from stall to no-load rpm at the chosen supply voltage (`ratingAt`). It treats peak speed, acceleration and gravity load as coincident, a conservative estimate rather than multibody inverse dynamics. A moving joint cannot pass on an actuator without published rpm. Static callers keep the same rules. `skeletonSizing` bounds inertia by summing each estimated mass times its maximum chain-path radius squared, with each link mass at its furthest end and payload at its tip, so a folded rest pose cannot hide extension load. Each link measures its length and center together once per sizing call, sharing the length across mass distribution and inertia bounds.
