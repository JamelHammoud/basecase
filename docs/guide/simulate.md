# Simulate and train

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 8d. Simulate & Train (MuJoCo)

Stages 3 and 4 of the robotics loop (§1); milestones in [docs/Robotics loop.md](../../docs/Robotics%20loop.md). The **Simulate** tab (right panel, shown while the scene has joints) runs the scene's robots under MuJoCo physics and trains policies for them.

- **One formulation, one model, one oracle.** MuJoCo's formulation runs everywhere: the live sim (`@mujoco/mujoco`, DeepMind's official WebAssembly build), Forge's own batched physics for training (§8g), and C MuJoCo as the unforked oracle, all from the same generated MJCF at the **same pinned version**. Forge may train on a cheaper profile of the model, but a result counts only after replay on the reference model in C. Never add a physics engine with a different formulation.
  - **Updating MuJoCo:** `node tools/sim/update-mujoco.mjs [version]` (latest when omitted) bumps `package.json` and `electron/trainer/pyproject.toml` + `uv.lock` together, then runs `node tools/sim/smoke.mjs`, which fails if the two engines differ in version or in result. Never bump one side alone. We consume upstream releases; we don't fork MuJoCo's C code.
- **The scene is the source of truth.** `src/sim/mjcf.ts` (`buildMjcf`) writes the MJCF from the scene; never hand-edit or store a model file as truth. Fix the design, and the model follows.
  - Bodies: one per joint (`buildRig`), holding the parts that joint moves, under a root body with the robot's base. `src/sim/morphology.ts` classifies each robot as fixed, legged, wheeled or wheel-legged from its joint tree, names and catalog parts; anything but fixed floats (free joint), and `MjcfInput.fixedBase` overrides it. Continuous wheel joints get `velocity` actuators, and `SimJoint.actuator` maps joints to actuators, so never assume the two share an index. Parts with `physics.dynamic` (SimReady objects, §8e) get a free body per group, with their own mass and friction (`priority="1"`, because MuJoCo otherwise takes the higher of two friction values and the 0.9 default would hide anything lower), and collide with robots, scenery and each other. Every other solid part is fixed scenery.
  - Body frames keep the world's orientation at rest (origin = the joint's pivot), so sim poses map back to parts without rotations (`simDeltas` in `useSimulation.ts`).
  - Frames: scene mm Y-up → MuJoCo m Z-up as `(x, −z, y) / 1000` (the agent frame). The robot faces −Y.
  - Geoms: the convex hull of each part's mesh (hull vertices only, `maxhullvert` 96, `inertia="convex"`), mass from `partMasses` (§8b), inertia from the hull. Robot geoms collide with the floor and scenery, not with each other (rest-pose hulls overlap); exact self-collision stays with the mesh sweeps in `three/interference.ts`.
  - **Measured servos** (every servo on a revolute joint has an `ActuatorSpec.fit`, §8a; `src/sim/servoModel.ts`, `mjcfServo.ts`): BAM's voltage P law, at the variant's `atVolts`. `kp` = kt / R × firmware P × error gain × V; `kv="0"` set explicitly (the firmware runs D 0, and `dampratio` resolves only at compile time, so randomized armature or mass would leave a stale `kv`); joint damping = viscous + kt²/R (back-EMF); armature from the fit; force range ±kt × max duty × V / R. Damping sits on the joint, outside the force clamp, so a saturated servo still loses torque with speed, as a voltage-limited motor does. `frictionloss` = M6 base friction + `load_friction_external` × the weight moment beyond the joint (`holdingLoad`), a static stand-in for BAM's load-dependent friction. The servo's command delay uses MuJoCo's native actuator delay (`delay`, `nsample` = ⌈delay / timestep⌉ + 2, linear interpolation). These joints read `dynamics: 'measured'`.
  - **Estimated servos** (no fit, or no servo): range from `RobotJoint`, force limit from the servos' stall torque (`actuatorOf`), else the published `effortNm`, else an estimate from the weight the joint holds. `kp` reaches full torque about 10° off target, `dampratio` 1, armature from the torque. The Model section and the agent's sim report count these (`dynamics: 'estimated'`).
  - **Not modelled:** the STS3215 firmware's target rate limit (BAM's `max_velocity`, 5.1 rad/s), current limits, Stribeck and motor-side load friction, supply sag and backlash. The gate shows the rate limit is the largest remaining gap on steps (a light-load step is 21 mrad RMS off BAM, against 22 for a fitted first-order-plus-delay); adding it means a per-substep target ramp in the engine, `train.py` and Forge alike.
  - **Delay history:** a delayed actuator reads past controls, and MuJoCo starts that history at zero, which would yank every joint toward 0 at reset. `SimEngine.reset` and `respawn` fill it with each joint's current position (`primeCtrlHistory` → `mj_initCtrlHistory`), and `train.py` does the same with `ctrlDefault` before saving its start state (`mjSTATE_FULLPHYSICS` includes the history, so every episode reset restores it). Anything else that resets a model with delays must do the same.
  - **Proof:** gate `s3-servo-models` (§0k) runs plant-bam's nominal STS3215 at 7.4 V and XL330 at 5 V on four pendulum loads through a step, a 0.2–5 Hz chirp, a multisine and a hold against gravity (`verify/servo/bam-reference/`), and requires the sim's RMS error to beat half the teleport baseline and a first-order-plus-delay filter fitted to the same BAM trajectory, on every run. It also checks no fixture robot is kicked at reset and that WASM matches C MuJoCo with delays.
  - The start state is the pose on the canvas, lifted to just above the floor.
- **Simulating never edits parts.** Like posing (§8c), the sim only moves what the canvas shows (`Viewport3D.setPose`). Ending it, or editing parts or joints while it runs, returns the canvas to the posed model.
- **World items** (§8h) come in through `MjcfInput.world` (`src/sim/mjcfWorld.ts`): bodies with no parts, posed by `worldDeltas`; markers don't collide.
- **Off the main thread.** `src/sim/sim.worker.ts` runs `SimEngine` (`src/sim/engine.ts`, no DOM, so it also runs in Node) in real time × speed and posts body poses per frame. `Simulation.run()` steps a fresh model headless in its own worker (for agent checks) without touching the live one. `Simulation.runRoutine()` loads the scene and sends a `routine` message: the live worker then steps a Routine's L1 run (§8j) in place of free stepping and posts its new trace events with each frame.
- **Controllers:** Hold pose, an animation's keyframes (does the walk cycle really walk?), a trained policy, Drive (live commands by robot class, §8h), or Limp (zero force range, so only passive joint damping and friction remain). Push shoves the robot sideways with half its weight for 0.15 s.
- **Grab and fling:** while a simulation is loaded, the Move and Rotate gizmos grab instead of edit (`Viewport3D` `onGrab`; parts never change). The dragged part's body is pulled toward the gizmo on a critically damped spring scaled by its robot's mass, with gravity compensated (`SimEngine.grab`, like MuJoCo's viewer perturbation), so a slow drag lifts or turns the robot and a quick release flings it. A multi-selection grabs its first part, and its gizmo follows the robot.
- **Result:** per robot "Standing" or "Fell at 2.4 s" (tilt over 60° or the root below 45% of its start height), tilt and distance moved; Peak torque lists the hardest-working joints (peak / limit, time at the limit), and a click selects the joint's link.
- **Training** (`useTraining.ts`, `electron/trainer.mjs`, `electron/trainer/train.py`):
  - One job runs at a time across the whole app (`electron/trainer.mjs`), because each job uses every core. Others queue, user training ahead of background work, and a crash reads as a crash rather than a stop. The time budget counts active time, so a sleeping laptop doesn't spend it.
  - Runs as a child process through **uv**, which installs the pinned Python, MuJoCo and PyTorch on first use into `library/trainer-env` (`--frozen` against `uv.lock`). Without uv the Train section says how to install it.
  - Users pick tasks, not rewards: **Stand** and **Walk** (a forward speed), with a 5 min / 20 min / 1 h budget. Floating robots only.
  - PPO (rsl_rl-style: ELU MLP 256-128-64, adaptive KL step, GAE) over 64 robots stepped together by MuJoCo's threaded `rollout`; about 20K steps/s on an M-series Mac.
  - **Sim-to-real by default:** every robot gets its own friction (0.6–1.3×), link masses (±15%) and servo stiffness (±15%), a random heading and start noise, and is shoved every 2–5 s.
  - Progress streams as `train-progress` events (one JSON line per batch). Stop saves what it learned. The policy is checkpointed every minute, then evaluated in a clean 8 s run.
  - Results go to `library/policies/<id>/`: `policy.json` (the app runs it), `policy.onnx` (anything else runs it) and the `model.xml` it trained on. The scene keeps a `PolicyRecord` in `project.policies`.
- **Stale models:** `SIM_MODEL_VERSION` (`src/sim/mjcfTypes.ts`) goes on every job and `PolicyRecord`, and a missing version counts as 0. Bump it when actuator or contact behaviour changes for existing robots; stale policies report Not run with "Train it again" instead of failing Peak torque, the `simulate` tool refuses them, and `training_status` marks them. `isPolicySpec` refuses another `POLICY_VERSION`. Version 1: measured servo models.
- **The policy contract** (`src/sim/policy.ts`, mirrored in `train.py` and Forge's `forge-train`, §8g): observation = gravity, angular and linear velocity in the root frame, command, joint positions − default, joint velocities, last action; action = default + 0.25 × clip(a, −1, 1), every 20 ms. Change all three together and bump `POLICY_VERSION`.
- **Agent tools** (`src/agent/agentSim.ts`, `cad-mcp-server.mjs`): `simulate` (seconds, controller `hold` / `limp` / `animation:<name>` / `policy:<name>`, shoves by direction and share of body weight, rendered frames) reports falls, tilt, travel and each joint's peak effort against its limit; `train_policy` (stand / walk, minutes) starts training and returns; `training_status` reports progress and the policies with their clean-run results. The prompt has the agent simulate every legged design (hold, then shoves) and fix the design, not the test. `__studio.simTool(kind, payload)` runs the same tools in dev.
- **Not done yet:** sim results as engineering check rows (Pass / Warn / Fail), Reach for arms, servo delay randomization, self-collision between non-adjacent links, cloud GPU training.
