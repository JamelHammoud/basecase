# Layout and sidebar views

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 5. Layout

- **Docked side panels** (Figma reverted floating panels because they cramped the canvas).
  - Each panel is a rounded card (§2) with a 44px header row; window-level controls live in the title bar.
  - **Left (288px):** shows one view at a time, picked in the view rail (§2): **Parts · Robots · Objects · Play · Layers · Library**, opening on Parts. Its header names the view, except Play's. Loading another scene into the tab sends the scene's own views (Cases, Routines) back to Parts, so a rail click always opens the view rather than collapsing it.
    - **Play** (`components/PlayTabs.tsx`, `playViews.ts`): one rail item for what the robot does once it's designed, with **Cases · Routines · Operate** as tabs in its header (`PanelTabs`, `aria-label="Play"`; Operate is desktop only). A tab's blue dot (`status`, tone `live`) says something is live there while you look at another, named in its tooltip: a Case try (“Trying”), a Routine run (“Running”), the emulated robot (“Connected”). The rail button reopens the last Play tab (Cases at first), and clicking it while any Play tab is open collapses the sidebar. Without a robot in the scene Cases and Routines show one “No robot” + [Browse Robots] (`PlayNoRobot.tsx`) instead of the tab. Operate keeps its own panel, because a connection and its messages (a lost daemon above all) outlive the scene's robot. Commands that open a view (⌘K, the agent) still name the tab, so they land on it directly.
    - **Robots** (`RobotsPanel`): published robots to insert with their joints (§8c). Search first (“Search robots”; Enter searches SimReady at once and inserts nothing), then Imported and a section per kind: Humanoids, Small bipeds, Quadrupeds, Arms (hands are in Parts → Hands). Tiles (2 columns, like Parts) show a preview, the name and the maker; a click inserts it (the toast shows progress) or drags onto the canvas, where it stands at the drop point (§7a-2). Imported comes first: robots inserted from a link, with the preview their import rendered (right-click ▸ Delete takes one off the list after asking, like a Library model; scenes that use it keep it). Last comes **SimReady**: the makers' own arms, grippers and robot bodies from SimReady Central (Franka, UR, FANUC, Comau, Robotiq, OnRobot, AgiBot…), searched with the same field and inserted with their joints. Import Robot (a URDF / MJCF / zip link, a GitHub repo or folder, or a local folder) is the + in the view's header.
    - **Parts** (`PartsPanel`): search first, then browse. Views stack with no header row of their own: inside a view the search field's glass icon becomes Back (Esc in an empty field also goes back), and the field names the view and searches only it (“Search servos”, “Search screws”; a part page searches its group). No scoped matches shows [Search All Parts].
      - **Search** (always on top, a `SearchField`; ⌘F opens Parts and focuses it from anywhere): matches name, maker, part number, aliases and group words, ranking a word that names the group first (“servo” → servos). Results start right under the field with no count above them, like every other sidebar search. Result rows (and a part page's Goes with) carry no room for their +: it floats over the row's right end on hover (`RowActions`), and stays on the highlighted row, so names and makers run the row's full width. A size makes hardware exact: “m3x12”, “M2.5 standoff 12”, “608”. ↑/↓ move, Enter opens the highlighted part, ⌘Enter adds it. Enter in a sidebar search never changes the scene on its own.
      - **Home:** Browse rows in two sections: Components (one row per non-empty `ComponentGroup`) and Hardware (one row per `HARDWARE_FAMILIES` family). Parts lists and searches only real components and hardware: basic shapes live in the toolbar's Shapes menu (A), and holes, slots and pivot axes in ⌘K.
      - **Added by Agent:** parts the agent sourced (§8a) join the panel the moment they're saved, with no reload (`useSourcedItems`, `partCategories`). Once any exist, Components opens with an Added by Agent row. Its view shows them as tiles with the maker as the meta line, then a Needs a 3D model list of parts without a mesh: those can't be placed, so a row opens the product page. They also appear in their own group and in search, where the meta line says “Added by Agent” in place of the maker; part pages and hover cards name the maker too. Materials and equipment have no geometry, so they stay in the BOM.
      - **Category:** 2-column grid of real 3D thumbnails (`usePartThumbnail`, cached per exact variant). Hover opens a `HoverCard` beside the sidebar (`CatalogDetails`): a preview of the page in the page's own words, with the hero (no badge), the name, the maker line and the description, then the same Key specs (Size across the card, the rest below), then Buy on Amazon with Datasheet and Product page icons. Click opens the page; + adds; drag places it. The + shows only on hover or focus and takes no clicks while hidden, so a click anywhere on the tile opens the page.
      - **Part page:** calm first, details on request. A draggable spotlight hero (no badge), the name (17px semibold), the maker on one muted line and the description; then **Key specs**: at most 3 plain-language rows for newcomers (`keySpecs` in `utils/keySpecs.ts`: Size rounded, Weight, and one headline per group, e.g. a servo's Strength as “Up to 2 kg at 10 cm”, a gripper's Can hold, a LiDAR's Range); **All specs**, collapsed: the exact datasheet values as label/value rows; **Details**, collapsed: part number, Mounting, 3D model source, Datasheet and Product Page; then Goes with. Amazon is a cart icon in the footer beside Add. The hardware page shares the hero and title.
      - **Footer:** part and hardware pages end in a bar below the scroll area, so the scroll fade never dims it: Add fills the bar, with the Amazon cart icon beside it. It doesn't repeat the name; the title and the configurator above already say what Add places.
      - **Scroll fade:** the list fades 28px at an edge only while there's more to scroll past it (`useScrollFade`, same look as the agent feed).
      - **Hardware page:** a live preview of the exact spec, the title, then the family's `HardwareConfigurator` and `HardwareSpecs` as hairline sections, and the footer with Add and Find on Amazon. Each family remembers its last spec.
        - **Head / Style / Ends:** the family's types as real 3D thumbnail tiles (3 across, `.preview-sm`, label under), drawn at the current size, finish and drive but the default length, so scrubbing the length doesn't re-render them. The chosen one has the Library's blue ring.
        - **Drive:** a `ChoiceGrid` of tall cells, each a `DriveIcon` over its name; the header's right side names the recess (“PZ2”, “2.5 mm hex key”, “1 mm slot”).
        - **Size** and **Finish** (swatch dot + name): `ChoiceGrid`s. Never solid blue chips: blue is for tools, tiles and focus.
        - **Length:** the field, then the stock lengths in one sideways-scrolling strip centred on the current length, its edges fading only while lengths are hidden past them. The strip is the only way to step between stock lengths: no − / + beside the field, and a custom length's hint doesn't repeat the nearest ones.
        - **Specs** (Size, Thread with pitch, Standard, Head Ø × height) and **Holes** (`hardwareHoleRows`: clearance, counterbore or countersink, tap drill, nut trap, printed hole…) use the part page's `SpecRow`s, units muted.
      - **Drive:** every headed screw (Socket, Button, Flat, Pan) takes Hex · Torx · Phillips · Pozidriv · Slotted; Set takes Hex · Torx · Slotted (cross recesses aren't made in grub screws). Chips keep the `DRIVES` order on every head; `defaultDrive` picks the one a head starts with (Pan: Phillips, others: Hex). Every drive comes in every size of its head.
        - Where an ISO standard covers the pair, its table sets the head and recess and the Standard row names it: Socket Hex / Torx (ISO 4762 / 14579), Button Hex / Torx (ISO 7380-1 / 7380-1 TX), Flat Hex / Torx / Phillips / Pozidriv / Slotted (ISO 10642 / 14581 / 7046-1 H / 7046-1 Z / 2009), Pan Torx / Phillips / Pozidriv / Slotted (ISO 14583 / 7045 H / 7045 Z / 1580), Set Hex / Slotted (ISO 4026 / 4766).
        - Other pairs are commercial screws: the head keeps its usual standard's shape and the recess is sized for the thread (`commercialRecess`: Torx per ISO 14579, cross number per ISO 7045, slot per ISO 1580, set screw Torx matched to the ISO 4026 key). The Standard row says where the shape comes from (“ISO 7380-1 head”), and the shopping search leaves the standard out.
        - Code: `screwHead` (set screws `setScrewRecess`) and `screwStandard`. The drive is part of the spec (`spec.drive`), of the name when it isn't the default (“M3 × 8 Slotted Button Head Screw”, “Hex Socket Pan Head”) and of catalog keys (`hardware:type|size|length|finish|drive`).
      - Recesses are cut into the head with a real boolean (`cutRecess` in `three/hardwareMesh.ts`, three-bvh-csg), at the standard's key-engagement depth; Phillips and Pozidriv crosses taper like the ISO 4757 cone (stopping inside any head but a countersunk one), and Pozidriv adds its diagonal ribs. The recess walls render dark.
      - **Lengths are free:** any length from the shortest stock length up to 300 mm in 0.5 mm steps. Stock lengths (ISO 888 preferred + common retail) are one-tap chips. A custom length says “Not a stock length.”; past the stock range it suggests threaded rod.
      - **Heat-set inserts come in stock lengths only** (`HEAT_INSERTS`, `stockOnly`), from the CNC Kitchen datasheet: M3 3 (Short), 4 (Voron, Ø5) and 5.7; M4 4 and 8.1; M5 5.8 and 9.5. Each length carries its own OD and printed hole, so the Length section is a `ChoiceGrid` with the variant and OD in its header. A key without a length (`hardware:heat_insert|M3`) is the standard (longest) insert.
      - **Finish** (`FINISHES`: Black, Stainless, Zinc, Brass, Nylon, Steel, and Silver / Black anodized for aluminum) sets the part color and the shopping search. Non-default finishes are part of the name (“M3 × 12 Button Head Screw, Black”). Chips keep one order across types.
      - **Motion and frame hardware** (`data/motionHardware.ts`, `data/frameHardware.ts`, tables in `data/motionTables.ts`): Ball Bearings (deep groove, flanged NSK F-series, thrust NSK 511xx and miniature F…M), Shafts & Linear Bearings (h6 shafts, THK LM bushings), Linear Guides (HIWIN MGN7–15 rails and C/H carriages), Aluminum Extrusion (Misumi HFS5 2020/2040, HFS6 3030, HNTT T-nuts for slot 6 and 8, HBLFSN5 brackets), Collars & Retaining Rings (Ruland set-screw and clamp collars, Rotor Clip DIN 471/472) and GT2 Pulleys (Misumi GPA pulleys and toothed idlers).
        - Every number comes from the named maker's catalog or standard; where a drawing leaves something out (slot wall slope, bearing internals, washer thickness, bracket plate thickness) the mesh draws it plainly and the envelope, bores, holes and pitches stay exact.
        - Each non-ISO type is one `MotionHardware` descriptor (name, dimensions, specs, holes, note, shop query, mass, seat cutout, size labels). `hardware.ts` routes every type that has one through it, so a new family needs a descriptor, a `HARDWARE_TYPES` entry, a family and a mesh builder (`three/bearingMesh.ts`, `frameMesh.ts`, `driveMesh.ts`, `motionHardwareMesh.ts`), nothing else.
        - Rails, shafts and extrusions are cut to length up to the maker's maximum (`maxLength`), so a custom length shows no “Not a stock length” warning. Rails lie along Z; extrusions and shafts stand along Y like pins. Mass comes from the catalog (kg/m or per piece), not from mesh volume.
    - **Objects** (`components/objects/ObjectsPanel.tsx`): things a robot handles or works around, searched live in NVIDIA's SimReady Central (§8e). Search first (“Search objects”; results follow typing after 300 ms, Enter searches at once and inserts nothing), else a row per category straight under the field, with no heading (Kitchen, Food, Boxes and bins… in `objectCategories.ts`, the same `BrowseRow` as Parts' Browse rows) that opens a scoped search (“Search kitchen”, Back or Esc returns). Results are a 2-column grid of the asset's own thumbnail, name and maker, loading the next 40 as you near the end (infinite scroll, an `IntersectionObserver` 400px ahead). Hover opens details (`SimReadyTile`: caption, Size, Weight, Moving parts, Open on Hugging Face), with no Insert of its own, like Parts' and Library's cards; SimReady robots in Robots use the same tile. Click or drag inserts; the toast shows download and conversion progress.
    - **Layers:** search first (“Search layers”, matches part and group names, every word; Esc clears), keeping each match under its group's row (expanded while searching; the group row still selects the whole group), “No matches for “⟨query⟩”” when none; then a header with the part and cutout counts and, when the scene has reference images, their show/hide toggle (⇧I); the selection's count is in the multi-selection Inspector (§7a). Then a footer with the overall size (visible, non-reference parts), and rows with color/role glyph. Double-click renames. Eye and lock float over the row's right end on hover (§4, hidden elements never push content), and stay while the part is hidden or locked, so names run the full width. Right-click opens the part menu. A boolean's operands nest under its row, indented one more step (base first), with the row's chevron to collapse them; they start expanded, nested booleans recurse, and search shows a matching operand under its parents (`layerRows.ts`). An operand row selects that operand (§7a) and has no rename, menu, eye or lock; the header counts top-level parts only. An empty scene leaves Layers empty: just its header, no empty state.
    - **Cases** (`components/cases/CasesPanel.tsx`, §8h): New Case (opens the builder card in place) and Ask the Agent (puts `/case ` in the composer), then the scene's Cases, with no heading (right-click Delete asks first; the open Case's menu also has Compare With, listing the other open scenes whose robot fits, and Export Case Mat for a ground Case on desktop, §8h). New Case is the only list of goals; there are no examples beside it. Picking a Case opens it in the Simulate tab. Without a robot it shows “No robot” + [Browse Robots], which opens Robots.
    - **Routines** (`components/routines/RoutinesPanel.tsx`, §8j): the scene's Routines, built by hand or by the agent, as a flowchart (React Flow laid out with dagre, `RoutineFlow.tsx`). Without one it shows “No Routines” and a primary New Routine. Above the flow: the Routine's name, which opens a menu of the scene's Routines with New Routine, Rename and Delete (asks first); the name is a menu button, so it has no double-click rename, then Add Step and Run (only while it compiles); then a status line (checking, running, how many steps to fix, or “Every scenario passes”; nothing when the Routine just compiles, since Run already says so), any compiler message that names no node, and one Add button per sensor the robot lacks (Add Camera places a Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 on the head, or on top of the body, and mounts it on that joint). A “+” on every edge and under the last step of each path opens the step menu; selecting a step shows its fields under the flow. Nodes light from the live trace; a node whose skill isn't ready shows a Train button, which calls `onTrain(skill)`; the node already names the skill. Its rail icon is a small flowchart (`RoutinesIcon`, boxes filled when open). ⌘K has Show Routines, New Routine, Add Step and Run Routine.
    - **Operate** (`components/operate/OperatePanel.tsx`, §8k): drives emulated servos through the robot daemon. Connect Emulated Robot; without joints, “No robot” + [Browse Robots] instead, and nothing over 32 servos; once connected, one line with the servo count, the run state, Stop while motors run, Clear Fault in Look and Disconnect, the newest faults and refusals in the daemon's words, the Ladder (one button per rung, the current one checked, only the next one enabled), Calibrate Servos in Test, then Emulated servos: one row per joint with its name, bus ID, angle and temperature, and its verdict and active version once calibrated (More floats over the row on hover, with the measured versions and Roll Back; clicking the row opens its replay).
    - **Library** (`LibraryPanel`) holds saved models, in the same voice as Parts. Saved scenes live in the Scenes menu (`ScenesMenu`, ⌘O, beside the sidebar toggle), not here.
      - **New Model** (+) sits in the view's header, like Robots' Import robot: Save Selection, Save Whole Scene, Import (§7), From Image.
      - **Search** first (“Search library”, matches names and descriptions; Esc clears), then the models as a 2-column grid, with no section title: Library holds only models.
      - **Tiles** (`library/Tile.tsx`) match Robots and Objects tiles: one button with a 4:3 thumbnail that zooms on hover, the name, and the size (`347 × 213 × 347 mm`). Clicking anywhere on a tile inserts the model, and tiles drag onto the canvas. They carry no hover controls: a + would only repeat the click.
      - **Hover** opens details beside the panel (`HoverCard`): large preview, where it came from and how long ago, the description, Size, Parts and From catalog (only when non-zero). It has no Insert of its own.
      - **Right-click:** Insert, Rename, Duplicate, Delete. Rename edits the name in place. There's no double-click rename, since a tile's first click already inserts.
      - **Delete** asks first (§3b-12), then toasts “Deleted ⟨name⟩” + [Undo], which saves it back.
      - Empty, it shows “No models” and one action: Save Selection while something is selected, else Import.
    - **Saved scenes** (`ScenesMenu`, `components/scenes/`): a popover with “Search scenes” and the scenes grouped by date, arrow keys and Enter to open. ⌘O opens and closes it, named Show Scenes in the File menu and ⌘K after the button's “Scenes” tooltip. Right-click: Open (except this tab's scene), Rename, Duplicate (named as a tab's Duplicate names it, §5a), Delete (asks first). Renaming a scene that's open renames its tab too.
      - **Thumbnails** come from the scene's saved `previewDataUrl`: a WebP rendered from the saved parts with the Parts thumbnail renderer when the scene is saved (`three/scenePreview.ts`: solid, visible parts only), never a viewport screenshot with grid and axes. Scenes saved before previews existed get one generated once, in the background, and written back (`scenes/useScenePreview.ts`).
      - Rewrites (rename, duplicate, Undo) load the full record (`platformBridge.getScene`) and never resend the stored thumbnail, since the backend copies it to `ai-reference/preview.png`.
      - The backend lists scenes from cached summaries (`electron/library/sceneLibrary.mjs`, cache in `library/.scene-summaries.json`, refreshed by file mtime and size); a saved robot that needs upgrading is upgraded when it's opened (`utils/sceneRecords.ts`), not at startup.
      - Save to Library keeps every part whole in `node.part` (CAD code and geometry, sculpt meshes, hardware); a boolean is saved as a `mesh` part holding its evaluated result, without its operands (`libraryModels.ts`). Inserting a model saved from a scene recreates those parts as one group named after the model, so they stay editable. Older saves without `part` come back as blocks, with a toast saying their CAD shapes weren't saved.
  - **Right (328px):** header tabs **Inspect · Pose · Simulate · Build · Agent** (`PanelTabs`; Pose while the selection is on a robot or the scene has a skeleton, §8c; Simulate while the scene has joints, §8d). Undo/Redo, Export ▾ and its toggle sit in the title bar above it. Build is described in §8b.
  - Mode panels (Sculpt, Sketch, Import SVG) replace the Inspector while they're active (§8). They, Print and a running Case share one header, `ModeHeader`: the title, then Done with Esc. Esc does what Done does once the mode has nothing smaller to step back from (a tool, a draft, a selection inside it), except that Sketch and Import SVG stay open while they hold unsaved work.
  - **Running a Case** (§8h): while a Case runs, the sidebar and inspector collapse and turn inert (the right panel stays, last, with only its Agent tab, when that tab was showing; ⌘I opens it on that tab), and the Case console's areas take their places: each a card of its declared width, before or after the canvas as the console lists them, with the last one headed by the Case's name and Done. A Case without a console gets its camera tiles on the left (400px) and the brief, failures, servos and tries on the right (328px, the inspector's default width). The rail shows no view as open; picking one leaves the running Case and opens it.
  - Both collapse with a width animation (⌘B / ⌘I); the animated width includes the 8px gap, so the gap closes with the card. The toggles stay in the title bar.
  - **Resizable (`PanelResizer`):**
    - The 8px gap beside the canvas card is the handle: hovering it shows a 2px blue line in the gap, and dragging resizes. Left: 230–480px. Right: 290–620px.
    - Double-click resets the default width (288 / 328).
    - Dragging about 90px past the minimum collapses the panel and restores its default width for next time.
    - Widths persist in `localStorage` (`panel-widths`). The width animation is off while dragging.
- **Canvas overlays:**
  - Centered bottom toolbar.
  - Toasts above the toolbar.
  - No empty-scene prompt: an empty scene is just the canvas.
  - **First launch** is the one exception: a profile with no saved scenes and no tabs to restore opens on the two-wheel rover driving to its flag, with Simulate in front (§5a, §8h). Every new scene after that, ⌘T included, is an empty canvas.
  - Annotation pins and cards.
