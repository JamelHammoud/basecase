# Real components (Parts library)

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 8a. Real Components (Parts library)

Servos, motors, boards and sensors are real products with exact geometry, never estimates. The step-by-step procedure for adding one (identify, mesh, measure, catalog entry, ports, price, verify) is the `add-part` skill, [.agents/skills/add-part/SKILL.md](../../.agents/skills/add-part/SKILL.md); keep it in sync with this section.

- **Measuring a mesh:** `node tools/parts/probe.mjs <id> [--axis=x|y|z]` prints a part's bounding box, colours, a height map and every through-hole with its diameter and faces, in the mesh frame `componentPorts.ts` uses.

- **Parts from outside the catalog:** the catalog is compiled in, so the in-app agent contributes to a library instead. `add_sourced_item` saves a part, material or piece of equipment with its link and sources to `library/sourced-parts.json`, after the app opens the link and looks for the part number (`electron/cadImport/linkCheck.mjs`; never Amazon, which waits for the user's Add Price). `import_maker_cad` downloads the maker's STEP, IGES or zip through `publicFetch`, converts it in the CAD kernel's Chromium sandbox (`electron/cadImport/convertWorker.mjs`, §0h; the same converter opens STEP and IGES files people drop on the canvas, one part per body, §7–8), measures it, checks the size against the datasheet and turns through-holes into ports, keeping everything in `library/sourced-parts/<id>/`. `registerSourced` adds them to the runtime registries (`COMPONENT_BY_ID`, `PART_CATALOG`, `SUPPLY_BY_ID`, `COMPONENT_PORTS`, `LIST_PRICES`), so `find_parts`, `add_parts`, scene parts, ports, the BOM and the Parts panel (§5) treat them like catalog parts, and meshes load through `platformBridge.sourcedMesh`. Workers can't reach that bridge, so thumbnail and guide jobs carry the meshes they need (`sourcedMeshFiles`, `provideMeshFiles`). Re-importing replaces the mesh for new placements and thumbnails; a copy already in the scene keeps the old one until the scene reopens. A copy of the record is kept in `project.sourced`, so a shared scene keeps it, though its mesh stays in the library it came from; a record whose link isn't `https` is never registered. A maintainer promotes them with the `add-part` skill (“Promote contributed parts”), keeping the id so scenes move over untouched. `tools/parts/convert-step.mjs` and the importer share `electron/cadImport/` (`stepCore.mjs`, `partGlb.mjs`, `meshopt.mjs`, which use plain `Uint8Array`s, no `Buffer`, so they run in the sandbox too; `tools/parts/glb.test.mjs` pins their output bytes) so both produce the same mesh; the built-in reader has no native fallback, so very large files need promoting.
- **Camera angles:** a camera's field of view lives in its specs (FOV, Field of view, Depth, Color, Lens, Camera or Stereo row, as `H° × V°` or one angle, `diagonal` when it is), and §8l reads its lens from there; write the maker's numbers in that form.
- **Connector ports** (`src/data/connectorPorts.ts`, merged into `COMPONENT_PORTS`): a port of kind `connector` has a `connector` type (`5264-3` for Feetech STS, `jst-eh-3` for DYNAMIXEL X TTL, `xt60`, `ec5`, `dc-barrel`, `wire` for pins and pads) and a `role` (`bus-in`, `bus-out`, `power-in`, `power-out`). Bus servos (STS3215 and the bodies it shares, XL330, XC330, XL430, XM430, XM540) carry a `bus-in` and `bus-out` pair; adapters a `bus-out` and, when they take servo power, a `power-in`; batteries and cell holders a `power-out`; regulators both power roles. They sit on the face the maker's socket or lead is on, and `componentPorts.test.ts` fails any that's over 1 mm off the mesh. Connectors never mate: `mate` refuses them (`src/agent/mate.ts`) and `portFit` has no pair for them, so snap skips them; the wiring netlist reads them (§8b). DYNAMIXEL X TTL is JST EH (ROBOTIS EHR-03 housings), not PH. The SCS0009 and LX-16A have none: the SCS0009's lead and plug aren't on its drawing.
- **Data:** `src/data/components.ts` (one `ComponentInfo` per product: maker, part number, specs, mounting, links, model source, `pairsWith`). Every number comes from the linked datasheet or drawing. Sizes come from the converted mesh (`component-meshes.json`), so they always match what you see.
- **Meshes are meshopt-compressed, losslessly** (`electron/cadImport/meshopt.mjs`, applied by `buildPartGlb`): vertices reordered for the vertex cache, then `EXT_meshopt_compression` on float32 positions and indices. Decoded triangles are bit-identical to the uncompressed file (123 → 52 MB). Don't quantize: `componentGeometry.ts` and the parts tools read positions as float32 and bake node matrices into them. Every reader decodes: `GLTFLoader.setMeshoptDecoder` in the app, `viewBytes` in Node tools (`probe.mjs`, `bench/glb.mjs`).
- **Scene parts:** `kind: 'component'` + `componentId`. The mesh (`public/parts/<id>.glb`) loads once and is shared (`three/componentMeshes.ts`; never dispose `userData.shared` geometry). Components can't be resized (Inspector, scale gizmo and agent `update_parts` all keep their size). The Inspector skips Size, Shape and Appearance and shows a Product section last, below Notes, collapsed by default: the maker and part number, then the part page's spec rows, Mounting and 3D model blocks (`SpecRows`, `DetailBlock`, `ModelOrigin` in `components/PartDetails.tsx`, so both say the same thing the same way), and the Amazon, Datasheet and Product Page links.
- **Where meshes come from** (`tools/parts/`):
  1. **Manufacturer CAD first.** `sources.json` lists each official STEP (URL, license, orientation). `node tools/parts/convert-step.mjs <id>|--all` tessellates it with OpenCascade (`occt-import-js`), keeps face colors, turns Z-up into Y-up and writes the GLB. Raw downloads live in git-ignored `tools/parts/cad/`.
     - `"files": [...]` combines several files that share one assembly frame (an enclosure and its PCBA). Check the result: files exported in their own origins don't line up.
     - Files too big for WebAssembly, or IGES whose trimmed surfaces fail, use `"reader": "native"` (`step-native.py`, native OpenCascade through `cadquery-ocp`; point `OCP_PYTHON` at that Python).
     - Makers that publish a robot description instead of an assembly (URDF/MJCF + STL, or one fused STL) go through `node tools/parts/convert-urdf.mjs <id>`: it assembles the meshes at the zero pose. `"rootLink"` takes just the gripper of a full arm; `"modelColors": false` ignores simulation debug colors.
     - Extra bodies (field-of-view cones, keep-out volumes, spare accessories laid out beside the product, the rest of an arm around its gripper): `"hideMeshes"` drops bodies by name, `"keepBox"` keeps only what lies inside a box in the file's own frame.
     - Colors: CAD "no color assigned" defaults (SolidWorks lavender `#cad1ee`, `#97a3da`) become neutral grey; `"colorMap"` sets a real finish for any CAD color (e.g. Trossen's red/blue part colors → black).
     - Orientation: set `"rotate"` so the part sits the way it's mounted (servo horn up, boards component side up, cameras lenses toward +Z, grippers and hands flange down with fingers up), then check front, back, top and side renders.
     - When a published file is incomplete (e.g. no housing surfaces), don't ship it: model the part from the official drawing instead (step 2) and record why in `sources.json` (`note`).
  2. **No official CAD:** the studio's own agent models it from the official drawings. `tools/parts/specs/<id>/` holds the record and drawing images; `node tools/parts/generate.mjs <id…>` (random free port, so stale tabs on familiar ports can't attach) runs the real app headless (like the benchmark) and saves the GLB plus the part code, renders and the agent's notes in `tools/parts/generated/<id>/`. Never hand-model or estimate a component.
  - Community models (GrabCAD, Thingiverse, other robots' repos) don't count as manufacturer CAD.
  - **Checked CAD:** when the maker publishes only drawings (Raspberry Pi 4) or the file comes from a reseller (Unitree GO-M8010-6), a third-party STEP may ship if it's measured against the official drawing first: board or body outline, mounting holes and connector or flange positions within about 0.1 mm. It gets `model.source: 'checked'` with `checkedAgainst: '<maker>'`, and its 3D model line says “By ⟨author⟩, checked against the ⟨maker⟩ drawing”. If no file passes, model it from the drawing (step 2).
- **Amazon links:** a verified product page (`kind: 'product'`) when the exact part is listed, else a search (`kind: 'search'`, labeled Find on Amazon). Never guess an ASIN. Hardware tiles link to an Amazon search for the exact spec (`hardwareShopUrl`).
- **Groups added for the whole loop:** Power (Pololu regulators, MPD 18650 holders, and battery packs and cells), Wheels (Pololu, goBILDA, REV, Pololu ball casters), Linear Actuators (Actuonix, Progressive Automations) and Displays & Audio (round and rectangular displays, Pi Touch Display 2, ReSpeaker mic arrays, speakers, I2S amp).
  - Power parts carry typed data the checks read: `battery` (nominal and full-charge volts, Ah, continuous C, `cell` format for single cells), `regulator` (input range, output volts and amps) and `holder` (cell format, count, series or parallel).
  - Gear-ratio versions (Actuonix) are `variants` with no `actuator`; a version's list price falls back to the base part's.
- **Servo fits:** a servo's `ActuatorSpec.fit` (`src/data/servoFits.ts`) is a published BAM model M6 identification, tagged with the bus voltage it was fitted at, and it drives the sim's actuator model (§8d). Copy fits exactly from [BAM's params](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/tree/main/bam/params) (`m6.json`, plus `kp`, `error_gain` and `max_pwm` from the actuator class in `bam/<maker>/actuator.py`), never round or refit them, and attach one only to the exact product and variant it was measured on (gear ratio and voltage matter: STS3215 C001 has one, C018, C044 and C046 don't). Today: Feetech STS3215 C001 (7.4 V) and Dynamixel XL330-M288 (BAM's XL330 class says 7.5 V). BAM also publishes MX-64, MX-106, XL-320, Waveshare ST3025 and eRob fits, and Feetech's [ftservo-bam](https://gitee.com/ftservo/ftservo-bam) adds HD1910; add them with the part. A servo with no fit keeps the estimated model, and Build ▸ Checks names it in the Servo models row (§8b).
  - Still without maker CAD (download blocked, behind a sign-in or terms, or never published): ODrive S1 and Micro (Onshape only), Jetson AGX Orin dev kit (NVIDIA sign-in), CANable 2.0, mjbots fdcanusb, Same Sky AMT10 encoders (terms), Omron D2F switches, ATI and Bota F/T sensors, Interlink FSR 402, Keystone 1121 21700 holder, Seeed Round Display for XIAO. Add them only through §8a's pipeline.
  - `tools/parts/generate.mjs` has `battery` (flat, long side along X) and `cell` (on its side along X) poses for packs and cells modeled from their drawings.
- **Generated actuators** (`src/actuator/`; placed by Make an Actuator, §8b): a printed cycloidal reducer (`cycloidal_drive`, §9 Recipes) on the catalog's one bare BLDC motor, the ODrive D6374 150KV, driven by an mjbots moteus-c1 or moteus r4.11 (`motors.ts` holds their current limits). The motor part carries a version id no catalog entry lists, `cycloidal-<ratio>-<driver>` (`cycloidal-15-moteus-r4`), for ratios 10, 12, 15 and 20. `componentVariant` falls back to `cycloidalVariant` (`generated.ts`), which builds the version's label, part number and `ActuatorSpec` from the id, so `actuatorOf`, Checks, the BOM, the MJCF writer and Forge read it like any version, and a saved scene reloads with the same spec.
  - The spec is an estimate, and its `source` says so: peak torque = Kt (8.27 / Kv, 0.055 N·m/A at 150 Kv) × the lower of the motor's 90 A and the driver's peak × ratio × 0.9; rated torque uses the motor's 50 A and the driver's continuous current; speed = Kv × 24 V / ratio; `armature` = rotor inertia × ratio². ODrive doesn't publish the D6374's rotor inertia, so 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m² stands in (a similar 63 mm outrunner measures 1.06 × 10⁻⁴). 10:1 on a moteus-c1 gives 9.92 N·m peak, 6.94 N·m rated and 360 rpm; 15:1 on a moteus r4.11 gives 66.99 N·m, 23.82 N·m, 240 rpm and 0.02475 kg·m²; 20:1 on the r4.11 peaks at 89.32 N·m.
  - The reducer (`cycloidal.ts`, design math and sampled interference checks): lobes = ratio, ring pins = ratio + 1, fixed in the housing; two discs 180° apart with 0.15 mm print clearance to the pins; 6 output pins take the output from the discs, so it turns once per `ratio` input turns. The housing is about 85 mm across and 35 mm from the motor face to the output face; with the motor and driver the actuator is about 120 mm long. One motor means it's oversized for small robots, and the printed reducer has no measured backlash or efficiency.
- Estimated generic parts (servo, stepper, board, camera, ultrasonic, hinge, linear actuator, L/U-brackets, beam, standoff, gripper, wheel, bearing block) live in `BLOCKOUT_ONLY`: the agent can block out with them and old scenes still render them, but Parts never lists them. Add a part to Parts only when its geometry is exact.
