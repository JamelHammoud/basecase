# Joints, poses and motion

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 8c. Joints, Poses and Motion

A robot is posable once its degrees of freedom are declared. Posing never edits parts: the modeled pose is the design, and a pose only changes what the canvas shows.

- **Data** (`types/robotics.ts`, saved with the scene in `ProjectSettings`): `joints` (`RobotJoint`: revolute in degrees or prismatic in mm, parent and child parts, `parentJoint` when links between joints have no geometry, origin and axis, range, limb), `pose` (the pose on the canvas: values by joint id plus an optional whole-body `root` offset), `poses` (saved) and `animations` (`RobotAnimation`: keyframes with every animated joint, smooth or linear, loop).
- **Several robots per scene:** `buildRig` finds each robot (joints linked through a parent joint or a shared part, plus unlisted parts grouped with one robot's parts) as `rig.robots` (key = its first base joint id, name = its parts' group). The scene pose holds every robot's joint values and each robot's body offset in `roots` by key; `robotRig` gives one robot alone for IK, gaits and mocap, and `withRobotPose` puts its result back without touching the others. Saved poses and animations carry `robot`; the Pose tab, ⌘K Rest Pose, walk cycles and Import BVH work on the selected robot only, and the agent tools take `robot` (default: the selected one, or the only one). Imported robots get unique joint ids (a second G1's are `left_knee_joint_2`), and scenes saved with repeated ids are renumbered when they open (`uniqueJointIds`).
- **Declaring joints by hand** (`src/joints/`): select two parts, parent first and child second, and run Add Hinge or Add Slider (right-click on the selection, or ⌘K; both appear only with exactly two parts selected). One module builds every joint: `declare.ts` (`jointProblem`, `jointFrom`, `withJoints` with first ranges) serves the agent's `define_joints` (`declareJoints` in `workspace/agentBuildRequests.ts`) and the hand-made joint (`handJoint`), so both give the same `RobotJoint`. The axis comes from `axisFinder.ts`, run in the analysis worker (`jointAxisIn`, the viewport's `jointAxis`, one cached `MeshIndex` per part and pose):
  - `MeshIndex` welds the part's world triangles (1 µm), links triangles across shared edges and marks a triangle curved when a neighbour's normal turns between 0.25° and 65°. Flat faces and 90° creases are never curved, so a box face gives no axis.
  - A round face is the curved triangles reachable from the clicked one without crossing a 65° crease. `fitCylinder` takes the axis as the least eigenvector of the area-weighted normal moments (normals must lie in one plane), fits the centre and radius as a least-squares circle of the face's vertices in the plane across the axis, and accepts it when the vertices sit within max(0.05 mm, 2% of the radius); otherwise it drops the triangles off the circle and refits, up to three times. A clicked face needs 30° of arc.
  - With no click, `coaxialPair` looks for a shaft (convex, at least 180° of arc) on one part inside a bore (concave) on the other: parallel within 1°, axes within max(0.5 mm, 5% of the radius), radii within max(1 mm, 20%), overlapping along the axis. The origin is the middle of the overlap. Two bores with no shaft between them propose nothing.
  - The agent's `find_joint_axes` (`jointCandidates.ts`, the analysis worker's `jointAxes` over the parts it names, every visible solid part by default) runs `coaxialPair` on every pair of parts. A shaft part in two collinear bores on two other parts is a pin: it proposes one joint between those two, naming the pin. Two parts joined on two or more distinct lines (bolts, dowels) are reported as fixed and propose nothing. Parent and child follow the joint graph out from the part with the lowest bottom (the biggest box breaks a tie within 1 mm); fixed pairs carry the walk across. Each candidate gives axis, origin, Ø, engaged length, slide travel and a confidence: the fit (axis offset and clearance against their tolerances) times the engaged share of the radius, capped at 1. Round faces with no partner (up to 3 per part, at least as long as their radius) are listed too. Answers are in the agent frame (z up), and the prompt rigs an import from them, then sweeps each range with `animate`. Planar slides (rails, dovetails) aren't found.
  - When nothing is proposed, the canvas waits for a click on a round face of either part: the cursor is a crosshair over one and not-allowed elsewhere, the gizmo hides, clicks don't change the selection, and Esc or a new selection stops it.
  - The axis points along its largest component. A hinge's range is ±90°; a slider's is the travel that keeps the shorter part within the longer one along the axis (`slideTravel`). The joint takes the child's name and an id `hinge_N` / `slider_N`. It goes in as one undo step (`commitJoints` in `useSceneDocument`), the child is selected and the Pose tab opens on the joint, whose ring turns the child.
  - In the Pose tab, right-click a joint row for Range ▸ (two fields, lowest and highest; each change is one undo step) and Delete, which asks first (“Delete ⟨name⟩?”) and is one undo step. A range drag that never ends in a final change still becomes its own undo step as soon as anything else is edited or undone.
- **Joints follow their mount:** `frame` stores the pivot in its first parent part's own frame (`anchorJoint`); `resolveJoints` reads it back, so moving or undoing the robot keeps joints in place.
- **Kinematics** (`utils/kinematics.ts`): `buildRig` turns joints into a tree; each part moves with the deepest joint that lists it as a child, a joint's mount moves with the joint above it, and an unlisted part follows its group only when the whole group moves with one joint (a group spanning a whole robot says nothing). `forwardKinematics` composes rest-frame rotations from the base out. `solveIK` is damped least squares (Levenberg–Marquardt) with joint limits; torso joints are stiffer so limbs reach first; `preBend` starts knees and elbows bent the way their range opens (straight joints are singular).
- **Linkages** (`src/linkage/`, `types/loopClosure.ts`): a four-bar from Sketch's Path mode or the agent's `synthesize_linkage` (§8, §9) is four printed bars compiled from the `four_bar` recipe at the drawn start (`cad.pending` until `useCadCompile` compiles them), grouped as Four-Bar, plus a Feetech STS3215 mated coaxially under the ground's pivot A (`planMate`). The whole mechanism is lifted so the servo stands on the sketch's plane with the bars above it. The crank is a revolute joint with that servo as its actuator, ranged over the drawn sweep, so the torque and sweep checks see it like any servo joint. The coupler (at B, under the crank joint) and the rocker (at D, on the ground) are fixed joints with `loop`: `forwardKinematics` turns each by the angle that closes the loop at the crank's value (`kinematicsLoop.ts`, the same open branch as `four_bar_pose`), so Pose, animations, sweeps of the pose and the canvas move the whole linkage. Everything else reads them as fixed: the Pose tab has no row for them, and the sweep check turns the coupler rigidly with the crank. Closed loops aren't simulated (§8d). `uniqueJointIds` renames `loop.driver` with the ids it renames.
- **Grippers** (`src/cases/gripper.ts`, `findGripper`): two movable leaf joints under the same joint whose parts face each other count as a gripper's jaws, when each one's motion at its part (a slide's axis, or a hinge's swing) points within about 45° of the other part and its range has a limit on that side, which is its closing limit; the joint above them is the palm. `robotFacts` reports it as `gripper` (jaws with open 0 and closed limit, the point between the jaws' parts, approach and across directions, the opening between their inner faces, finger depth and the palm-to-centre length) and the `gripper` capability, leaves the jaws out of the arm's chain, and makes the point between the jaws the end effector. A gripper that is one catalog part with no jaw joints isn't one in simulation, since nothing would close. The fixture `arm-6dof` carries a palm and two 5 N sliding fingers 50 mm apart.
- **Payload** (§8b, `utils/payload.ts`): a fixed arm's chain is sampled inside its joint ranges, value 0 being the modeled pose, turning each joint about its rest axis from the base out as `forwardKinematics` does, with every link's parts lumped at their centre of mass.
- **Fasteners follow what they clamp** (`src/assembly/followers.ts`, `rigJoints.ts`): in verify, poses and the sim, a screw moves with the deepest joint whose moving set holds everything it clamps, ignoring that joint's own servo (the horn-screw exception); nuts follow their screw, inserts their host. Joints never list fasteners. On Ribbit it reproduced the agent's 672 hand-listed moving screws on all but one fixed pseudo-joint.
- **Joints belong to their parts.** Deleting parts deletes every joint whose moving side is gone and drops the deleted ids from the rest (`jointsWithoutParts` in `kinematics.ts`, run by every `commitParts`); undo brings parts and joints back together.
- **Undo:** pose changes are on the scene's undo timeline. Each history step (`Snapshot` in `SceneWorkspace`) holds the parts, the joints, the pose, the marks and the skeleton; `usePosing` reports every pose edit (sliders, the ring, saved poses, Rest pose, the agent's `pose`) through `onPoseEdit`, and edits less than 700 ms apart fold into one step, so a drag undoes in one go. Playback, scrubbing and pausing aren't recorded.
- **Canvas:** `Viewport3D` takes per-part rest → posed motion (`pose`). Measurements, interference (check_scene), servo outputs and sweeps run in the modeled pose (`withRest`); renders show the pose. Moving a part while posed maps the edit back to the modeled pose.
- **Pose tab** (right panel, beside Inspect, while the selection is on a robot or the scene has a skeleton, or while Edit Skeleton is on: `posable` in `usePoseScope`; P opens it, selecting the first robot if needed). The Skeleton section heads it (Edit Skeleton, below); the robot sections show only while the selection is on a robot. It shows that robot's animations, poses and joints (right-click a joint row for Range ▸ and Delete): while it's open, clicking a part picks the joint that turns it, and a single ring on its axis (an arrow for a slider) turns it in 1° / 1 mm steps (Shift is free). Picking a toolbar tool leaves the tab. Contents: Animations (play, scrub, + → Walk Cycle or Import BVH, or straight to Import BVH when the robot has no legs; right-click for Loop, Rename, Delete), Poses (Rest pose and Save pose in the header; click applies; right-click for Apply, Rename, Delete), then one collapsible section per limb (Head, Torso, Left arm…) with label · slider · value rows. A limb's rows share one grid (`grid-cols-subgrid`), so the label column fits the longest label with its Measured badge (§8k), up to 60% of the width, and every slider in the section starts at the same x. Animations and poses rename from the right-click menu only: a click already loads the animation or applies the pose, so there's no double-click rename.
- **Edit Skeleton** (`workspace/useSkeletonEdit.ts`, `components/pose/SkeletonSection.tsx`; the Skeleton section's switch at the top of the Pose tab, or ⌘K Edit Skeleton, which also opens the tab): edits `project.skeleton`, the plan the CAD agent designs from (§9 Relations), as a stick figure. With the switch on, the canvas draws the skeleton (§12) and the section lists every joint with its torque margin; in a scene with no skeleton it offers Arm and Biped, which come from the recipe templates (`armSkeleton` / `bipedSkeleton` in `electron/cad-mcp/recipes/templates.mjs`, the joint frames at each servo's horn, the STS3215 actuators and an estimated mass, read through `agent/skeletonTemplates.ts`), then you ask the agent to build around it.
  - **Dragging a joint ball** (`agent/skeletonDrag.ts`, `dragJoint`) moves its origin and its subtree (tips included) in 1 mm steps (Shift is free) on the plane facing the camera; ⌥ moves the joint alone, so the bones on both sides change length. The paired joint (`skeleton.pairs`) moves the mirror way, with its subtree. Parts framed to a moved joint follow through `framedFollowOps` as live edits, and the end of the drag records one history step (`recordSkeletonEdit`), so ⌘Z restores the skeleton and the parts together.
  - **Margins** (`utils/skeletonMargins.ts`): `skeletonSizing` gives each joint's stance and hold loads from link lengths, actuator weights and `massG`; `jointVerdict` (the same grading `size_actuators` uses) rates them against the joint's actuator at its rated voltage. The margin is the share of stall torque left at the binding load, shown as a percentage beside the joint and as the ball's colour (pass, warn, fail; `MARGIN_COLORS` in `workspace/skeletonView.ts`); a joint with no actuator, or a skeleton with no mass, shows none. It recomputes on every drag step: eight joints take well under a millisecond, so it stays on the main thread (§0f). Under a subtree drag a joint's own load holds (everything it carries moved with it) and its parent's changes; a ⌥ drag changes both.
  - **Changing servos:** clicking a ball or a row opens a menu with the next stronger catalog servo on the same bus (`servoUp`, over 5% more stall torque, cheapest first among equals); picking it writes the joint's `actuator`, the field `define_skeleton` sets and `size_actuators` reads, as one undo step.
- **Size an arm** (`utils/armSizing.ts`, pure, in a worker through `src/arm/armSizingClient.ts`; ⌘K or right-click Size Arm to Targets with 1 to 8 Guide parts selected (role `reference`, reference images aside), or the agent's `size_arm`, §9): sizes the `arm_template` arm (base yaw, shoulder, elbow and wrist pitch, §9 Recipes) from the points its tool flange must reach and the grams it carries there. The sheet lists each target as its part's center in mm and takes the Payload in grams (200 to start).
  - **Search** (`armSearch.ts`): link lengths from 75 mm in 5 mm steps (up to 400) and a riser under the base from 0 in 5 mm steps (up to 300), shortest total first, ties to the lower riser, then the shorter first links. The base stands at the origin, turned to face the middle of the targets' bearings (`facingYaw`). Each candidate is screened by `armReach.ts`, a closed form for the arm's plane: the yaw that faces a target (the flange sits 8 mm off the arm's plane), then a scan of the flange's pitch in 2° steps with two-link IK for the shoulder and elbow, every joint at least 10% of its range inside its limits. The first candidate that passes is confirmed with `solveIK` on the skeleton's own rig (`utils/skeletonRig.ts`, ranges shrunk by 10% each side, seeded from the closed form): every target within 2 mm, or the search goes on. `armReach.armTool` matches the rig's forward kinematics and the template's flange (`armSizing.test.ts`). A target no arm on the grid reaches, or a search past 400,000 screens, gives no arm and names the target (the farthest one when no single target is to blame). About 30 ms for three targets.
  - **Servos** (`armServos.ts`): with the payload at the flange (`payloadG` on the wrist joint's tip, which `skeletonSizing` adds as a point mass) and each link at 15 g + 0.35 g/mm, `sizeActuators` picks per joint the cheapest servo that passes, among the ones in the STS3215 outline the links are cut for (STS3215 versions, STS3250, ST3215-HS), at the cheapest supply where every joint passes (§8b). No passing servo gives no arm and names the joint. The same input always gives the same arm.
  - **Result** (`src/arm/armBuild.ts`, `placeArm`): one undo step (`commitSkeleton`) writes the skeleton through `buildSkeleton` (what `define_skeleton` runs, replacing any skeleton), places each picked servo at its joint and, on desktop, the `arm_template` parts at the solved lengths (Arm Base, Riser when there is one, Shoulder Bracket, Upper Arm, Forearm, Wrist Flange) as `cad.pending` boxes that `useCadCompile` compiles through the kernel pool. The web demo places the skeleton and servos only. Every part is grouped as Arm and framed to its joint, so dragging a joint carries them. Edit Skeleton then opens on it with the margins live. Links aren't checked for clearance against the table or each other.
- **Animation** (`utils/animation.ts`): `sampleAnimation` (Catmull-Rom or linear; whole-body turns slerp), `buildKeyframes` (values and IK goals per keyframe), `buildGait` (any number of feet; stance feet stay planted, swing feet lift; stride, lift, period, duty, crouch, bob, sway, heading and oscillators default from the leg length). Playback runs in `usePosing` and drives the canvas directly; pausing commits the frame as the pose.
- **Animations as moves:** in the sim an animation plays as position targets, which a fixed arm follows but a legged robot usually can't (a squat, a bow or a dance step tips it over). Every animation of a legged robot (two or more keyframes, its own joints or `robot`) is also a row in Simulate's Train section, named after it: Train has Forge learn a policy that performs it under physics, and Arena judges it against the animation itself (§8i, Moves). The animation is sampled once per 20 ms control step; a looping one repeats, and one that doesn't loop holds its last key. Editing the animation marks its move for training again.
- **Posable hands** (`data/articulatedParts.ts`): catalog parts whose maker publishes a URDF / MJCF with the joints (Amazing Hand, LEAP, ORCA, Ability, Dex3-1, Dex5-1, Allegro, Linker L10 and O6) go in from Parts → Hands (click, + or drag) as that articulated model: links as `mesh` parts, fingers as joints, turned by the same `rotate` as their catalog mesh (`importRobot`'s `rotate` option uses tools/parts' `(x, z, −y)` frame), framed and selected. The palm carries `componentId`, so the Inspector shows Product and the BOM counts the hand once. If the description can't be downloaded, the fixed catalog mesh goes in instead. Hands without a published description (Inspire, HOPEJr, Aero) stay one mesh.
- **The maker's data is never missing:** an import captures everything the checks and tools use (link masses and centres of mass, joint ranges, torque and speed limits), and every link records where it came from (`ScenePart.robotLink`: robot, link, source link, `rotate`, data `version`). When imports start capturing something new: add it to `publishedData` (it feeds both the import and the upgrade) and bump `ROBOT_DATA_VERSION`. Robots below the current version are upgraded from their description alone (no meshes), matched by link and joint name so poses and edits are kept: open scenes when they load (undo history included), saved scenes in the background when the app starts (saved back in place, `keepUpdatedAt`). Older robots without a recorded source are found by name (a preset, a Parts hand, Robots → Imported); one that can't be found gets a toast to insert it again. Animations report joints faster than their speed limit.
- **Character shells** (`src/wrap/`): Wrap Robot (§7a) turns an imported mesh (`mesh.file`) and one jointed robot into one shell per link. `wrapPlan` splits the robot into links (the base, then one per revolute or prismatic joint; parts on a fixed joint stay with the link above it). In the wrap worker (`wrap.worker.ts`, Manifold), `wrapRobot` then:
  - fits the mesh to the robot's rest bounds (uniform scale and centring, 6 mm margin on each side) and trims it at the floor when the robot stands on it;
  - hollows it to a 2 mm wall: the outer surface is the mesh itself when it's a closed manifold (flipped if inside out), otherwise a level set of its winding-number distance field, and the inner surface is the level set 2 mm in, simplified to 0.1 mm;
  - cuts it at a plane through each joint's origin, square to the line from the parent link's centre to the child's, turned to lie across the axis of a joint that turns along that line (a pan or roll) and to contain the axis otherwise; sibling joints are split by the bisector of their origins;
  - carves away the robot's parts at rest, with a cell of clearance, then for each joint, base first: the sweep of the moving side's parts over the whole range and of its shells over half the range from the fixed side's shells, and the reverse sweep of the fixed side's parts and shells over the whole range from the moving shells. Splitting the shells' range that way leaves both sides of a joint with material; carving only ever removes, so each joint stays clear after the next ones are carved. A designed, non-leg joint's moving shells also lose what would dip below the floor;
  - drops pieces under 20 mm³.

  Sweeps are exact over the continuous range: `SweepRaster` bins surface samples by (radius, height) about the axis and tests the arc a point sweeps against each bin's sorted angles (along the axis for a slide). Carve fields are sampled on a grid, dilated by a cell and blurred before Manifold's `levelSet` (`gridField.ts`), because a binary field with a one-cell-thin sheet crashes it (`memory access out of bounds`); each cutter is limited to a band around the shells it carves (`nearSurface`). Grids hold about 800,000 points, so the cell grows with the mesh (1.2 mm at least). Each shell is a printed `mesh` part named after the mesh and its joint, coloured like the mesh, in the robot's group; it's added to its joint's `child`, and the base shell to the root joints' `parent`, so it poses, animates, simulates and weighs like a link part. The mesh is hidden, and the parts and joints change in one undo step. The SO-101 takes about 20–35 s.
- **Published robots** (`utils/robotImport.ts`): URDF, xacro and MJCF (defaults classes, includes, hinge chains on one body, visual geoms) with STL / OBJ / DAE / GLB meshes from a link, a GitHub or Hugging Face repo, a zip or a folder (Git LFS works; `package://` is resolved against the repository). Each link with geometry becomes a `mesh` part (simplified with meshoptimizer toward a ~220k-triangle budget, never past 0.2% error, so cracked published meshes keep more triangles instead of tearing; not printed, not resized); every revolute / prismatic joint keeps its axis and limits. Robot frame (REP-103) → scene: `(y, z, x) × 1000`. Collada keeps the file's own axes. Every import from a link renders a preview (`robotPreviewImage` in `utils/robotPreview.ts`: the Library's thumbnail renderer, cropped to the robot, WebP) after the robot is inserted and remembers it (`localStorage` `robots-imported`). Presets ship theirs in `public/robots/<id>.webp`, made by the same import: `node tools/robots/previews.mjs [id…]` (rerun it when a preset is added). Presets (`ROBOT_PRESETS`, each checked to import whole): Unitree G1, H1, H1-2, Apptronik Apollo, Booster T1, Berkeley Humanoid; ROBOTIS OP3, Open Duck Mini v2; Unitree Go2, Go1, B2, ANYmal C, Spot; SO-101, Franka Panda, UR5e (the Robots view, ⌘K, File → Import Robot). MJCF `<frame>` elements are followed, collision-class or group-3 geoms are skipped, and a `<mesh>` given inline by its `vertex` points (as Export ▸ Robot Package's `robot.xml` writes them, §8d) becomes their convex hull, the shape MuJoCo collides with.
- **Motion capture** (`utils/mocap.ts`): BVH through three's `BVHLoader`, bones matched to landmarks by name (Mixamo, CMU, Daz `lShldr`, UE `upperarm_l`…), robot joints matched by name (`left_knee`, `LL_KFE`, `FL_calf`…). Retargeting points each robot segment the way the performer's segment points (so proportions and rest poses can differ), moves the body by the hips scaled by leg length, and keeps feet on the floor while the performer stands. Import (§7) reads a BVH when it's picked: one that can't be parsed gives a toast naming it and opens nothing. Otherwise a sheet titled Import ⟨name⟩, with the clip's length beside it, shows the match (changeable), In place, Loop and frame rate; Import retargets and adds the animation. Whether the animation loops is its right-click Loop in the Pose tab, like any other animation.
- **Retargeting frame by frame** (`utils/retargeter.ts`, free of the DOM and three scenes, so it runs in a worker): `createRetargeter({ rig, pointOf, bones?, joints?, upperBody?, inPlace?, rest?, restFrames? })` gives `step(human, t, rootQuaternion?)`. `human` maps keypoints (`mocapBones` `KEYPOINTS`, or the source's own names through `bones`) to positions in one unit throughout (metres for a live source). Each step warm-starts from the last and returns the joint values (clamped to their limits), the body offset and a keyframe at `t`. Scale and facing come from a rest frame: `rest` when given (a BVH's bind pose), otherwise the mean of the first `restFrames` (10) frames whose keypoints all stay within 3% of the body's size; `step` returns null until then. Without `rootQuaternion` the body turns with the hip line (or the shoulders) and the spine, measured against the rest frame. `upperBody` holds every leg joint at its rest bend (`preBend`) and the body still, so only the arms, waist and head follow a camera that sees a person's top half. A keypoint the rest frame had but a step lacks holds its whole limb (or the waist or head) at the last values, and the body offset too when the hips go. `retarget` (the Import BVH sheet, `retarget_motion`) is a loop over `step` through the clip, then grounds the feet; `mocapRetarget.test.ts` pins its output on a G1-style fixture (`mocap.fixtures.ts`). One step on that 29-joint fixture costs 0.6 ms of CPU on average and 1.8 ms on its slowest frame (Node 26, Apple M5 Pro; upper body 0.2 ms and 0.6 ms).
- **Where robots come from** (`utils/robotImport/`): one import, one field (Import Robot sheet). It takes a link to a `.urdf`, `.xacro`, `.xml` / `.mjcf` or `.zip`, a GitHub repo, folder or Download ZIP link (`github.com/o/r`, `…/tree/<ref>/<folder>`, `…/archive/<ref>.zip`: the file list comes from the GitHub trees API, files from `raw.githubusercontent.com`, only paths in that list are read), a Hugging Face Hub model or dataset repo, folder or file link (`huggingface.co/o/r`, `…/datasets/o/r`, `…/tree/<rev>/<folder>`, `…/blob|resolve/<rev>/<file>`, `hub.ts`: the file list comes from the Hub's public tree API `api/<models|datasets>/o/r/tree/<rev>?recursive=true`, following its `Link` next page only on that same API path; files from `…/resolve/<rev>/<path>`, only paths in that list are read; a file link lists the repo and imports that file only, never another robot in the repo, and fails naming the link when a complete listing lacks it; the listing stops at 20 pages, after which a file link still reads its file straight and a repo or folder link fails as unopened; a `.zip` file is downloaded like any zip link; `datasets`, `spaces`, `docs` and the Hub's other top-level pages aren't repos), a chosen folder, or a folder, zip, `.urdf`, `.xacro`, `.mjcf` or `.xml` dropped on the canvas or picked with Import (§7): those import at once, with no sheet, like every other dropped file, and the robot lands at the drop point (`useCanvasDrop` → `droppedFolders.ts`, which walks dropped folders and skips `.git` / `node_modules`; an `.xml` that isn't a description gets the import's error toast). Links, zips and folders become one file tree (`tree.ts`), and `chooseEntry` (`entry.ts`) picks the description: an MJCF with a `<worldbody>` that isn't a `scene*.xml`, then a URDF (a `.urdf`, or an `.xml` whose root is `<robot>`), then a scene, then a xacro (below), then an MJCF whose bodies sit in its `<include>`s. A plain URDF beside its xacro still wins. Among `.xacro` / `.urdf.xacro` files whose root is `<robot>`, the import takes the top-level one: files another xacro includes (matched by file name) drop out, then those in `urdf/`, `description/` or a `*_description/` folder and those named after their `<robot name>` or package win, then the shallowest. A link with no description fails with one toast naming the link (`noRobotAt`); a repo that can't be listed (missing or private) names it too (`unopenedRepo`). ROS package files (`package.xml`, launch, config, rviz, worlds, tests) never count, and only the shallowest candidates compete. One left: it imports. Several at the same depth: the sheet opens with the list (`RobotChoiceError` → `robotChoice`), and the pick comes back as the source's `entry`. The agent's `import_robot` gets the list as text and passes `entry`. A part's `robotLink.url` is the file the import read, so a repo import upgrades like a file link, and Robots → Imported remembers that file, not the repo. **Xacro** (fusion2urdf packages, ROS description repos) is expanded in the page before `urdf.ts` reads it (`xacro.ts`, with `xml.ts`, `xacroScope.ts` and `xacroExpr.ts`): `xacro:include` (read through the import's own resolver; `$(find pkg)` becomes `package://pkg`), `property` (value, default, block, `scope`), `macro` with params (`:=` defaults, `^` and `^|` from the caller, `*` and `**` blocks), `insert_block`, `if` / `unless`, `call`, `element`, `arg` defaults, `$(arg)`, `$(find)`, `$(dirname)`, `$(optenv)` with a default, `$(eval)`, `$$` escapes and `${}` expressions. Expressions are Python's arithmetic, comparisons, `and` / `or` / `not`, `a if c else b`, strings, `pi` and a fixed list of math functions (`radians`, `sin`, `atan2`, `min`…), parsed by `xacroExpr.ts`, never `eval`. Root attributes are read after the body, so `<robot name="$(arg name)">` works. Anything else (`xacro.load_yaml`, `$(env)`, namespaced includes, `xacro:attribute`, lists and dicts, any other call) fails the import with `Couldn’t read <file>`, naming the file it was in; nothing is skipped silently. gkjohnson's `xacro-parser` was checked and not used: it needs DOM nodes, pulls in `yaml` and `yargs`, and evaluates through `expr-eval-fork`, a general expression language (its parent `expr-eval` had a code-execution advisory), where this importer wants a parser that knows only arithmetic and fails closed.
- **Onshape assemblies** (`electron/onshape/`, `src/onshape/`): the same Import Robot field takes an Onshape assembly tab's link (`https://cad.onshape.com/documents/<id>/w|v/<id>/e/<id>`, `isOnshapeLink`; the desktop app only, so the web demo's sheet doesn't accept one), and `loadRobot` hands it to `loadOnshape` instead of the URDF reader. The main process does the fetching with the API keys from Settings ▸ Accounts (§6a): the assembly definition (occurrence transforms, instances, mates, mate groups, subassemblies' mates under their occurrence path), the mate features (for limits), the document name, and per distinct part its mass properties and a STEP export (a translation, polled, then downloaded), converted to bodies by the CAD kernel's sandboxed converter (`openCadFile`, §0h) and sent as plain GLB (`partMesh.mjs`, simplified toward 600k triangles across the robot; a part whose bodies simplify away gets no visual). `assembly.mjs` maps it to a converted model (`format: 'basecase-onshape'`, the SimReady path's `importRobot({ kind: 'converted' })`): parts held by a fastened mate or a mate group fuse into one link (one scene part, a visual per Onshape part); a revolute mate becomes a hinge and a slider a slide, its axis the mate connector's Z, its limits the mate's (`limitAxialZMin/Max`, `limitZMin/Max`) measured from where the assembly sits, so 0 in Pose is the pose it was modelled in. Limits come from the root assembly's `/features` only, so a revolute or slider mate inside a subassembly imports with no range. The fixed occurrence is the base; parts no mate reaches hang off it, fixed. Any other mate (cylindrical, ball, pin slot, planar…) becomes a fixed joint, and the part it holds keeps its name in `robotLink.fixedMates`, which Checks lists as one Fixed Mates note per mate (`fixedMateFindings`). A mate that closes a loop (a four-bar's last pin, a gripper's second finger link) or joins two parts already fused is left out: a rig is a tree, so it can't be a joint, and nothing about it is lost. A part whose Onshape material gives it mass gets `massG` and its centre of mass; one without keeps none. `electron/onshape/onshape.test.mjs` drives the whole path against a fake Onshape API built in code (`e2e/fakeOnshape.ts`), and `e2e/onshapeImport.e2e.test.ts` pastes a link into Import Robot and poses the hinge.
- **Update from Onshape** (`src/onshape/`, `workspace/useOnshapeUpdate.ts`): an Onshape import keeps its source as ids only (`assemblyRobot` → `withOnshapeIds`): the robot's group carries `group.onshape` (`did`, `w|v`, its id, `eid`), each part `robotLink.onshape` (the Onshape occurrence paths fused into it, from the converted link's `occurrences`), each joint `onshape.mate` (the mate feature's id, from the converted joint's `mate`). Update from Onshape, in the group's right-click menu (one part or the selected group) and ⌘K (the selected group, else the scene's only Onshape robot; desktop only), rebuilds the link from those ids (`onshapeLinkOf`, which refuses anything but 24-hex ids, so a crafted scene file never reaches a request) and runs the same import, then `planOnshapeUpdate` diffs it against the scene by Onshape id. A fresh link matches the scene part that holds any of its occurrences; the placement is the transform from a matched part whose mesh didn't change (else any matched part), so a robot moved or turned in the scene stays put. A matched part whose mesh, size, place, mass or link record changed is replaced under its old id with the new geometry and transform, keeping its name, colour, material and everything else the person set; new links come in as parts of the same group; parts whose Onshape occurrences are gone are deleted. Joints match by mate id: axis, origin, range, type and ends come from the mate (re-anchored with `anchorJoint`), and the joint keeps its id, `actuators` (servo assignment), effort and limb, so poses and calibrations keyed to it carry on. Parts and ids the person added (a servo in a joint's `parent`, fasteners, cameras) stay. A joint whose mate is gone keeps everything and gets `onshape.gone`, which Checks lists as Missing Mates, naming the joint (`goneMateFindings`, a note); the mate coming back clears it. The update is one commit (`commitParts` then the joints), so one ⌘Z reverts it; an unchanged document changes nothing and adds no undo step. One toast counts the changed parts and joints (“3 changes from Onshape”, “No changes from Onshape”). `electron/onshape/update.test.mjs` runs it against the fake API's versions (`armMm`, `wrist`, `elbow`, `limits`), and the e2e updates a longer arm and undoes it.
- **Zips** unpack in the geometry worker (`archive.ts`, fflate). An entry that climbs out (`..`), starts at `/` or a drive, or sits in `__MACOSX` is dropped. Each zip unpacks to at most 600 MB and 20,000 entries, and a zip link downloads at most 200 MB (checked against `Content-Length`, then counted as it streams); GitHub file links to a zip go through `raw.githubusercontent.com`, so Git LFS zips work. A description's references resolve only inside the tree, so `../../secret.stl` reads nothing (`archive.test.ts`). Every fetch is https only (`fetchBytes`).
- **Agent tools:** `get_rig`, `pose` (values, IK goals, body offset, save / apply), `animate` (keyframes or a gait; reports IK misses, limits, collisions and the floor at 8 times, including the last pose of a motion that doesn't loop, with the worst reading of each problem; preview frames), `retarget_motion` (an attached .bvh or a URL), `import_robot` (preset, URL, repo, zip or Onshape assembly link; `entry` picks one robot when a repo holds several), `wrap_robot` (Wrap Robot on a mesh and a robot, each the only one when left out; the robot defaults to the selected one); `make_leader` (below); `define_joints` takes prismatic joints and `parent_joint`.
- **Leader arms** (`src/leader/`, `workspace/useLeader.ts`): Make a Leader (⌘K, or right-click a part of the arm) and the agent's `make_leader` turn a fixed-base arm (`classifyRobot` is `fixed`) whose movable joints form one chain into a hand-held leader, opened as a new scene tab named "⟨arm⟩ Leader". `leaderArm.ts` finds the chain and takes its last joint as the gripper when it's prismatic or named like one (grip, jaw, finger, claw, pinch). The leader keeps every movable joint's id, name, type, axis and range, in order, so forward kinematics turns its handle exactly as the follower's end effector turns at any joint values; its joint positions differ. `leaderFrames.ts` works in the CAD frame like the arm template: each link runs along the common normal of its two joint axes (for parallel axes, along the follower's offset at the first link and straight on after), leaning toward the follower's offset, then up, and never folding back past 120°; at rest the leader runs straight along those normals. Link lengths are the follower's scaled to half its reach (the sum of the distances between consecutive arm joints), but at least 56 mm, the shortest `servo_link` between two XL330s, so a small arm like the SO-101 comes out nearer 0.6×. Every joint has a Dynamixel XL330-M288 in the cradle of the part before it, read as an angle sensor; the printed parts are `servo_base`, one `servo_link(length, twist, s = XL330, m = 2)` per link, and a handle (`servo_u_link` plus a printed grip) on the last arm joint. With a gripper, the handle also holds a cradle for the trigger's XL330 (the grip pocketed around it), and the trigger (a horn-side `servo_u_link` with a lever) is the gripper joint, with its axis, type and range; a prismatic gripper keeps its type. The printed parts arrive as `cad.pending` placeholders sized to the compiled bounds and compile in the desktop app (`useCadCompile`); `leader.test.mjs` compiles them in the kernel. The leader stands on the floor, centred. Its project records what it leads (`project.leads`: the follower's scene id, robot key, and leader → follower joint ids, the same ids), and its servos are numbered 1…n in joint order (`servoLayout`).
