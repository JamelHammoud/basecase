# 3D canvas

Part of the Basecase guide. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and the index of these files; section numbers (§) are shared across them.

## 12. 3D Canvas

- Background `#1b1b1b` (the canvas card) with a soft CSS vignette.
- **Lighting:** a `RoomEnvironment` environment map (intensity 0.5, like the Parts thumbnails), a hemisphere light, the shadow-casting key light, a cool rim light, and a soft fill that follows the camera. Dark plastic reads through its reflections, so don't remove the environment map.
  - Near-black part colors render no darker than HSL lightness 0.13 (about `#212121`; `displayColor` in `three/buildPartMesh.ts`). Maker CAD often paints black plastic `#020202` (the STS3215), which no light can show.
- **Stationary camera views** (§8l) draw as small frustums (`three/viewport/cameraGlyphs.ts`) on `stage.overlays`, so captures never show them; `currentView` and `lookThrough` on the handle read and set the framing. Every camera call on the handle stops a running framing animation first.
- **Build step display** (`three/viewport/buildStep.ts`, the `buildStep` prop, §8b): a display state, never a scene edit. Parts outside `shown` are hidden through their groups' `visible` (the batches follow it), so measurements, which read `part.visible`, are unchanged. While it's set the layer's pose map is the step's own: each move starts at its offset and eases to the seat (450 ms hold, 900 ms move) through `RenderLoop.animators`, which re-poses only the moved parts (`PartLayer.applyPoseTo`) and renders only while they move; reduced motion skips it. Arrows are blue cylinders and cones on `stage.overlays`, drawn over everything; servo labels are glass chips placed after each render at the top of the part's box. The gizmo, clicks, marquee and selection outline are off. Clearing it restores visibility and the scene's pose. `shownPartIds` on the handle lists the parts on screen.
- **Skeleton** (§8c, `three/viewport/skeletonOverlay.ts`): while Edit Skeleton is on, `project.skeleton` draws on `stage.overlays` as white 2px bones (parent to child, joint to tip) and 14px joint balls coloured by torque margin, both over everything (no depth test), so parts never rebuild and captures never show it. A press within 12px of a ball grabs it before orbit and selection (capture-phase `pointerdown`, `draggingGizmo` set until the release); the cursor turns to move over a ball. A release without moving is a click, which opens the joint's servo menu. When a skeleton appears in a scene with no parts (Arm or Biped), the camera frames it, down to the floor (`frameBox`).
- **Data layers** (`src/dataLayers/`, drawn by `three/viewport/dataLayers.ts`): a robot's sensor data in 3D, on `stage.overlays` (so captures never show it), redrawn only when a layer's message changes. One `DataTrack` per topic or source: its message times and a `message(i)` read on demand, in the ROS message shapes (`dataLayers/messages.ts`, ROS field names). Kinds: point clouds (`PointCloud2`, colored by a field: a float field on a turbo ramp, `rgb` / `rgba` as packed bytes), laser scans (`LaserScan`, and the sim's own hits in scene mm, `scene/Points`), TF frames (`TFMessage`: 100 mm red, green and blue axes, the frame name in a glass chip within 12 px of the pointer, `frameLabel.ts`), paths (`Path`, and `Odometry` as a trail to the playhead with an arrow at its end), markers (`Marker` / `MarkerArray` arrows, cubes, spheres, cylinders, cube and sphere lists, points, line strips and lists, with add, delete and delete-all; `foxglove.SceneUpdate` cubes, spheres, cylinders, arrows and lines), occupancy grids (`OccupancyGrid` as a textured plane: unknown cells clear, free cells dark at 25% opacity, occupied up to white) and poses (`PoseStamped`) as arrows.
  - **Frames.** `three/viewport/dataFrames.ts` resolves a message's `frame_id` through the TF tree at the playhead (moving transforms interpolated, static ones held) to the robot's links by name, else to a link elsewhere in the same tree, else the tree's root to the robot's base. ROS metres with Z up become scene millimetres with Y up (`ROS_TO_SCENE`, the same mapping as MuJoCo's `fromMj`). A frame it can't resolve isn't drawn, and the layer's row in Layers shows the warn mark with the frame's name.
  - **Time.** The layer store (`dataLayers/store.ts`, one per scene tab, made by `useDataLayers`) holds a playhead: none while live, so each layer draws its newest message, and the try timeline's time while a try replays, so each draws the message at or before it (`spanAt`). A layer's `decayS` keeps older scans and clouds in the window; odometry and markers accumulate from the start.
  - **Buffers.** Each layer owns one point buffer (positions as floats, colors as normalized bytes) that grows by fourfold steps from 4,096 to 1,000,000 points and then stays; later messages rewrite it in place with an update range, never a new array. Markers draw as one `InstancedMesh` per shape and one `LineSegments`; a grid reuses its `DataTexture` while its size holds.
  - **The sim's LiDARs** (§8l) are layers named after their part, under the robot in Layers: live scans from each frame message, and during a replay the try's recorded ranges re-placed from the recorded body poses (`dataLayers/simTracks.ts`).
  - `setDataLayers` on the handle takes the store's input and returns each layer's counts (`dataLayerState`: points, shapes, segments, grid, axes, the missing frame and the time of the message shown); `scanPointCount` sums the scan layers. The dev `simState()` reads both, with `dataPlayhead`.
- **Check dots** (`three/viewport/checkDots.ts`): an expanded check with points to show (Payload, §8b) draws them as 5 px dots on `stage.overlays`, so captures never show them, coloured from grey `#8a8a8a` to the accent by each dot's `shade`. They test depth but don't write it. `checkDotCount` on the handle counts them for e2e tests.
- **Case trails** (`three/viewport/caseTrails.ts`): a Case opened from its Checks row (§8b) draws each seeded run's path (§8h) as a 1.5 px line on `stage.overlays`, so captures never show them, passing runs green and failed ones red, over everything (no depth test). A click within 8 px of a failed trail picks the nearest one before part selection (`PointerInput`, `onPickTrail`); passing trails take no clicks. `__studio.caseTrails()` lists each trail's seed and the screen point of its end.
- **The measured robot** (`three/viewport/ghostLayer.ts`): while a robot connected from this scene runs in Test or above (§8k), a translucent Figma-blue copy of its moving parts follows the servos' measured positions beside the solid sim robot. `useRobotGhost` poses it with `poseDeltas` from the latest status (`robotGhost` in `src/robot/robotGhost.ts`) and hands it to the handle's `setGhost` once per status event, every 100 ms, then the render is invalidated; there's no loop. Its meshes reuse each part group's geometry with one shared material (no copies) and are rebuilt only when the part is. It lives on `stage.overlays` with raycasting off, so captures, thumbnails and picking never see it. A chip (`[data-testid="ghost-gap"]`) at the joint with the largest gap shows that gap in signed degrees; `ghostState` on the handle reports the posed parts, values and label for tests.
- **Infinite grid** (`three/infiniteGrid.ts`): a camera-following shader plane on y = 0, not a fixed `GridHelper`.
  - 1 / 10 / 100 / 1000 mm lines fade in and out by on-screen density, so there's no moiré when zoomed out or at grazing angles.
  - The red X and blue Z axes run forever, and the grid fades into the distance (the fade distance scales with zoom).
  - `toneMapped: false` keeps line contrast exact; lines sit about 15–25 levels above the background, and the axes are brighter.
- Edges: Figma blue `#0d99ff` for selection, red `#ef4444` for cutouts.
- **Selection outline** (`three/viewport/selectionOutline.ts`): selected parts also get a 2.5px Figma-blue outline around their combined silhouette, drawn over everything (so a part inside a shell still shows), and around a multi-selection or group as one shape. Before each render the selected meshes are drawn into a 4× MSAA single-channel mask (camera layer 7, override material), then a quad in `stage.overlays` (so captures never show it), covering only the selection's screen rectangle, dilates the mask. The dilation loop spans only the outline's radius at the current pixel ratio (`REACH`: 7 × 7 samples at 1×, 13 × 13 at 2×), since every pixel of the rectangle runs it. Nothing is rebuilt; with no selection the pass is skipped.
- **Software GL** (`three/softwareGl.ts`, read once from the unmasked renderer name: SwiftShader, llvmpipe): the canvas, the outline mask and the camera feed drop MSAA. With SwiftShader every pixel is drawn on the CPU, and in a Linux container like CI's, MSAA at 1440 × 900 cut the idle canvas from about 28 to 7 frames a second. Nothing changes on a GPU.
- Catalog components (manufacturer meshes) and hardware (fasteners) draw their edge lines only when selected or set as a cutout. Unselected, their dense feature edges showed through enclosing shells as dark specks; the judge read those as overlapping geometry.
- B-rep edges are drawn only at creases (over 20° between the faces on each side). Smooth seams from lofts, splines and tangent fillets aren't outlined, so lofted shells don't get contour stripes.
- The directional light's shadow `normalBias` scales with the shadow frustum (about 1.5 shadow-map texels), so large robots don't get shadow-acne specks.
- Solid part faces use a small polygon offset, so edge lines always draw cleanly on top. Without it, outlines lying on another surface (a flush panel, a coincident face) flicker as dark speckles.
- Gestures: two-finger orbit (or pan), Space-drag pan, pinch zoom, gizmo drag.
- **Clicks for Sketch** (§8): a click that selects a part also reports the point it hit (`onPickPoint`), which Sketch uses to open on that face; a double-click on a part reports it too (`onOpenPart`), which reopens the sketch of a part Sketch made. Sketch's snap glyphs (coincident, horizontal, vertical) and constraint glyphs draw on the sketch plane in the Inspector, not in 3D.
- **Cursors** (`CURSORS` in `src/design-system/cursors.ts`, Figma-style): the canvas has its own arrow (near-black, white 1.5px stroke, every corner rounded, soft shadow), the same shape as the blue `AgentCursor`, so the two read as a pair. Comment mode shows a bubble in the pin's shape and the arrow's colors; its tip corner is where the pin's corner lands. Sculpt shows an outlined crosshair. Picking a joint axis (§8c) shows the crosshair over a round face of the two parts and the system not-allowed cursor elsewhere. Space-pan keeps the system grab hands.
  - They're CSS cursors drawn by the OS (never a DOM element following the pointer), so they never lag. Each is a 32 × 32 px SVG shipped at 2x in `image-set()` for Retina, with its hotspot on the exact pixel the tip shows (the arrow's stroke tip at (3, 3)).
  - The canvas element inherits its wrapper's cursor (`.canvas-cursor > canvas` in `index.css`), because three.js controls write `cursor: auto` onto the canvas. Comment pins use the arrow too.
- **Zoom range** 25 mm – 12 m camera distance (`MIN_DIST` / `MAX_DIST` in `three/viewport/types.ts`), enough for humanoids.
  - The far plane is 80 m, and the near plane scales with distance.
  - Grid fade, shadow plane and the directional light's shadow frustum all follow the camera distance.
- **The camera can go under the floor** to inspect undersides: orbit (drag and two-finger scroll) is limited only near the poles (`MIN_POLAR` / `MAX_POLAR` in `three/viewport/types.ts`). From below, the grid draws at 60% strength (it never writes depth, so parts on the floor show through it without z-fighting), and the shadow plane faces up, so it's culled and never hides parts.
- **Port snapping** (`three/viewport/portSnap.ts`, logic in `agent/snap.ts`, §9): moving one part with the gizmo, or dropping a Parts or Library tile, snaps it onto another part by their mounting ports, the way the agent's `mate` places parts.
  - While the drag lasts, ports that have a compatible partner nearby show as dots (`portDots.ts`, a `THREE.Points` overlay in `stage.overlays`, so captures never show them): on the dragged part and on parts within 60 mm or the part's own size. The pair about to snap turns Figma blue.
  - A dragged port within the snap distance of a compatible port snaps: the part takes the pose `planMate` gives that pair, live, under the pointer. The snap distance is 8 mm (`SNAP_MM`) out to 400 mm of camera distance and grows with it beyond, so it stays about the same on screen.
  - Releasing commits the pose and the mate (`PartMate`, the newest one replacing the part's older mates) as one undo step (`onPlacePart` → `placePart`). Dragging a mated part without a snap drops its mates.
  - Holding ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows; `runtime.freeHeld`) moves freely; pressing or letting go mid-drag re-solves at once. Grid snapping still applies; Shift turns that off as before.
  - Ports come from part data (`portsAt`), never meshes. The target parts' world ports are gathered once when the drag starts and the dots update only on pointer moves while dragging. Snapping is off while the robot is posed, for multi-part selections and groups (the pivot), and in Rotate and Scale.
- **Shift while using the gizmo** turns off grid, angle and scale snapping for smooth motion. A “Snapping off” glass chip shows while dragging. Commits keep 0.1 mm / 0.1° precision instead of rounding to 0.5 mm / 1°. Shift-dragging a gizmo handle never starts a box select (`transformControls.axis` check).
